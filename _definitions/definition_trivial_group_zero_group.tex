A \hldef{trivial group} is a \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:group}{group} consisting of exactly one element. That is, a trivial group is a group $(G, \cdot)$ such that $|G| = 1$. The unique element of a trivial group is necessarily the \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:group}{identity element} of the group.

A trivial group is always an \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:group}{abelian group} (since the unique element commutes with itself). When the fact that it is an abelian group is emphasized and its group structure is written additively, a trivial group is also called a \hldef{zero group} (or \hldef{zero object} in the \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:category_of_abelian_groups}{category of abelian groups}).

Up to \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:isomorphism_in_a:isomorphism_of_groups}{isomorphism of groups}, there is exactly one trivial group. In other words, the trivial group is the unique group of cardinality one up to isomorphism.
\end{definition}

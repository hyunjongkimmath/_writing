Let $r$ and $s$ be positive integers. The \hldef{two-color Ramsey number $R(r,s)$}, denoted by \hl{$R(r,s)$}, is the smallest integer $N$ such that every \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:simple_graph_directed_graph_multigraph_weighted_graph}{simple graph} on $N$ vertices contains either a \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:clique_independent_set_of_a_graph}{clique of size $r$} or an \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:clique_independent_set_of_a_graph}{independent set of size $s$}.

Equivalently, $R(r,s)$ is the smallest integer $N$ such that every coloring of the edges of the complete graph \hl{$K_N$} with two colors (say red and blue) yields either a red \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:complete_graph_empty_graph}{complete subgraph on $r$ vertices} or a blue complete subgraph on $s$ vertices.
\end{definition}

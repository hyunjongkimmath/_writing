\begin{definition} \label{definition:module_of_kahler_differentials_of_a_ring_homomorphism}
    Let $\varphi: B \to A$ be a \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:ring_homomorphism}{ring homomorphism}.

    \begin{enumerate}
        \item The module \hl{$\Omega_{A/B}$}, called the \hldef{module of Kähler differentials of $A$ over $B$}, is the \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:module_of_a_ring}{$A$-module} generated by symbols $da$ for all $a \in A$, subject to the following relations:
        \begin{enumerate}
            \item $d(a + a') = da + da'$ for all $a, a' \in A$,
            \item $d(aa') = a\,da' + a'\,da$ for all $a, a' \in A$,
            \item $d(\varphi(b)) = 0$ for all $b \in B$.
        \end{enumerate}

        Equivalently, $\Omega_{A/B}$ is defined as the quotient of the \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:free_bimodule_over_rings}{free $A$-module} on symbols $\{da \mid a \in A\}$ by the submodule generated by elements of the form:
        $$ d(a + a') - da - da', \quad d(aa') - (a\,da' + a'\,da), \quad d(\varphi(b)) $$
        for all $a, a' \in A$ and $b \in B$.

        An explicit construction of $\Omega_{A/B}$ is given by
        $$\Omega_{A/B} \cong I / I^2,$$
        where $I \subset A \otimes_B A$ is the kernel of the multiplication map $\mu: A \otimes_B A \to A$, and the isomorphism sends $da$ to the class of $a \otimes 1 - 1 \otimes a$ in $I/I^2$.

        \item The map $d: A \to \Omega_{A/B}$ given by $a \mapsto da$ is called the \hldef{universal derivation of the algebra $A$ over the ring $B$}. It satisfies the following universal property:

        For any \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:module_of_a_ring}{$A$-module} $M$ and any \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:derivation_in_an_algebra_over_a_ring}{derivation} $D: A \to M$ over $B$, there exists a unique $A$-linear map $\phi: \Omega_{A/B} \to M$ such that $D = \phi \circ d$. In other words, the following diagram commutes:

        $$
        \begin{array}{c}
        A \\
        \downarrow{\scriptstyle d} \quad \searrow{\scriptstyle D} \\
        \Omega_{A/B} \xrightarrow{\exists! \, \phi} M
        \end{array}
        $$
    \end{enumerate}

    The pair $(\Omega_{A/B}, d)$ is unique up to unique isomorphism. Thus, the module of Kähler differentials $\Omega_{A/B}$ together with the universal derivation $d$ provides a complete characterization of derivations on $A$ over $B$.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition} \label{definition:affine_hull_of_a_subset_of_an_affine_module_over_a_ring}
    Let $R$ be a \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:ring}{ring}. Let $A$ be an \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:affine_module_over_a_ring}{affine module} over $R$ with underlying (translation) module $M$. Let $S$ be a subset of $A$.

    Given a finite nonempty index set $I$, elements $\lambda_i \in R$ for each $i \in I$ satisfying $\sum_{i \in I} \lambda_i = 1$, and elements $a_i \in A$ for each $i \in I$, one defines the \hldef{affine combination} \hl{$\sum_{i \in I} \lambda_i a_i$} of the points $a_i$ with coefficients $\lambda_i$ as follows. Pick any point $p \in A$. Write each $a_i = p + v_i$ for a unique $v_i \in M$, and set
    $$\sum_{i \in I} \lambda_i a_i \;=\; p + \sum_{i \in I} \lambda_i v_i \;\in\; A.$$
    The result is independent of the choice of the base point $p \in A$.

    The \hldef{affine hull} (or \hldef{affine span}) \hl{$\operatorname{AffineHull}(S)$} of $S$ is the subset of $A$ consisting of all finite affine combinations of elements of $S$:
    $$\operatorname{AffineHull}(S) \;=\; \left\{ \sum_{i \in I} \lambda_i a_i \;\middle|\; I \text{ finite nonempty},\; a_i \in S,\; \lambda_i \in R,\; \sum_{i \in I} \lambda_i = 1 \right\}.$$

    Equivalently, fix any point $p \in A$. Let $N_p$ be the submodule of $M$ generated by $\{a - p : a \in S\}$. Then
    $$\operatorname{AffineHull}(S) \;=\; p + N_p.$$
    In particular, $\operatorname{AffineHull}(S)$ is an \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:affine_submodule_of_an_affine_module_over_a_ring}{affine submodule} of $A$. If $S$ is nonempty then the submodule $N_p$ is independent of the choice of $p \in S$, and $\operatorname{AffineHull}(S)$ is the unique affine submodule of $A$ containing $S$ with translation module equal to the submodule of $M$ generated by $\{a - b : a, b \in S\}$.

    The affine hull $\operatorname{AffineHull}(S)$ is the smallest affine submodule of $A$ containing $S$, i.e. it is contained in every affine submodule of $A$ that contains $S$.
\end{definition}

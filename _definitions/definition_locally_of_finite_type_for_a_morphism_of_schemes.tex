\begin{definition}[locally of finite type for a morphism of schemes] \label{definition:locally_of_finite_type_for_a_morphism_of_schemes}
    Let $f\colon X \to Y$ be a \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:morphism_of_schemes}{morphism of schemes}. We say that $f$ is \hldef{locally of finite type} if for every point $x \in X$, there exist affine open neighborhoods $\Spec B = V \subseteq Y$ of $f(x)$ and $\Spec A = U \subseteq X$ of $x$ such that $U$ is an \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:affine_open_subscheme_of_a_scheme}{affine open subscheme} of $X$, $V$ is an affine open subscheme of $Y$, $f(U) \subseteq V$, and the induced \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:ring_homomorphism}{ring homomorphism} $B \to A$ makes $A$ a \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:finitely_generated_algebra_over_a_not_necessarily_commutative_ring}{finitely generated $B$-algebra}.

    Equivalently, $f$ is locally of finite type if it can be covered by affine open subsets $\Spec A_i \subseteq X$ and $\Spec B_i \subseteq Y$ such that $f(\Spec A_i) \subseteq \Spec B_i$ for each $i$, and each ring homomorphism $B_i \to A_i$ exhibits $A_i$ as a finitely generated $B_i$-algebra.
\end{definition}

Let $\sin$ and $\cos$ be the \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:real_sine_and_cosine_functions_on_real_line_via_power_series}{sine and cosine functions}.

The \hldef{secant function}, denoted by \hl{$\sec$}, is defined on $\{x \in \mathbb{R} : \cos(x) \neq 0\}$ by
$$\hlin{\sec(x) = \frac{1}{\cos(x)}}$$

The \hldef{cosecant function}, denoted by \hl{$\csc$}, is defined on $\{x \in \mathbb{R} : \sin(x) \neq 0\}$ by
$$\hlin{\csc(x) = \frac{1}{\sin(x)}}$$

The \hldef{cotangent function}, denoted by \hl{$\cot$}, is defined on $\{x \in \mathbb{R} : \sin(x) \neq 0\}$ by
$$\hlin{\cot(x) = \frac{\cos(x)}{\sin(x)}}$$

The secant and cotangent functions are $\pi$-periodic; the cosecant function is $2\pi$-periodic. The following identities hold wherever both sides are defined:
\begin{align*}
    1 + \tan^2(x) &= \sec^2(x) \\
    1 + \cot^2(x) &= \csc^2(x)
\end{align*}

\end{definition}

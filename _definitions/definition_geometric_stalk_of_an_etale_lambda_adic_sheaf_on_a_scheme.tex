Let $R$ be a commutative ring and let $\lambda\subseteq R$ be an ideal. Let $X$ be a scheme. Let $\overline{x} \in X$ be a geometric point.  
    Let 
    $$\calF = (\calF_n, u_n)_{n \in \bbZ_{\geq 1}}, \quad u_n: \calF_n \to \calF_{n-1}$$
    be a \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:lambda_adic_sheaf_on_the_small_etale_site_of_a_scheme_for_an_ideal_of_a_commutative_ring}{$\lambda$-adic sheaf} (on the small \'etale site of $X$). 

    The \hldef{(geometric) stalk of $\calF$ at $\overline{x}$} is the inverse limit 
    $$\hlin{\calF_{\overline{x}} \coloneq \varprojlim_{n} \calF_{n, \overline{x}}}.$$
    (\Cref{definition:stalk_of_a_presheaf_on_the_small_etale_site_of_a_schem_at_a_geometric_point})
    It is naturally a $\widehat{R}$ module where $\widehat{R}$ is the \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:complete_ring_with_respect_to_an_ideal}{$\lambda$-adic completion} of $R$. In particular, every geometric point $\overline{x} \in X$ yields a functor
    $$\calF \mapsto \calF_{\overline{x}}: \Sh(X,R) \to \Mod(\widehat{R})$$
    \CrefIfExists{definition:category_of_modules_and_bimodules_over_rings} that is in fact exact.

\begin{definition} \label{definition:center_of_a_group}
Let $(G,\cdot)$ be a \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:group}{group}.
The \hldef{center of $G$}, denoted by \hl{$Z(G)$}, is the \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:subset_of_a_set}{subset} of $G$ defined by
$$\hlin{Z(G) = \{ z \in G : zg = gz \text{ for all } g \in G \}}.$$
The center $Z(G)$ is a \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:normal_subgroup_of_a_group}{normal subgroup} of $G$.
Elements of $Z(G)$ are called \hldef{central elements of $G$}.

One says that $G$ is \hldef{center free} if $Z(G) = \{1\}$.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition} \label{definition:commutator_ideal_of_two_subsets_of_a_ring}

    Let $R$ be a \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:ring}{ring}. For nonempty subsets $A, B \subseteq R$, the \hldef{commutator ideal of $A$ and $B$}, denoted by \hl{$[A,B]$}, is the \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:ideal_generated_by_a_subset_of_a_ring}{two-sided ideal of $R$ generated by} all commutators of elements from $A$ and $B$:
    $$\hlin{[A,B] = \langle [a,b] \mid a \in A, b \in B \rangle},$$
    where $[a,b]$ denotes the \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:commutator_of_two_elements_of_a_ring}{commutator of $a$ and $b$}.

    When $A = B$, we write $[A,A]$ instead of $[A,B]$. In particular, the \hldef{commutator ideal (or derived ideal) of $R$}, denoted by \hl{$[R,R]$}, is
    $$[R,R] = \langle [a,b] \mid a,b \in R \rangle.$$

    The ring $R$ is commutative if and only if $[R,R]$ is the zero ideal.
\end{definition}

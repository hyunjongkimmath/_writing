\begin{definition}[Compactified modular curve $X_s(N)$] \label{definition:X_s_N_nonclassical_modular_curve_quotient_by_noncongruence_subgroup}
Let $N \geq 1$ be an integer and let $\Gamma \subset \mathrm{GL}_2(\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z})$ be a subgroup that is not the image of any \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:congruence_subgroup_of_SL_2_Z}{congruence subgroup} of $\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb{Z})$. Such a subgroup is called a \hldef{non-congruence subgroup}. The \hldef{non-classical modular curve} \hl{$X_s(N)$} associated to $\Gamma$ is the compact Riemann surface (or \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:algebraic_curve_over_a_field}{complete algebraic curve}) defined as the quotient
$$\hlin{X_s(N) = \Gamma \backslash \mathbb{H}^*}$$
where $\mathbb{H}^* = \mathbb{H} \cup \mathbb{P}^1(\mathbb{Q})$ is the \CrefAndHyperref{definition:extended_upper_half_plane}{extended upper half-plane} and $\Gamma$ acts via the \CrefAndHyperref{definition:mobius_transformation}{Möbius action}.

More precisely, $X_s(N)$ is obtained by taking the quotient of \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:X_N_compactified_modular_curve_parameterizing_level_N_structures_on_generalized_elliptic_curves}{the compactified modular curve $X(N)$} by the action of $\Gamma$ on the full level $N$ structures. The resulting curve is a \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:finite_cover_of_curves}{finite cover} of $X(1)$, but unlike the classical modular curves $X_0(N)$, $X_1(N)$, and $X(N)$, it does not parameterize elliptic curves with a moduli-theoretically natural level structure arising from a congruence subgroup.

The curve $X_s(N)$ was studied by Serre as an example of a modular curve that does not arise from the classical congruence subgroup theory.

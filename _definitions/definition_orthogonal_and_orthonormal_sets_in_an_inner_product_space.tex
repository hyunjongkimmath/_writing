\begin{definition} \label{definition:orthogonal_and_orthonormal_sets_in_an_inner_product_space}
    Let $V$ be an \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:inner_product_on_a_vector_space_over_the_reals_or_complex_numbers}{inner product space} over $\mathbb{R}$ or $\mathbb{C}$.

    A set of vectors $\{v_1, \dots, v_n\} \subseteq V$ is called \hldef{orthogonal} if $\langle v_i, v_j \rangle = 0$ for all $i \neq j$. It is called \hldef{orthonormal} if in addition $\|v_i\| = 1$ for all $i$ (equivalently, $\langle v_i, v_j \rangle = \delta_{ij}$).

    An orthonormal set that is also a \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:basis_and_dimension_of_a_vector_space_over_a_field}{basis} of $V$ is called an \hldef{orthonormal basis for $V$}.

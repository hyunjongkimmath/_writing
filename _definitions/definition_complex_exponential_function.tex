    The \hldef{exponential function on $\mathbb{C}$}, denoted by \hl{$\exp : \mathbb{C} \to \mathbb{C}$}, is defined for each $z \in \mathbb{C}$ by the power series
    $$\hlin{\exp(z) = \sum_{n=0}^\infty \frac{z^n}{n!}}$$
    which \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:absolute_convergence_of_a_series_in_module_over_a_commutative_ring_with_an_absolute_value_valued_in_an_ordered_semiring}{converges absolutely} for every $z \in \mathbb{C}$, where $z^n$ is the \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:exponentiation_in_a_field}{$n$th power} of $z$ in $\mathbb{C}$ and $0! := 1$.

    We write \hl{$e^z$} for $\exp(z)$. For $x, y \in \mathbb{R}$, Euler's formula states that
    $$\exp(x + iy) = e^x (\cos(y) + i \sin(y)),$$
    where $\cos$ and $\sin$ are the \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:real_sine_and_cosine_functions_on_real_line_via_power_series}{sine and cosine functions on $\mathbb{R}$}. The proof of Euler's formula follows from comparing power series expansions.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Semi-abelian variety] \label{definition:semi_abelian_variety_over_a_field}
    Let $k$ be a \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:field}{field}. A \hldef{semi-abelian variety over $k$} is an \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:algebraic_group_scheme_over_a_scheme}{algebraic group} $G$ over $k$ that fits into an extension of an \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:abelian_variety_over_a_field}{abelian variety} by a \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:algebraic_torus_over_a_field}{torus}, i.e., there exists an exact sequence of algebraic groups over $k$:
    \TODO{exact sequence of algebraic groups}
    $$\hlin{1 \to T \to G \to A \to 0,}$$
    where $T$ is an algebraic torus over $k$ and $A$ is an abelian variety over $k$. If $T$ has dimension $r$, we say that $G$ has \hldef{torus rank $r$}. When $r = 0$, the semi-abelian variety is simply an abelian variety.

    Equivalently, a semiabelian variety over $k$ is a \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:semi_abelian_group_scheme_over_a_scheme}{semiabelian scheme} over $\Spec k$.
\end{definition}

Let $S$ be a \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:scheme}{scheme}, and let $f \colon X \to S$ be an \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:scheme_over_a_scheme}{$S$-scheme}. A \hldef{relative effective Cartier divisor on $X$ over $S$} is an \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:cartier_divisor_on_a_scheme}{effective Cartier divisor} $D \subset X$ such that the structure morphism $D \to S$ (the composition of the closed immersion $D \hookrightarrow X$ with $f$) is a \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:flat_morphism_of_schemes}{flat morphism}.

Equivalently, $D$ is a closed subscheme of $X$ such that:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item The ideal sheaf $\mathcal{I}_D \subset \mathcal{O}_X$ is an invertible $\mathcal{O}_X$-module (i.e.\ locally generated by a non-zero-divisor), and
    \item the structure morphism $D \to S$ is flat.
\end{enumerate}

If $D_1, D_2$ are relative effective Cartier divisors on $X/S$, then their sum $D_1 + D_2$ (defined via the product of local generators) is again a relative effective Cartier divisor on $X/S$. If $D_1 \subset D_2$ as closed subschemes, then the difference $D$ such that $D_2 = D_1 + D$ is also a relative effective Cartier divisor.

When $f \colon X \to S$ is flat and locally of finite presentation (e.g.\ when $X$ is smooth over $S$), any relative effective Cartier divisor $D \subset X$ implies that $f$ is flat at points of $D$ in many cases; the definition ensures this holds globally. In particular, if $x \in D$ and $\mathcal{O}_{X,x}$ is Noetherian, then $f$ is flat at $x$.

When $X = C$ is a relative curve (i.e.\ $f \colon C \to S$ is flat, proper, and of relative dimension one), a relative effective Cartier divisor on $C/S$ corresponds to a family of effective divisors on the fibers $C_s$, varying flatly over $S$. If such a divisor $D \subset C$ is moreover finite and étale over $S$, then $D$ consists of $n$ disjoint sections locally on $S$ for some fixed $n$, i.e.\ it corresponds to an unordered configuration space point.
\end{definition}

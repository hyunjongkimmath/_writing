\begin{definition}[Separable closure of a field] \label{definition:separable_closure_of_a_field}
    Let $K$ be a \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:field}{field}. A \hldef{separable closure of $K$} is a \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:separable_algebraic_field_extension}{separable algebraic extension} \hl{$K^{\text{sep}}$} of $K$ such that every element of $K^{\text{sep}}$ is separable over $K$, and $K^{\text{sep}}$ has no nontrivial separable algebraic extensions (i.e., the only separable algebraic extension of $K^{\text{sep}}$ inside a fixed algebraic closure $\overline{K}$ is itself).

    $K^{\text{sep}}$ is often also denoted as \hl{$K^s$}, \hl{$K_s$}, or \hl{$\overline{K}$}, the latter of which should not be mistaken for notation for an \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:algebraic_element_minimal_polynomial_of_an_algebraic_element_algebraic_field_extension_transcendental_element_field_extension}{algebraic closure} of $K$.

    Equivalently, $K^{\text{sep}}$ is the compositum of all \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:finite_field_extension}{finite} separable subextensions of $K$ in a fixed algebraic closure $\overline{K}$. A separable closure always exists and is unique up to (non-unique) isomorphism over $K$. If $K$ has \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:characteristic_of_a_ring}{characteristic} zero, then every algebraic extension is separable, so the separable closure coincides with the algebraic closure.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition} \label{definition:similar_matrices_and_change_of_basis}
    Let $F$ be a \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:field}{field}. Two $n \times n$ \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:matrix_over_a_ring}{matrices} $A$ and $B$ over $F$ are called \hldef{similar} (over $F$) if there exists an invertible matrix $P$ over $F$ such that
    $$\hlin{B = P^{-1} A P}.$$

    Let $V$ be a finite-dimensional $F$-vector space, and let $T: V \to V$ be a linear endomorphism. If $\mathcal{B}$ and $\mathcal{B}'$ are two ordered bases of $V$, the matrices $[T]_{\mathcal{B}}^{\mathcal{B}}$ and $[T]_{\mathcal{B}'}^{\mathcal{B}'}$ are similar. The invertible matrix $P = [I]_{\mathcal{B}'}^{\mathcal{B}}$ is called the \hldef{change-of-basis matrix from $\mathcal{B}'$ to $\mathcal{B}$}.
\TextIfExists{definition:homomorphism_of_modules_over_a_ring}{Similarity of matrices corresponds to the fact that two matrix representations of the same endomorphism of a free module with respect to different bases are related by conjugation; this is a special case for $F$-modules of the change-of-basis behavior for \Cref{definition:homomorphism_of_modules_over_a_ring}s.}

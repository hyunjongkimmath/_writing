\begin{definition}[Logarithmic derivative of a real-valued function] \label{definition:logarithmic_derivative_of_a_real_valued_function}
Let $I \subseteq \bbR$ be an \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:interval_in_a_site_with_enough_points}{interval} and let $f : I \to \bbR$ be a \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:derivative_of_a_real_valued_function_on_a_real_interval}{differentiable} function that does not vanish on $I$ (i.e., $f(x) \neq 0$ for all $x \in I$).

The \hldef{logarithmic derivative of $f$} is the function
$$
\frac{f'}{f} : I \to \bbR, \qquad x \mapsto \frac{f'(x)}{f(x)}.
$$
\CrefIfExists{definition:derivative_of_a_real_valued_function_on_a_real_interval}

Equivalently, if $f$ is positive on $I$, the logarithmic derivative is the derivative of the \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:real_natural_logarithm_function_as_inverse_of_real_exponential_function}{natural logarithm} of $f$:
$$
\frac{f'(x)}{f(x)} = \frac{d}{dx} \log f(x),
$$
where the right-hand side exists by the \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:real_natural_logarithm_function_as_inverse_of_real_exponential_function}{natural logarithm} and the \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{proposition:sum_rule_product_rule_quotient_rule_chain_rule_for_real_derivatives_of_real_valued_functions_on_real_intervals}{chain rule} for differentiation.

\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Semigroup] \label{definition:semigroup}
    % EDITOR APPROVED
A \hldef{semigroup} is a pair $(M,\cdot)$ where $M$ is a \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:zermelo_fraenkel_set_theory}{set} and $\cdot : M \times M \to M$ is an \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:associative_binary_operation_on_a_set}{associative} \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:n_ary_operation_on_a_set}{binary operation}, i.e. $\cdot$ satisfies the following:
$$ (a \cdot b) \cdot c = a \cdot (b \cdot c) \quad \text{for all } a,b,c \in M. $$
A semigroup is said to be \hldef{commutative} or \hldef{abelian} if $\cdot$ is also \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:commutative_binary_operation_on_a_set}{commutative}, i.e. $\cdot$ satisfies the following:
$$a \cdot b \quad \text{ for all } a,b \in M.$$
\end{definition}

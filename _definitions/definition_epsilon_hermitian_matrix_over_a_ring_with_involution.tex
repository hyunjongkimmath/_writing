\begin{definition} \label{definition:epsilon_hermitian_matrix_over_a_ring_with_involution}
    Let $(A,\sigma)$ be a \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:involution_on_a_ring}{ring with involution}, and let $\varepsilon \in Z(A)$ satisfy $\varepsilon \cdot \overline{\varepsilon} = 1$.

    Let $H \in M_n(A)$ be a matrix. We say that $H$ is a \hldef{$\varepsilon$-Hermitian matrix over $(A,\sigma)$}: 
    if 
    $$H^* = \overline{\varepsilon} H.$$
    We remark that the choice of $\overline{\varepsilon}$ here as opposed to $\varepsilon$ is to ensure that the \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:epsilon_hermitian_form_induced_by_an_epsilon_hermitian_matrix}{Hermitian form $h_H$ associated to $H$} is a \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:varepsilon_hermitian_form_on_a_module_over_a_ring_with_involution}{$\varepsilon$-Hermitian form}.

    When $\varepsilon = 1$, $H$ is simply called a \hldef{Hermitian matrix} (or \hldef{self-adjoint matrix}). When $\varepsilon = -1$, $H$ is called a \hldef{skew-Hermitian matrix}.
\end{definition}

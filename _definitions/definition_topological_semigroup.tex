\begin{definition}[Topological semigroup] \label{definition:topological_semigroup}
    A \hldef{topological semigroup} is a \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:semigroup}{semigroup} $(S,\cdot)$ together with a \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:topological_space}{topology} $\mathcal{T}$ on $S$ such that the multiplication map
    $$\mu : S \times S \to S, \quad (x,y) \mapsto x \cdot y,$$
    is \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:continuous_map_of_topological_spaces}{continuous} with respect to the \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:product_topology_on_a_cartesian_product_of_topological_spaces}{product topology} on $S \times S$ and the topology $\mathcal{T}$ on $S$.

    A \hldef{topological abelian semigroup} (or \hldef{topological commutative semigroup}) is a topological semigroup that is \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:semigroup}{abelian as a semigroup}.

    \medskip
    \noindent\textbf{Remark (topological monoid).} If $S$ is a \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:monoid}{monoid} (i.e., it has an identity element), then $S$ together with a topology making multiplication continuous is called a \hldef{topological monoid}. In particular, every topological monoid is a topological semigroup.
\end{definition}

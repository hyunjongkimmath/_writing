\begin{definition} \label{definition:quasi_projective_variety_over_a_field}
Let $k$ be a \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:field}{field}. A \hldef{quasi-projective variety over $k$} is an \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:locally_closed_subscheme_of_a_scheme}{locally closed subscheme} of the \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:projective_space_of_dimension_n_over_a_scheme}{projective space} $\mathbb{P}^n_k$ for some integer $n \geq 0$.

Equivalently, a quasi-projective variety over $k$ is a scheme $X$ that admits an \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:locally_closed_imersion_of_schemes}{immersion} (i.e., a composition of an open immersion followed by a closed immersion) $j: X \hookrightarrow \mathbb{P}^n_k$ for some $n \geq 0$.

As is the case with the general notion of \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:variety_over_a_field}{variety}, modern definitions of quasi-projective varieties usually also include the assumption that the variety be \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:reduced_scheme}{reduced}. Unless otherwise specified, we assume that quasi-projective varieties are reduced.




A quasi-projective variety over $k$ is called \hldef{projective} if it is isomorphic to a \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:projective_variety_over_a_field}{projective variety} over $k$, and it is called \hldef{affine} if it is isomorphic to an affine variety over $k$.

When the quasi-projective variety is required to be an \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:integral_scheme}{integral scheme}, it is called an \hldef{irreducible quasi-projective variety over $k$}.
\end{definition}

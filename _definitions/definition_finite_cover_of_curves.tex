\begin{definition} \label{definition:finite_cover_of_curves}
Let $C$ and $D$ be proper algebraic curves over a field $k$. A \hldef{finite cover of curves} is a non-constant morphism $f : C \to D$ such that the induced field extension $K(C)/f^*K(D)$ is finite.
Equivalently, in the language of Riemann surfaces (when $k = \mathbb{C}$), a finite cover is a holomorphic map $f : C \to D$ between compact Riemann surfaces that is surjective and has finite fibres.
The \hldef{degree of the cover $f$} is defined as $\deg(f) = [K(C) : f^*K(D)]$, which equals the number of points in a generic fibre $f^{-1}(y)$ for $y \in D$.
If the degree is $1$, then $f$ is an isomorphism. If the degree is greater than $1$, then $f$ has branch points (ramification points) on $D$ and corresponding ramified points on $C$.
\end{definition}

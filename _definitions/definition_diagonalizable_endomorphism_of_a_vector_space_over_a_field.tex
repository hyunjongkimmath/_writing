\begin{definition}[Diagonalizable Endomorphism] \label{definition:diagonalizable_endomorphism_of_a_vector_space_over_a_field}
Let $K$ be a \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:field}{field}, let $V$ be an \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:vector_space_over_a_field}{$K$-vector space} of finite \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:basis_of_a_vector_space_over_a_field}{dimension}, and let $\phi : V \to V$ be an \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:morphism_of_vector_spaces}{$K$-linear endomorphism} of $V$.

The endomorphism $\phi$ is called \hldef{diagonalizable} if $V$ admits a basis consisting entirely of eigenvectors of $\phi$. Equivalently, $V$ decomposes as a direct sum of eigenspaces
$$V = \bigoplus_{\lambda \in K} E_\lambda(\phi),$$
where $E_\lambda(\phi)$ is the \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:eigenspace_of_an_endomorphism_of_a_vector_space_over_a_field}{eigenspace} of $\phi$ corresponding to $\lambda \in K$.
\end{definition}

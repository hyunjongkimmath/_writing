\begin{definition}[Ideal Class Group of a Dedekind Domain] \label{definition:ideal_class_group_of_a_dedekind_domain}
Let $R$ be a \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:dedekind_domain}{Dedekind domain} with \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:field_of_fractions_of_an_integral_domain}{field of fractions} $K$. Let $\operatorname{FracId}(R)$ denote the monoid of \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:fractional_ideal_of_an_integral_domain}{fractional ideals} of $R$, let $\operatorname{InvFracId}(R) \subseteq \operatorname{FracId}(R)$ denote the subgroup of \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:fractional_ideal_of_an_integral_domain}{invertible fractional ideals}, and let $\operatorname{PFracId}(R) \subseteq \operatorname{InvFracId}(R)$ denote the subgroup of \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:principal_fractional_ideal_of_an_integral_domain}{principal fractional ideals}.

The \hldef{ideal class group of $R$}, denoted by \hl{$\operatorname{Cl}(R)$}, is defined as the \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:quotient_of_a_group_by_a_normal_subgroup}{quotient group}
$$\operatorname{Cl}(R) = \operatorname{InvFracId}(R) / \operatorname{PFracId}(R).$$

The order of $\operatorname{Cl}(R)$, denoted by \hl{$h_R$} or \hl{$h(R)$}, is called the \hldef{class number of $R$}. The class group measures the failure of unique factorization in $R$: $R$ has \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:unique_factorization_domain}{unique factorization} into \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:irreducible_element_of_a_ring}{irreducible elements} (i.e., $R$ is a \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:principal_ideal_ring}{principal ideal domain}) if and only if $\operatorname{Cl}(R)$ is trivial.

When $R = \mathcal{O}_K$ is the \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:ring_of_integers_of_a_global_or_local_ring}{ring of integers} of a \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:number_field}{number field} $K$, we write $\operatorname{Cl}(K)$ for $\operatorname{Cl}(\mathcal{O}_K)$.
\end{definition}

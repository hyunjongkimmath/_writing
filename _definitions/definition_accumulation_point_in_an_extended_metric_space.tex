\begin{definition}[Accumulation point in an extended metric space] \label{definition:accumulation_point_in_an_extended_metric_space}
    Let $T$ be an \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:ordered_semiring}{ordered semiring}, let $T^\infty$ be the \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:ordered_semiring_with_adjoined_infinity}{ordered semiring with adjoined infinity} obtained from $T$, and let $(X,d)$ be an \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:extended_metric_on_a_set_valued_in_an_ordered_semiring_with_adjoined_infinity}{extended metric space valued in $T^\infty$}. Let $A \subseteq X$ be a subset.

    A point $x \in X$ is called an \hldef{accumulation point/limit point of $A$ (with respect to $d$} if for every $\varepsilon \in T_{>0}$ there exists a point $y \in A \setminus \{x\}$ such that $d(x,y) < \varepsilon$.
    Equivalently, $x$ is an accumulation point of $A$ if every open ball $B_d(x,\varepsilon) = \{y \in X : d(x,y) < \varepsilon\}$ with $\varepsilon \in T_{>0}$ contains a point of $A$ distinct from $x$.

    By an \hldef{accumulation point/limit point of $X$ (with respect to $d$)}, we simply mean an accumulation point in the above set of $X$ as a subset of itself.
\end{definition}

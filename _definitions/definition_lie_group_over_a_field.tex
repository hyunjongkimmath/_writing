    Let $K \in \{\mathbb{R}, \mathbb{C}\}$. A \hldef{$K$-Lie group} is a \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:C_k_manifold}{$C^\infty$ manifold} $G$ over $K$, together with the structure of a \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:group}{group}, such that the multiplication map
    $$\hlin{\mu : G \times G \to G, \quad (g, h) \mapsto g \cdot h}$$
    and the inversion map
    $$\hlin{\iota : G \to G, \quad g \mapsto g^{-1}}$$
    are smooth maps of manifolds over $K$.

    When $K = \mathbb{R}$, we say \hldef{real Lie group}; when $K = \mathbb{C}$, we say \hldef{complex Lie group}. If the context is clear, we simply say \hldef{Lie group}.

    A \hldef{Lie group homomorphism} between two $K$-Lie groups $G$ and $H$ is a map $\varphi : G \to H$ that is both a group homomorphism and a smooth map.
\end{definition}

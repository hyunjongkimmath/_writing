\begin{definition}[Product probability space] \label{definition:product_probability_space}
Let $(\Omega_1, \mathcal{F}_1, P_1)$ and $(\Omega_2, \mathcal{F}_2, P_2)$ be two \CrefAndHyperref{definition:probability_measure_space_for_a_measure_on_a_measurable_space}{probability measure spaces}.

The \hldef{product probability space} is the triple $(\Omega_1 \times \Omega_2, \mathcal{F}_1 \otimes \mathcal{F}_2, P_1 \times P_2)$ where:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item $\mathcal{F}_1 \otimes \mathcal{F}_2$ is the \CrefAndHyperref{definition:product_sigma_algebra_and_product_measure_for_two_measure_spaces}{product $\sigma$-algebra} on $\Omega_1 \times \Omega_2$, and
    \item $P_1 \times P_2$ is the \CrefAndHyperref{definition:product_sigma_algebra_and_product_measure_for_two_measure_spaces}{product measure}, which satisfies $(P_1 \times P_2)(A_1 \times A_2) = P_1(A_1) P_2(A_2)$ for all $A_1 \in \mathcal{F}_1$ and $A_2 \in \mathcal{F}_2$.
\end{enumerate}

Since $P_1(\Omega_1) = P_2(\Omega_2) = 1$, the product measure satisfies $(P_1 \times P_2)(\Omega_1 \times \Omega_2) = 1$, so the product is again a probability space. This construction extends inductively to finite products and, via the Kolmogorov extension theorem, to countably infinite products.
\end{definition}

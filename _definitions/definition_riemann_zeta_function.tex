\begin{definition} \label{definition:riemann_zeta_function}
Let $s \in \mathbb{C}$. The \hldef{Riemann zeta function} \hl{$\zeta(s)$} is defined as
$$\hlin{\zeta(s) = \sum_{n=1}^\infty \frac{1}{n^s},}$$
\CrefIfExists{definition:infinite_series_and_convergence_of_infinite_series_in_a_topological_abelian_semigroup}
which \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:absolute_convergence_of_a_series_in_module_over_a_commutative_ring_with_an_absolute_value_valued_in_an_ordered_semiring}{converges absolutely} when $\Re(s) > 1$\CrefIfExists{lemma:riemann_zeta_function_converges_absolutely_on_the_right_half_plane_with_real_part_greater_than_one}, and in this case $\zeta(s)$ can be expressed as an Euler product:
$$\zeta(s) = \prod_{n=1}^\infty \frac{1}{(1-p^{-s})}.$$
\CrefIfExists{definition:infinite_series_and_convergence_of_infinite_series_in_a_topological_abelian_semigroup}

Here are alternate definitions of $\zeta(s)$: 
\begin{enumerate}
    \item It is a special case of the \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:dirichlet_L_function_of_a_dirichlet_character_modulo_a_nonnegative_integer}{Dirichlet $L$-function $L(s,\chi)$} in case that $\chi: \bbZ \to \bbC$ is the trivial Dirichlet character (modulo $0$) such that $\chi(n) = 1$ for all integers $n \neq 0$\CrefIfExists{definition:dirichlet_L_function_of_a_dirichlet_character_modulo_a_nonnegative_integer}. 
    \item It is a special case of the \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:dedekind_zeta_function_of_a_number_field}{Dedekind zeta function $\zeta_K(s)$} in the case that the number field $K$ is simply the field $\bbQ$ of rational numbers\CrefIfExists{definition:dedekind_zeta_function_of_a_number_field}.
\end{enumerate}
\end{definition}

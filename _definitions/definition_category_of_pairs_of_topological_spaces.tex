\begin{definition} \label{definition:category_of_pairs_of_topological_spaces}
The \hldef{category of pairs of topological spaces}, often denoted by \hl{$\mathbf{Top}_{\text{pair}}$} or \hl{$\mathbf{Top}_{\text{rel}}$} ($\text{rel}$ for relative), is the \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:category}{category} defined by:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Objects:} \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:pair_of_topological_spaces}{Pairs} of topological spaces $(X, A)$.
    \item \textbf{Morphisms:} \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:map_of_pairs_of_topological_spaces}{Maps of pairs} $f: (X, A) \to (Y, B)$.
    \item \textbf{Composition:} The composition of morphisms is the usual composition of continuous maps, which naturally preserves the subset relation: if $f(A) \subseteq B$ and $g(B) \subseteq C$, then $(g \circ f)(A) \subseteq C$.
    \item \textbf{Identity:} For each object $(X, A)$, the identity morphism is the identity map $\operatorname{id}_X: (X, A) \to (X, A)$.
\end{itemize}
It is a \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:locally_small_category}{locally small category}.

\end{definition}

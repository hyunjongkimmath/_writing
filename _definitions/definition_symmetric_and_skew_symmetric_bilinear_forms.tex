\begin{definition} \label{definition:symmetric_and_skew_symmetric_bilinear_forms}
    Let $F$ be a \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:field}{field}, and let $B$ be a \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:bilinear_form_on_a_vector_space}{bilinear form} on an $F$-vector space $V$.

    \begin{itemize}
        \item $B$ is called \hldef{symmetric} if $B(v, w) = B(w, v)$ for all $v, w \in V$.
        \item $B$ is called \hldef{skew-symmetric} (or \hldef{alternating}) if $B(v, w) = -B(w, v)$ for all $v, w \in V$. Equivalently, $B(v, v) = 0$ for all $v \in V$ (assuming $\operatorname{char}(F) \neq 2$).
    \end{itemize}

    A symmetric bilinear form is a special case of a \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:quadratic_form_over_a_ring_with_involution}{quadratic form} via the correspondence $Q(v) = B(v, v)$ (when $\operatorname{char}(F) \neq 2$).

\TextIfExists{definition:quadratic_form_over_a_ring_with_involution}{Symmetric and skew-symmetric bilinear forms on vector spaces are special cases of symmetric and skew-symmetric sesquilinear forms on modules over a ring with involution, relating to \Cref{definition:quadratic_form_over_a_ring_with_involution} via the polarization identity.}

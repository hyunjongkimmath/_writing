\begin{definition}[Duration and convexity of a bond] \label{definition:duration_and_convexity_of_a_bond}

Let $P(y)$ denote the price of a bond as a function of its yield to maturity $y$.

The \hldef{Macaulay duration} of the bond is
$$\hlin{D_{\text{Mac}} = -\frac{1}{P} \sum_{i=1}^n t_i \cdot \text{PV}(CF_i)},$$
where $\text{PV}(CF_i)$ is the present value of the $i$-th cash flow occurring at time $t_i$.

The \hldef{modified duration} is
$$\hlin{D_{\text{mod}} = -\frac{1}{P} \frac{dP}{dy} = \frac{D_{\text{Mac}}}{1 + y/k}},$$
where $k$ is the compounding frequency. Modified duration measures the first-order percentage price change per unit change in yield:
$$\frac{\Delta P}{P} \approx -D_{\text{mod}} \cdot \Delta y.$$

The \hldef{convexity} of the bond is
$$\hlin{C = \frac{1}{P} \frac{d^2P}{dy^2}}.$$
The second-order approximation to the price change is
$$\frac{\Delta P}{P} \approx -D_{\text{mod}} \cdot \Delta y + \frac{1}{2} C \cdot (\Delta y)^2.$$

For a zero-coupon bond maturing at time $T$, Macaulay duration equals $T$.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Lebesgue integral over a measurable subset of $\mathbb{R}^n$] \label{definition:lebesgue_integral_over_a_measurable_subset_of_Rn}

    Let $E \subseteq \mathbb{R}^n$ be a \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:lebesgue_measure_on_Rn}{Lebesgue measurable subset} and let $f : E \to \overline{\mathbb{R}}$ be a measurable function.

    The \hldef{Lebesgue integral of $f$ over $E$} is defined by extending $f$ by zero outside $E$:
    $$\hlin{\int_E f\,d\lambda^n := \int_{\mathbb{R}^n} \tilde f\,d\lambda^n,}$$
    \CrefIfExists{definition:lebesgue_integral_of_measurable_functions_into_the}
    where $\tilde f : \mathbb{R}^n \to \overline{\mathbb{R}}$ is defined by $\tilde f(x) = f(x)$ for $x \in E$ and $\tilde f(x) = 0$ for $x \notin E$.

    The integral is said to \hldef{converge} (or $f$ is \hldef{integrable over $E$}) when $\int_E |f|\,d\lambda^n < \infty$.

    In particular, for a nonnegative measurable function $f : E \to [0,\infty]$, the integral $\int_E f\,d\lambda^n$ is always defined as an element of $[0,\infty]$ (possibly infinite).
\end{definition}

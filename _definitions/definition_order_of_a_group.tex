\begin{definition} \label{definition:order_of_a_group}
Let $G$ be a \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:group}{group}. The \hldef{order of $G$}, denoted by \hl{$|G|$}, is the \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:cardinal_number_and_cardinality_of_a_set}{cardinality} of the underlying set of $G$. If $|G|$ is \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:countable_finite_uncountable_sets}{finite}, then $G$ is called a \hldef{finite group}. Otherwise, $G$ is called an \hldef{infinite group}.
\end{definition}

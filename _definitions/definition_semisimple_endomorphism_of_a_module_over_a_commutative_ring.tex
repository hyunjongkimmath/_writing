\begin{definition}[Semisimple endomorphism of a module over a commutative ring] \label{definition:semisimple_endomorphism_of_a_module_over_a_commutative_ring}

Let $R$ be a \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:commutative_ring}{commutative ring}, let $M$ be an $R$-module, and let $\phi : M \to M$ be an $R$-linear endomorphism.

The endomorphism $\phi$ is called \hldef{semisimple (endomorphism of a module over a commutative ring)} if every $\phi$-invariant submodule $N \subseteq M$ admits a $\phi$-invariant complement — that is, there exists an $R$-submodule $N' \subseteq M$ such that $M = N \oplus N'$ and $\phi(N') \subseteq N'$.

Equivalently, viewing $M$ as an $R[t]$-module via the action $t \cdot m := \phi(m)$ (where $R$ acts by scalar multiplication), the endomorphism $\phi$ is semisimple if and only if this $R[t]$-module is \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:simple_irreducible_semisimple_module_over_a_ring}{semisimple}.
\end{definition}

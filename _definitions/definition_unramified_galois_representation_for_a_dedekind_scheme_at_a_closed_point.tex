\begin{definition}[Unramified Galois representation at a closed point] \label{definition:unramified_galois_representation_for_a_dedekind_scheme_at_a_closed_point}
    Let $S$ be a \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:dedekind_scheme}{Dedekind scheme} with \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:function_field_over_a_field}{function field} $K$. Fix a \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:separable_closure_of_a_field}{separable closure} $\bar{K}$ of $K$, and let $G_K = \Gal(\bar{K}/K)$ be the \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:absolute_galois_group_of_a_field}{absolute Galois group} of $K$. Let $s \in S$ be a \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:closed_point_of_a_topological_space}{closed point}, and fix a prime $\bar{\mathfrak{s}}$ of the \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:integral_element_over_a_ring}{integral closure} of $\mathcal{O}_{S, s}$ in $\bar{K}$. Let $I_s \subset G_K$ be the corresponding \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:inertia_group_in_the_absolute_galois_group_of_the_function_field_of_a_dedekind_scheme_at_a_closed_point}{inertia subgroup}.

    Let $M$ be a \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:topological_module_over_topological_rings}{continuous} $G_K$-module (such as a finite torsion module or an \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:ell_adic_tate_module_of_an_abelian_variety_over_a_field}{$\ell$-adic Tate module}). We say that $M$ is \hldef{unramified at $s$} if the \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:inertia_group_in_the_absolute_galois_group_of_the_function_field_of_a_dedekind_scheme_at_a_closed_point}{inertia group} $I_s$ acts \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:trivial_G_module_of_a_group_over_a_ring}{trivially} on $M$, i.e.,
    $$\sigma \cdot m = m \quad \text{for all } \sigma \in I_s \text{ and all } m \in M.$$

    In particular, for an abelian variety $A_K$ over $K$ and a prime $\ell$ distinct from the characteristic of $k(s)$, we say that the Tate module $T_\ell(A_K)$ (resp. $V_\ell(A_K)$) is \hldef{unramified at $s$} if $I_s$ acts trivially on $T_\ell(A_K)(\bar{K})$ (resp. $V_\ell(A_K)$).

    \begin{remark}
        Although the inertia group $I_s$ depends on the choice of $\bar{\mathfrak{s}}$, the property of being unramified at $s$ does not: all inertia groups at $s$ are conjugate in $G_K$, and since any continuous $G_K$-module is stable under the full Galois group, if one inertia subgroup acts trivially on $M$, then all do.
    \end{remark}
\end{definition}

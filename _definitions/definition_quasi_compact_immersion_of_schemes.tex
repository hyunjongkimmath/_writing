\begin{definition}[Quasi-compact immersion of schemes] \label{definition:quasi_compact_immersion_of_schemes}

    Let $f: X \to Y$ be a \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:morphism_of_schemes}{morphism of schemes}.

    The morphism $f$ is called a \hldef{quasi-compact immersion} if it is both an \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:locally_closed_imersion_of_schemes}{immersion} and \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:quasi_compact_morphism_of_schemes}{quasi-compact}.

    When $f$ is a quasi-compact immersion, it factors as $X \xrightarrow{j} \overline{X} \xrightarrow{i} Y$, where $j$ is an \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:open_and_closed_immersions_of_schemes}{open immersion} and $i$ is a \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:open_and_closed_immersions_of_schemes}{closed immersion}. This contrasts with a general immersion, which factors as closed followed by open.
\end{definition}

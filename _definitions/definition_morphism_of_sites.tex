\begin{definition} \label{definition:morphism_of_sites}
    Let $(\calC,J)$ and $(\calD,K)$ be \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:grothendieck_topology_on_a_category_site_covering_sieve_topologically_generating_family}{sites}. 
    A \hldef{morphism of sites} $f: (\calD, K) \to (\calC, J)$ consists of a \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:continuous_functor_of_sites}{continuous functor} $u: \calC \to \calD$ such that the \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:inverse_image_of_a_sheaf_under_a_continuous_functor_of_sites_or_a_site_morphism}{inverse image functor} $u^*: \Sh(\calD, K; \Sets) \to \Sh(\calC, J; \Sets)$ (\Cref{definition:sheaf_on_a_site}) is \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:left_right_exact_functor_between_categories}{left exact} , i.e. commutes with finite \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:projective_and_inductive_limits_in_categories}{projective limits}. 

    When the sites are \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:small_site_locally_small_site_U_site_U_small_site_U_topology}{small}, a morphism of sites is exactly a continuous and \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:flat_functor_between_categories}{flat functor}.

\end{definition}

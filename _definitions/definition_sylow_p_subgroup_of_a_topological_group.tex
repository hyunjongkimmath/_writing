\begin{definition}[Sylow $p$-Subgroup of a Topological Group] \label{definition:sylow_p_subgroup_of_a_topological_group}
Let $G$ be a \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:profinite_group}{profinite group} and let $p$ be a prime number. A closed subgroup $P \subseteq G$ is called a \hldef{Sylow $p$-subgroup of $G$} if:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item $P$ is a \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:pro_p_subgroup_of_a_topological_group}{pro-$p$ subgroup} of $G$, and
    \item the \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:index_of_a_subgroup_in_a_group}{index} $[G : P]$ is prime to $p$, i.e., for every open \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:normal_subgroup_and_characteristic_subgroup_of_an_algebraic_group_over_a_scheme}{normal subgroup} $N \triangleleft G$, the integer $[G/N : PN/N]$ is not divisible by $p$.
\end{enumerate}

Equivalently, a Sylow $p$-subgroup of $G$ is a maximal pro-$p$ closed subgroup of $G$. Any two Sylow $p$-subgroups of $G$ are \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:conjugation_of_group_elements_and_subgroup_by_a_group_element}{conjugate} in $G$, and every pro-$p$ closed subgroup of $G$ is contained in a Sylow $p$-subgroup.

The set of all Sylow $p$-subgroups of $G$ is denoted by \hl{$\operatorname{Syl}_p(G)$}. When $G$ is finite, this recovers the classical notion of \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:sylow_p_subgroup_of_a_finite_group}{Sylow $p$-subgroups}.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition} \label{definition:finite_field}
    A \hldef{finite field} is a \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:field}{field} whose underlying set has \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:countable_finite_uncountable_sets}{finite} \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:cardinal_number_and_cardinality_of_a_set}{cardinality}.

    All finite fields have cardinality equal to the \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:power_of_an_element_of_a_ring_by_a_nonnegative_integer}{power} of a \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:prime_number}{prime number}. Moreover, for every $q = p^k$ for $p$ a prime number and $k \geq 0$ an integer, any two fields of cardinality $q$ are \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:field_homomorphism}{isomorphic as fields}, and there exists a field of cardinality $q$\CrefIfExists{theorem:finite_fields_are_exactly_characterized_by_their_cardinality}. One denotes by \hl{$\bbF_q$} a finite field of cardinality $q$. 
\end{definition}

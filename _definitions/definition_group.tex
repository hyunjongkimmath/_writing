\begin{definition}[Groups] \label{definition:group}
    A \hldef{group} is a \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:monoid}{monoid} such that every element has an \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{lemma:inverse_of_an_element_for_a_binary_operation_on_a_set_is_unique_if_exists}{inverse}.

    In other words, a group is a pair $(G,\cdot)$ where $G$ is a \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:zermelo_fraenkel_set_theory}{set} and $\cdot : G \times G \to G$ is a \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:n_ary_operation_binary_operation_ternary_operation_on_a_set}{binary operation}, subject to the following conditions:

    \begin{enumerate}
        \item (Associativity) For all $g,h,k \in G$ one has 
        $$ (g \cdot h) \cdot k = g \cdot (h \cdot k). $$
        \item (Identity element) There exists an \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:left_right_identity_element_of_a_binary_operation_on_a_set}{identity element}, i.e. an element \hl{$e \in G$} such that for all $g \in G$, 
        $$ e \cdot g = g \cdot e = g. $$
        \item (Inverse elements) For all $g \in G$, there exists an \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:left_right_inverse_of_an_element_for_a_binary_operation_on_a_set}{inverse element} of $g$, i.e. an element \hl{$g^{-1} \in G$} such that 
        $$ g \cdot g^{-1} = g^{-1} \cdot g = e. $$
    \end{enumerate}

    \TextIfExists{definition:group_object_in_a_category_with_a_final_object}{Equivalently, a group is a \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:group_object_in_a_category_with_a_final_object}{group object} in the \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:category_of_sets}{category of sets}.}

    A group $(G, \cdot)$ is often simply written as $G$, when the notation for the binary operation $\cdot$ is clear. 
    
    Assuming that the notation for the identity element of a group is not explicitly specified, 
    \begin{enumerate}
        \item if the group operation is written multiplicatively, then one usually implicitly writes \hl{$e$} or \hl{$1$} as the identity of $G$, and 
        \item If the group operation is instead written additively, then one usually implicitly write \hl{$0$} as the identity of $G$.
    \end{enumerate}

    An \hldef{abelian group} or synonymously, a \hldef{commutative group}, is a group $(G,\cdot)$ whose binary operation $\cdot$ is \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:commutative_binary_operation_on_a_set}{\hldef{abelian} or \hldef{commutative}}, i.e. satisfies 
    $$g \cdot h = h \cdot g$$
    for all $g,h \in G$. A group operation written additively, i.e. with \hl{$+$}, is almost usually abelian. An abelian group's group operation may still often be written multiplicatively.


    \TextIfExists{definition:module_of_a_ring}{An abelian group is equivalent to a $\bbZ$-module.}

\end{definition}

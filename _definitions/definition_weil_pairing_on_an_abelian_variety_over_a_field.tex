\begin{definition}[Weil pairing] \label{definition:weil_pairing_on_an_abelian_variety_over_a_field}

    Let $k$ be a \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:field}{field} and let $A$ be an abelian variety over $k$. For each integer $n \geq 1$, the \hldef{Weil pairing on $A[n]$}, denoted by
        $$e_n^A : A[n](\bar{k}) \times A^\vee[n](\bar{k}) \to \mu_n(\bar{k}),$$
    is a perfect, bilinear, alternating pairing between the $n$-torsion points of $A$ and the $n$-torsion points of its dual abelian variety $A^\vee$, taking values in the group $\mu_n$ of $n$th roots of unity. If $A$ is equipped with a \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:principal_polarization_of_an_abelian_variety_over_a_field}{principal polarization} $\lambda : A \xrightarrow{\sim} A^\vee$, then the composition
        $$\langle \cdot, \cdot \rangle_n := e_n^A(\cdot, \lambda(\cdot)) : A[n](\bar{k}) \times A[n](\bar{k}) \to \mu_n(\bar{k})$$
    is a perfect, alternating pairing on $A[n]$. The Weil pairing is functorial in $A$ and compatible with base change.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Degree of an isogeny] \label{definition:degree_of_an_isogeny_of_group_schemes}
    Let $f\colon G_1 \to G_2$ be an \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:isogeny_of_algebraic_group_schemes_over_a_scheme}{isogeny of group schemes over a scheme $S$}. 

    The \hldef{degree of $f$}, denoted by \hl{$\deg(f)$}, is the degree of the \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:degree_of_a_finite_morphism_of_schemes}{finite morphism} $f$. %(i.e., the degree of the field extension induced on function fields when $G_1$ and $G_2$ are connected).
     Equivalently, if $f$ is a \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:finite_morphism_of_schemes}{finite flat morphism}, then
    \TODO{rank}
    $$\deg(f) = \operatorname{rank}_{\mathcal{O}_{G_2}} f_*\mathcal{O}_{G_1},$$
    the rank of $f_*\mathcal{O}_{G_1}$ as a \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:free_and_locally_free_modules_on_a_ringed_site}{locally free} $\mathcal{O}_{G_2}$-module. When $S$ is $\Spec k$ for an \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:algebraically_closed_field}{algebraically closed field} $k$ and the kernel $\ker(f)$ is \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:etale_morphism_of_schemes}{étale}, this equals the order of the finite group $\ker(f)(k)$.
\end{definition}

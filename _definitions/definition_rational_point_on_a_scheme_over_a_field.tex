\begin{definition} \label{definition:rational_point_on_a_scheme_over_a_field}
    Let $K$ be a \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:field}{field}. Let $X$ be a \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:scheme_over_a_scheme}{scheme over} $K$.  

    A \hldef{$K$-rational point of $X$} or a \hldef{$K$-point of $X$} is a \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:scheme_over_a_scheme}{$K$-morphism} $\operatorname{Spec}(K) \to X$. 
    
    Equivalently, a $K$-rational point is an element of $h_X(\operatorname{Spec} K)$ where $h_X$ is the \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:representable_functor_on_a_locally_small_category}{representable functor} $\Sch_K \to \Sets$. One writes the set of $K$-rational points of $X$ as \hl{$X(K)$}.
    \TextIfExists{definition:point_on_a_scheme_valued_on_a_scheme}{However, the notion of $K$-rational points should not be mistaken in the above sense should technically not be mistaken for the notion of $K$-points of $X$ in the sense of \Cref{definition:point_on_a_scheme_valued_on_a_scheme}, where general scheme morphisms $X \to \Spec K$ not necessarily over $\Spec K$ are taken}
\end{definition}

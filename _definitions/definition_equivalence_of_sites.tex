\begin{definition} \label{definition:equivalence_of_sites}
Let $(\mathcal{C}_1, J_1)$ and $(\mathcal{C}_2, J_2)$ be \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:grothendieck_topology_on_a_category_site_covering_sieve_topologically_generating_family}{sites}. An \hldef{equivalence of sites} from $(\mathcal{C}_1, J_1)$ to $(\mathcal{C}_2, J_2)$ consists of an \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:equivalence_of_categories}{equivalence of categories}
$$F: \mathcal{C}_1 \to \mathcal{C}_2, \qquad G: \mathcal{C}_2 \to \mathcal{C}_1$$
with \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:natural_transformation_between_functors_between_categories}{natural isomorphisms}
$$\eta: \mathrm{Id}_{\mathcal{C}_1} \xrightarrow{\sim} G \circ F, \qquad \epsilon: F \circ G \xrightarrow{\sim} \mathrm{Id}_{\mathcal{C}_2}$$
such that both $F$ and $G$ are \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:continuous_functor_of_sites}{continuous functors of sites}. In this situation, we say that $(\mathcal{C}_1, J_1)$ and $(\mathcal{C}_2, J_2)$ are \hldef{equivalent as sites} (or simply \hldef{equivalent sites}).

\begin{remark}
    The continuity of both $F$ and $G$ ensures that the Grothendieck topologies $J_1$ and $J_2$ correspond under the equivalence of the underlying categories. In particular, a sieve $S$ on an object $U$ of $\mathcal{C}_1$ is $J_1$-covering if and only if the sieve generated by $\{F(\phi) \mid \phi \in S\}$ is $J_2$-covering on $F(U)$, and similarly for $G$.
\end{remark}
\end{definition}

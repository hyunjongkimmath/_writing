\begin{definition} \label{definition:absolutely_simple_abelian_variety_over_a_field}
    Let $k$ be a \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:field}{field}, let $\overline{k}$ be an \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:algebraic_closure_of_a_field}{algebraic closure} of $k$, and let $A$ be an \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:abelian_variety_over_a_field}{abelian variety over $k$}. Let $A_{\overline{k}} = A \times_k \overline{k}$ denote the \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:base_change_of_a_morphism_in_a_category_by_a_morphism}{base change} of $A$ to $\overline{k}$.

    We say that $A$ is an \hldef{absolutely simple abelian variety over $k$} (or a \hldef{geometrically simple abelian variety over $k$}) if $A_{\overline{k}}$ is a \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:simple_abelian_variety_over_a_field}{simple abelian variety over $\overline{k}$}.

    Equivalently, $A$ is absolutely simple if $\dim A > 0$ and the only closed subgroup schemes of $A_{\overline{k}}$ which are abelian varieties over $\overline{k}$ are the trivial subgroup scheme $\{0_{A_{\overline{k}}}\}$ and $A_{\overline{k}}$ itself.
\end{definition}

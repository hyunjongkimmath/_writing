\begin{definition} \label{definition:representation_of_a_lie_group_on_a_topological_vector_space}

    Let $G$ be a \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:lie_group_over_a_field}{Lie group} over $\mathbb{R}$ or $\mathbb{C}$, and $V$ a finite-dimensional \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:vector_space_over_a_field}{vector space} over $\mathbb{C}$.

    A \hldef{(finite-dimensional) representation of $G$ on $V$} is a group homomorphism
    $$\hlin{\rho : G \to \mathrm{GL}(V)}$$
    that is smooth when $\mathrm{GL}(V)$ is viewed as a Lie group. Equivalently, the map
    $$\hlin{G \times V \to V, \quad (g, v) \mapsto \rho(g)v}$$
    is smooth. The pair $(\rho, V)$ is often referred to as a \hldef{representation of $G$}.

    Every finite-dimensional representation $\rho : G \to \mathrm{GL}(V)$ gives rise to a representation of the associated Lie algebra $\mathfrak{g}$, called the \hldef{derived representation (or differential) of $\rho$}, denoted by
    $$\hlin{\mathrm{d}\rho : \mathfrak{g} \to \mathfrak{gl}(V)},$$
    which is the tangent map at the identity $e \in G$.
\end{definition}

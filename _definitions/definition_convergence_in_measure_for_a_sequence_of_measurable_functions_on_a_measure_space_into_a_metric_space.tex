\begin{definition}[Convergence in measure] \label{definition:convergence_in_measure_for_a_sequence_of_measurable_functions_on_a_measure_space_into_a_metric_space}
Let $(X, \mathcal{M}, \mu)$ be a \CrefAndHyperref{definition:measure_on_a_measurable_space_and_measure_space}{measure space} and let $(Y, d)$ be a \CrefAndHyperref{definition:extended_metric_on_a_set}{metric space}. Let $\{f_n\}_{n=1}^\infty$ be a sequence of \CrefAndHyperref{definition:measurable_function_from_a_measure_space_into_the_extended_real_line_or_complex_plane}{measurable functions} $f_n: X \to Y$, and let $f: X \to Y$ be a measurable function.

The sequence $\{f_n\}$ is said to \hldef{converge in measure (on $(X, \mathcal{M}, \mu)$ into $(Y, d)) to $f$} if for every $\varepsilon > 0$,
$$\mu(\{x \in X : d(f_n(x), f(x)) \geq \varepsilon\}) \to 0 \quad \text{as } n \to \infty.$$
That is, for every $\varepsilon > 0$ and every $\delta > 0$, there exists $N \in \mathbb{N}$ such that for all $n \geq N$,
$$\mu(\{x \in X : d(f_n(x), f(x)) \geq \varepsilon\}) < \delta.$$
\end{definition}

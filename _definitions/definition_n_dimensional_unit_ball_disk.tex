Let $n \ge 0$ be an integer. 
The \hldef{$n$-dimensional (unit) disk (or (unit) ball)}, often denoted by \hl{$D^n$} (or \hl{$B^n$}), is the \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:subspace_of_a_topological_space}{subspace} of \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:euclidean_n_space}{$\mathbb{R}^n$} defined by:
$$D^n = \{x \in \mathbb{R}^n \mid \|x\| \le 1\}$$
For $n=1$, the disk $D^1$ is the \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:interval_in_the_real_line}{closed interval} $I = [-1, 1]$, which is \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:homeomorphism_of_topological_spaces}{homeomorphically} to the standard unit interval $[0, 1]$.

One also often means by an \hldef{$n$-dimensional disk} any space homeomorphic to $D^n$.
\end{definition}

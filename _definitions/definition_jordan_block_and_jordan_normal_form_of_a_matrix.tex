\begin{definition} \label{definition:jordan_block_and_jordan_normal_form_of_a_matrix}
    Let $F$ be a \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:field}{field}, let $\lambda \in F$, and let $k \geq 1$.

    The \hldef{Jordan block of size $k$ with eigenvalue $\lambda$} is the $k \times k$ matrix
    $$\hlin{J_k(\lambda) = \begin{pmatrix}
        \lambda & 1 & 0 & \cdots & 0 \\
        0 & \lambda & 1 & \cdots & 0 \\
        \vdots & \vdots & \ddots & \ddots & \vdots \\
        0 & 0 & \cdots & \lambda & 1 \\
        0 & 0 & \cdots & 0 & \lambda
    \end{pmatrix}}.$$

    A square matrix over $F$ is in \hldef{Jordan normal form} (or is a \hldef{Jordan matrix}) if it is block diagonal with Jordan blocks as its diagonal blocks:
    $$\hlin{J = \operatorname{diag}\bigl(J_{k_1}(\lambda_1), \dots, J_{k_r}(\lambda_r)\bigr)}.$$
\TextIfExists{definition:bimodule_over_rings_module_over_a_ring}{Jordan blocks correspond to the cyclic submodule decomposition of $V$ as an $F[t]$-module via $T$: each Jordan block $J_k(\lambda)$ represents the module $F[t]/(t-\lambda)^k$. The Jordan normal form is the special case of the invariant-factor decomposition for finitely generated modules over a principal ideal domain when all irreducible factors are linear.}

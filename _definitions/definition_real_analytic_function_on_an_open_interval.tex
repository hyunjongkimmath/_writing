\begin{definition} \label{definition:real_analytic_function_on_an_open_interval}

    Let $I \subseteq \mathbb{R}$ be an open interval, let $a \in I$, and let $f : I \to \mathbb{R}$ be a function that is \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:derivative_of_a_real_valued_function_on_a_real_interval}{infinitely differentiable} at every point of $I$. The function $f$ is called \hldef{real analytic at $a$} if there exists a radius $\delta > 0$ such that the open interval $(a - \delta, a + \delta) \subseteq I$, and for all $x \in (a - \delta, a + \delta)$ the function equals its \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:taylor_and_maclaurin_series_of_a_real_valued_function_smooth_at_a_point_in_an_interval_in_the_real_line}{Taylor series centered at $a$}:
    $$f(x) = \sum_{k=0}^\infty \frac{f^{(k)}(a)}{k!}(x - a)^k.$$
    The function $f$ is called \hldef{real analytic on $I$} if it is real analytic at every point of $I$. Equivalently, for each $a \in I$, the Taylor series of $f$ centered at $a$ has a positive \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:real_power_series_and_radius_of_convergence}{radius of convergence} and \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:convergence_and_limit_of_sequence_in_extended_metric_space_valued_in_ordered_commutative_monoid_with_adjoined_infinity}{converges to} $f(x)$ in some neighborhood of $a$.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Net in a topological space] \label{definition:net_in_a_topological_space}

    Let $D$ be a \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:directed_set}{directed set}. A \hldef{net in a topological space $X$ indexed by $D$} is a function from $D$ to $X$, i.e., a family $(x_\alpha)_{\alpha \in D}$ of points of $X$. We often write simply "a net" when the indexing set and ambient space are clear from context.

    Nets generalize sequences: a sequence is exactly a net indexed by $\mathbb{N}$ with its usual ordering. Unlike sequences, nets can have uncountable or otherwise non-well-ordered directed sets as indices, which allows them to capture convergence in arbitrary topological spaces.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Closed point of a topological space] \label{definition:closed_point_of_a_topological_space}
    Let $X$ be a \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:topological_space}{topological space}. A point $x \in X$ is called a \hldef{closed point of $X$} if the singleton set $\{x\}$ is \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:topological_space}{closed} in the topology of $X$, i.e., its \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:closure_of_a_subspace_of_a_topological_space}{closure} satisfies $\overline{\{x\}} = \{x\}$.

    In a \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:separation_axioms_of_topology}{T$_1$ space}, every singleton set is closed, so every point is a closed point. 
    % In the Zariski topology on a scheme over a field, a point $x$ is closed if and only if its \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:residue_field_of_a_scheme_at_a_point}{residue field} $\kappa(x)$ is a finite extension of the base field.
\end{definition}

Let $G$ be a \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:simple_graph_directed_graph_multigraph_weighted_graph}{simple graph}.

The graph $G$ is called a \hldef{bipartite graph} if there exists a partition of $V(G)$ into two disjoint sets $A$ and $B$, such that every edge in $E(G)$ has one endpoint in $A$ and the other in $B$. The sets $A$ and $B$ are called the \hldef{parts of the bipartite graph}.

Let $m, n$ be positive integers. A \hldef{complete bipartite graph with parts of size $m$ and $n$}, denoted by \hl{$K_{m,n}$}, is a bipartite graph with parts $A$ and $B$ where $|A| = m$, $|B| = n$, and every vertex in $A$ is adjacent to every vertex in $B$.
\end{definition}

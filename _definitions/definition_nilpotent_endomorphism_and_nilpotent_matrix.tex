\begin{definition} \label{definition:nilpotent_endomorphism_and_nilpotent_matrix}
    Let $F$ be a \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:field}{field}, and let $V$ be an \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:vector_space_over_a_field}{$F$-vector space}.

    A linear endomorphism $T: V \to V$ is called \hldef{nilpotent} if there exists a positive integer $k$ such that $T^k = 0$. The smallest such $k$ is called the \hldef{index of nilpotency of $T$}.

    An $n \times n$ \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:matrix_over_a_ring}{matrix} $A$ over $F$ is called \hldef{nilpotent} if $A^k = 0$ for some positive integer $k$.

\begin{definition}[Principal Fractional Ideal] \label{definition:principal_fractional_ideal_of_an_integral_domain}
Let $R$ be an \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:domain_integral_domain}{integral domain} and let $K = \operatorname{Frac}(R)$ be its \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:field_of_fractions_of_an_integral_domain}{field of fractions}. Let $\operatorname{FracId}(R)$ denote the set of \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:fractional_ideal_of_an_integral_domain}{fractional ideals} of $R$.

A fractional ideal $I$ of $R$ is called a \hldef{principal fractional ideal} if there exists an element $\alpha \in K^\times$ such that $I = (\alpha) = \alpha R$. The set of all principal fractional ideals is denoted by \hl{$\operatorname{PFracId}(R)$}.

The map $K^\times \to \operatorname{FracId}(R)$ given by $\alpha \mapsto (\alpha)$ is a surjective homomorphism from the multiplicative group $K^\times$ onto $\operatorname{PFracId}(R)$, with kernel $R^\times$. Thus $\operatorname{PFracId}(R) \cong K^\times / R^\times$ as groups.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition} \label{definition:free_bimodule_over_rings}
Let $R$ and $S$ be \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:ring}{(not necessarily commutative) rings}. Let $I$ be a set. 

The \hldef{free $R-S$-bimodule indexed by $I$} is the \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:coproduct_of_modules_of_rings}{direct sum} $(R \otimes_{\mathbb{Z}} S^{\op})^{\oplus I}$ where $R \otimes_{\mathbb{Z}} S^{\op}$\CrefIfExists{definition:tensor_product_of_a_ring_and_an_algebra_over_a_ring}\CrefIfExists{definition:opposite_ring_of_a_ring} is equipped with the natural $R$-$S$-bimodules structure. 

An $R$-$S$-bimodule is said to be \hldef{free} if it is isomorphic to such a bimodule.

Equivalently, a free $R$-$S$-bimodule can be described by a \hldef{basis} $B = \{b_i\}_{i \in I}$ --- the \hldef{free $R$-$S$-bimodule generated by $B$} is the abelian group of formal finite sums
\begin{equation} \label{eq:general_elements_of_free_R_S_bimodule}
\sum_{j=1}^n r_{i_j} b_{i_j}  s_{i_j}
\end{equation}
where $n \in \mathbb{N}$, $i_j \in I$, $r_{i_j} \in R$, and $s_{i_j} \in S$, subject only to the relations required by the $R$-$S$-bimodule axioms. More explicitly, the relations are, for all $i \in I$:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Distributivity (Left):} $(r_1 + r_2) b_i s = r_1 b_i s + r_2 b_i s$
    \item \textbf{Distributivity (Right):} $r b_i (s_1 + s_2) = r b_i s_1 + r b_i s_2$
    \item \textbf{Scalar Interaction:} $r b_i (s_1 s_2) = (r b_i s_1) s_2$ and $(r_1 r_2) b_i s = r_1 (r_2 b_i s)$
    \item \textbf{Centrality of $\mathbb{Z}$:} For any $n \in \mathbb{Z}$, $n(r b_i s) = (nr) b_i s = r b_i (ns)$
\end{itemize}
Furthermore, an element of the form \eqref{eq:general_elements_of_free_R_S_bimodule} is zero if and only if for each $i \in I$, the term $\sum_{j: i_j = i} r_{i_j} \otimes s_{i_j}$ is zero in the tensor product $R \otimes_{\mathbb{Z}} S^{\text{op}}$.

A \hldef{free left $R$-module} can thus simply be defined/identified as a free $R-\bbZ$-bimodule and a \hldef{free right $R$-module} can thus simply be defined/identified as a free $\bbZ-R$-bimodule.
\end{definition}

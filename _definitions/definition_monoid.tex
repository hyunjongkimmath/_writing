\begin{definition}[Monoid] \label{definition:monoid}
    % EDITOR APPROVED
A \hldef{monoid} is a \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:semigroup}{semigroup} $(M,\cdot)$ such that there exists an \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:left_right_identity_element_of_a_binary_operation_on_a_set}{identity element} $e \in M$ for $\cdot$, i.e. $e$ has the property:
$$ e \cdot a = a \cdot e = a \quad \text{for all } a \in M. $$

A monoid is called \hldef{commutative} or \hldef{abelian} if its semigroup structure is.

\TextIfExists{definition:monoid_object_in_a_category_with_a_final_object}{Equivalently, a monoid is a \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:monoid_object_in_a_category_with_a_final_object}{monoid object} in the \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:category_of_sets}{category of sets}.}
\end{definition}

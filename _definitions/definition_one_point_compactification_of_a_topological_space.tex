\begin{definition}[One-point compactification of a topological space] \label{definition:one_point_compactification_of_a_topological_space}

    Let $X$ be a \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:topological_space}{topological space} that is Hausdorff and locally compact. The \hldef{one-point compactification} of $X$, denoted by \hl{$X^\infty = X \cup \{\infty\}$}, is the set $X \sqcup \{\infty\}$ equipped with the topology whose open sets are:
    \begin{enumerate}
        \item all open subsets $U \subseteq X$, and
        \item all sets of the form $(X \setminus K) \cup \{\infty\}$ where $K \subseteq X$ is compact.
    \end{enumerate}
    With this topology, $X^\infty$ is a compact Hausdorff space, and the inclusion $X \hookrightarrow X^\infty$ is a homeomorphism onto a dense open subspace.

    If $X$ is already compact, then $X^\infty$ is the disjoint union of $X$ and an isolated point $\{\infty\}$.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[CM Field] \label{definition:cm_field}
Let $K$ be a \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:number_field}{number field}.
The field $K$ is called a \hldef{CM field} if it is a \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:totally_real_and_totally_imaginary_number_fields}{totally imaginary} \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:finite_field_extension}{quadratic extension} of a \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:totally_real_and_totally_imaginary_number_fields}{totally real} \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:extension_of_a_field}{subfield} $F$. That is, there exists a totally real number field number field $F \subset K$ such that $[K : F] = 2$ and $K$ is totally imaginary.

Every CM field has a canonical involution called the \hldef{complex conjugation}, which is the unique nontrivial automorphism of $K/F$ when $F$ is the maximal totally real subfield of $K$.
\end{definition}

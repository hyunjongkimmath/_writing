    Let $I \subseteq \mathbb{R}$ be an \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:interval_in_the_real_line}{interval}, let $c \in I$, and let $f : I \to \mathbb{R}$ be a function. Let $k \geq 0$ be an integer such that $f$ is \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:derivative_of_a_real_valued_function_on_a_real_interval}{$k$-times differentiable at $c$}.

    The \hldef{Taylor polynomial of order $k$ for $f$ at $c$} is the polynomial
    $$P_{k,f,c}(x) := \sum_{j=0}^k \frac{f^{(j)}(c)}{j!} (x - c)^j$$
    where $f^{(j)}(c)$ denotes the \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:derivative_of_a_real_valued_function_on_a_real_interval}{$j$-th derivative of $f$ at $c$}, and $j!$ is the \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:factorial_of_a_nonnegative_integer}{factorial of $j$}.

    When $c = 0$, the Taylor polynomial of order $k$ for $f$ at $0$ is called the \hldef{Maclaurin polynomial of order $k$ for $f$}, denoted by \hl{$M_{k,f}(x)$}:
    $$M_{k,f}(x) := \sum_{j=0}^k \frac{f^{(j)}(0)}{j!} x^j.$$
\end{definition}

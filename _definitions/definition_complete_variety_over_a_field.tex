\begin{definition} \label{definition:complete_variety_over_a_field}
Let $k$ be a field. A variety $X$ over $k$ is said to be a \hldef{complete variety over $k$} if the structure morphism $f: X \to \operatorname{Spec}(k)$ is a proper morphism of schemes (\CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:proper_morphism_of_schemes}{proper morphism}).

Equivalently, a variety $X$ over $k$ is complete if for every scheme $Y$ over $k$, the projection morphism $X \times_k Y \to Y$ is a closed map of topological spaces. This is known as the "valuative criterion for completeness."

Every projective variety over $k$ is complete, but not every complete variety is projective. However, by Chow's Lemma, every complete variety over an algebraically closed field admits a projective birational morphism from a projective variety.
\end{definition}

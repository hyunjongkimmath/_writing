\begin{definition} \label{definition:subobject_of_an_object_of_a_category}
Let $\mathcal{C}$ be a \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:category}{category}. Let $X \in \Ob(\calC)$ be an object. 
A \hldef{subobject of $X$} refers to a \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:monomorphism_and_epimorphism_in_categories}{monomorphism} $i: Y \hookrightarrow X$ in $\mathcal{C}$. We regard two subobjects $(Y,i)$ and $(Y',i')$ of $X$ as isomorphic if there exists an isomorphism $f: Y \to Y'$ such that $i = i' \circ f$. One often leaves the monomorphism $i$ implicit, suprressing it from the notation. One also often write \hl{$Y \subseteq X$} to denote that $Y$ is a subobject of $X$.
\end{definition}

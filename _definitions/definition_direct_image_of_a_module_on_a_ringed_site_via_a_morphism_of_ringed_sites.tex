\begin{definition} \label{definition:direct_image_of_a_module_on_a_ringed_site_via_a_morphism_of_ringed_sites}
    Let $f: (\mathcal{C}, J, \mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{C}}) \to (\mathcal{D}, K), \mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{D}})$ be a \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:morphism_of_ringed_sites}{morphism of ringed sites} with \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:locally_small_category}{small} \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:grothendieck_topology_on_a_category_site_covering_sieve_topologically_generating_family}{topological generating families}.

    Given a (right) \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:module_over_a_sheaf_of_rings_on_a_site}{$\calO_\calC$-module} $\calF$, its \CrefAndHyperref{definition:direct_image_of_a_sheaf_of_abelian_groups_on_a_site_under_a_continuous_functor_of_sites_or_a_site_morphism}{direct image} $f_* \calG$ has the natural structure of a $f_* \calO_\calC$-module. Since there is a natural map 
    $$f^\#: \calO_D \to f_* \calO_\calC$$
    of sheaves of rings on $(\calD, K)$ coming from the morphism of ringed sites, $f^* \calG$ can be regarded as a $\calO_\calD$-module. This $\calO_\calD$-module is also called the \hldef{direct image/pushforward of $\calG$ under $f$} and is still denoted by \hl{$f_* \calG$}.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition} \label{definition:prime_of_a_dedekind_domain}
    Let $R$ be a \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:dedekind_domain}{Dedekind domain}. A \hldef{prime of $R$} is a nonzero \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:prime_and_maximal_ideal_of_a_ring}{prime ideal} $\mathfrak{p}$ of $R$.

    Since every nonzero prime ideal of a Dedekind domain is \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:prime_and_maximal_ideal_of_a_ring}{maximal}, a prime of $R$ is equivalently a nonzero maximal ideal of $R$. For each such $\mathfrak{p}$, the localization $R_{\mathfrak{p}}$ is a \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:discrete_valuation_ring}{discrete valuation ring}, and the corresponding discrete valuation on the \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:field_of_fractions_of_an_integral_domain}{fraction field} of $R$ is called the $\mathfrak{p}$-adic valuation.

    In particular, a \hldef{prime of a global field $K$} a refers to a prime, in the above sense, of the \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:ring_of_integers_of_a_global_or_local_ring}{ring of integers} $\mathcal{O}_K$. 
\end{definition}

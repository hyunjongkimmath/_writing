\begin{definition} \label{definition:tree_forest_in_graph_theory}

Let $G$ be a \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:simple_graph_directed_graph_multigraph_weighted_graph}{simple graph}.

A graph $G$ is called a \hldef{tree} if it is \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:connected_graph_connectivity_of_a_graph}{connected} and contains no \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:walk_path_trail_cycle_in_a_graph}{cycle}. A vertex of degree 1 in a tree is called a \hldef{leaf of the tree}.

A graph $G$ is called a \hldef{forest} if it contains no cycle. Equivalently, a forest is a disjoint union of trees. Each connected component of a forest is a tree.
\end{definition}

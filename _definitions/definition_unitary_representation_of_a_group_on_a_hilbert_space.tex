\begin{definition} \label{definition:unitary_representation_of_a_group_on_a_hilbert_space}

    Let $(G, \cdot)$ be a \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:group}{group} and $\mathcal{H}$ a \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:hilbert_space}{Hilbert space} over $\mathbb{C}$. Let $\mathrm{U}(\mathcal{H})$ denote the group of unitary operators on $\mathcal{H}$.

    A \hldef{unitary representation of $G$ on $\mathcal{H}$} is a \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:group_homomorphism}{group homomorphism}
    $$\pi : G \to \mathrm{U}(\mathcal{H}).$$
    The pair $(\pi, \mathcal{H})$ is often referred to as a \hldef{unitary representation of $G$}.

    If $G$ is a \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:topological_group}{topological group} and $\mathcal{H}$ is a Hilbert space, a unitary representation $\pi : G \to \mathrm{U}(\mathcal{H})$ is called \hldef{strongly continuous (for $G$ on $\mathcal{H}$)} if for every $v \in \mathcal{H}$, the map
    $$G \to \mathcal{H}, \quad g \mapsto \pi(g)v$$
    is continuous with respect to the norm topology on $\mathcal{H}$.
\end{definition}

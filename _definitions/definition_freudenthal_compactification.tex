\begin{definition}[Freudenthal compactification of a topological space] \label{definition:freudenthal_compactification}

    Let $X$ be a connected, locally compact, Hausdorff space. The \hldef{Freudenthal compactification} (also called the \hldef{end compactification by components}) of $X$ is constructed as follows. For each compact subset $K \subseteq X$, let $\mathcal{C}_\infty(K)$ denote the set of non-compact connected components of $X \setminus K$. When $K' \supseteq K$ is another compact subset, inclusion induces a surjective map $\mathcal{C}_\infty(K') \to \mathcal{C}_\infty(K)$ by sending a component of $X \setminus K'$ to the unique component of $X \setminus K$ containing it.

    The \hldef{space of ends} of $X$ is the inverse limit
    $$\mathcal{E}(X) = \varprojlim_{K} \mathcal{C}_\infty(K),$$
    taken over all compact subsets ordered by inclusion. Each end $e \in \mathcal{E}(X)$ can be viewed as a nested sequence $(C_K)_{K}$ of non-compact components, one for each compact set.

    The \hldef{Freudenthal compactification} of $X$ is the disjoint union
    $$\hat{X} = X \sqcup \mathcal{E}(X),$$
    equipped with the topology generated by:
    \begin{enumerate}
        \item open subsets of $X$, and
        \item for each compact $K \subseteq X$ and each end $e = (C_K)_K \in \mathcal{E}(X)$, the sets $C_K \cup \{e' \in \mathcal{E}(X) : C'_K \subseteq C_K\}$ where $C'_K$ is the component of $e'$ at level $K$.
    \end{enumerate}

    With this topology, $\hat{X}$ is compact and Hausdorff. The space $X$ embeds as an open dense subspace, and $\mathcal{E}(X) = \hat{X} \setminus X$ is a closed subset. When $X$ has finitely many ends, the Freudenthal compactification coincides with the one-point or end compactification described above.
\end{definition}

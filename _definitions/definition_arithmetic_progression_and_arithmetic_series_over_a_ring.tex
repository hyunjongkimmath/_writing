\begin{definition}[Arithmetic progression and arithmetic series over a ring] \label{definition:arithmetic_progression_and_arithmetic_series_over_a_ring}

    Let $R$ be a \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:commutative_ring}{commutative ring}. Let $a \in R$ and $d \in R$. For each integer $n \geq 1$, let
    $$a_n = a + (n-1)d,$$
    where $(n-1)d$ denotes the element $d$ added to itself $n-1$ times in the additive group of $R$. The sequence $(a_n)_{n=1}^\infty$ is called an \hldef{arithmetic progression over $R$} with \hldef{first term} $a$ and \hldef{common difference} $d$.

    For each integer $N \geq 1$, the \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:finite_series_of_a_sequence_in_an_abelian_semigroup}{finite series} $\sum_{n=1}^N a_n = \sum_{n=1}^N (a + (n-1)d)$ is called an \hldef{arithmetic series of length $N$ over $R$}.

    If $R$ is a \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:topological_ring}{topological ring}, then the \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:infinite_series_and_convergence_of_infinite_series_in_a_topological_abelian_semigroup}{infinite series} $\sum_{n=1}^\infty (a + (n-1)d)$ is an \hldef{infinite arithmetic series in $R$}, understood as the limit of its partial sums in the topology of $R$\CrefIfExists{definition:infinite_series_and_convergence_of_infinite_series_in_a_topological_abelian_semigroup}.
\end{definition}

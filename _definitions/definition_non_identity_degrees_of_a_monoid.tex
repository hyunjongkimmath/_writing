\begin{definition} \label{definition:non_identity_degrees_of_a_monoid}

    Let $\Gamma$ be a \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:monoid}{monoid} with \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:left_right_identity_element_of_a_binary_operation_on_a_set}{identity} $e$.
    The \hldef{set of non-identity (or positive) degrees of $\Gamma$} is the complement of the identity:
    $$\hlin{\Gamma_+ = \Gamma \setminus \{e\}.}$$
    In a \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:graded_ring_by_a_monoid}{$\Gamma$-graded ring} $R = \bigoplus_{\gamma \in \Gamma} R_\gamma$, the direct sum of the homogeneous components indexed by $\Gamma_+$ is the \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:irrelevant_ideal_of_a_graded_ring_by_a_monoid_with_an_absorbing_zero_or_ordered_monoid_with_zero_degre_the_identity}{irrelevant ideal} $R_+ = \bigoplus_{\gamma \neq e} R_\gamma$.

    The set $\Gamma_+$ is not a \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:monoid}{submonoid} of $\Gamma$ (it does not contain the identity $e$), but it is closed under the monoid operation precisely when $e$ is not in the product of any two non-identity elements — i.e., when $\Gamma$ has no non-trivial left- or right-divisors of the identity.
\end{definition}

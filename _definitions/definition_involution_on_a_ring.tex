\begin{definition} \label{definition:involution_on_a_ring}
Let $R$ be a unital associative \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:ring}{ring}. 
An \hldef{involution on $R$} is a anti-ring homomorphism of order $2$, i.e. a map $\sigma: R \to R$ satisfying the following conditions for all $a, b \in R$:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item $\sigma(a+b) = \sigma(a) + \sigma(b)$;
    \item $\sigma(ab) = \sigma(b)\sigma(a)$ (anti-homomorphism property);
    \item $\sigma(1) = 1$;
    \item $\sigma(\sigma(a)) = a$.
\end{enumerate}
In many contexts, an involution may simply be notated by \hl{$\cdot \mapsto \overline{\cdot}$}
A \hldef{ring with involution} is a ring eqiupped with an involution.

Equivalently, a ring with involution is a \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:dagger_object_in_a_category}{dagger object} in the category of rings.
\end{definition}

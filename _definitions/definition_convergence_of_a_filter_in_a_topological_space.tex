\begin{definition}[Convergence of a filter] \label{definition:convergence_of_a_filter_in_a_topological_space}

    Let $X$ be a topological space and let $\mathcal{F}$ be a filter on the underlying set of $X$. We say that the filter $\mathcal{F}$ \hldef{converges} to a point $x \in X$ (written $\mathcal{F} \to x$) if every open neighborhood of $x$ belongs to $\mathcal{F}$, i.e.,
    $$\mathcal{N}(x) \subseteq \mathcal{F},$$
    where $\mathcal{N}(x)$ denotes the collection of all open neighborhoods of $x$.

    The point $x$ is called a \hldef{limit} of the filter $\mathcal{F}$. As with nets, in a Hausdorff space every convergent filter has at most one limit.

    Filters and nets provide equivalent descriptions of convergence:
    \begin{enumerate}
        \item A net $(x_\alpha)_{\alpha \in D}$ converges to $x$ if and only if the filter generated by its tails $\{\, x_\gamma \mid \gamma \geq \alpha \,\}_{\alpha \in D}$ converges to $x$.
        \item A filter $\mathcal{F}$ converges to $x$ if and only if it is finer than the neighborhood filter $\mathcal{N}(x)$.
    \end{enumerate}
\end{definition}

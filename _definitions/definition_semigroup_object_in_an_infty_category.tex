    Let $\mathcal{C}$ be an \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:infty_category_quasi_category}{$\infty$-category} admitting \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:product_of_finitely_many_objects_in_a_category}{finite products}, viewed as a cartesian monoidal $\infty$-category.

    A \hldef{semigroup object in $\mathcal{C}$} can be defined in two equivalent ways:
    
    \begin{enumerate}
        \item \textbf{(Via Segal Conditions)} A functor $X : \mathrm{N}(\Delta_{\mathrm{semi}}^{\mathrm{op}}) \to \mathcal{C}$ from the \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:nerve_of_a_category}{nerve} of the opposite of the semi-simplex category $\Delta_{\mathrm{semi}}$ (the category of non-empty finite linear orders and injective order-preserving maps) such that for every integer $n \ge 2$, the \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:category_object_in_an_infty_category}{Segal map}
        $$X_n \to \prod_{i=0}^{n-1} X_1 = \underbrace{X_1 \times X_1 \times \cdots \times X_1}_{n \text{ factors}}$$
        is an equivalence in $\mathcal{C}$. Here, the map is induced by the $n$ inert morphisms $\rho_i : [1] \to [n]$ in $\Delta_{\mathrm{semi}}$ given by $\rho_i(0) = i$ and $\rho_i(1) = i+1$. The underlying object of the semigroup is identified as $A := X_1$.

        \item \textbf{(Via $\infty$-Operads)} An algebra object in $\mathcal{C}$ over the non-unital associative $\infty$-operad $\mathcal{A}ss^{\mathrm{nu}}$. That is, an object of the $\infty$-category $\mathrm{Alg}_{\mathcal{A}ss^{\mathrm{nu}}}(\mathcal{C})$.
        \TODO{algebra object, non-unital associative $\infty$-operad}
    \end{enumerate}

    These two characterizations are equivalent via the \hldef{monoidal Rezk completion}. Specifically, the nerve $\mathrm{N}(\Delta_{\mathrm{semi}}^{\mathrm{op}})$ serves as a simplicial model for the non-unital associative operad, and the $\infty$-category of functors satisfying the Segal condition in (1) is equivalent to $\mathrm{Alg}_{\mathcal{A}ss^{\mathrm{nu}}}(\mathcal{C})$ under the cartesian symmetric monoidal structure.

    A semigroup object is called \hldef{commutative} (or \hldef{abelian}) if it satisfies either of the following equivalent conditions:
    \begin{itemize}
        \item Under definition (1), the functor $X$ extends from $\mathrm{N}(\Delta_{\mathrm{semi}}^{\mathrm{op}})$ to the nerve of the opposite of the category of finite non-empty sets and surjective maps.
        \item Under definition (2), the algebra object extends to an algebra over the non-unital commutative $\infty$-operad $\mathcal{C}om^{\mathrm{nu}}$.
    \end{itemize}
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Canonical principal polarization of a Jacobian] \label{definition:canonical_principal_polarization_of_a_jacobian}
    Let $C$ be a smooth, projective, connected curve over a field $k$. The \hldef{Jacobian} of $C$, denoted by $\Jac(C)$ or $\Pic^0_{C/k}$, is an abelian variety of dimension $g = g(C)$. There exists a canonical polarization
    $$\hlin{\theta_C\colon \Jac(C) \to \Jac(C)^*}$$
    induced by the theta divisor on $\Jac(C)$, constructed as follows. Fix a base point $p_0$ on $C$ and consider the Abel-Jacobi embedding $i\colon C \hookrightarrow \Jac(C)$ given by $x \mapsto [x - p_0]$. The image of $C^{\times g}$ under the addition map $\Jac(C)^{\times g} \to \Jac(C)$ defines a divisor class, whose associated line bundle yields the polarization $\theta_C$. This polarization is principal, making $(\Jac(C), \theta_C)$ a \hldef{principally polarized abelian variety}.
\end{definition}

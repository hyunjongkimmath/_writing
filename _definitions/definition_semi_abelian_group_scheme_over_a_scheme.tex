\begin{definition}[Semi-abelian group scheme over a scheme] \label{definition:semi_abelian_group_scheme_over_a_scheme}
    Let $S$ be a \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:scheme}{scheme}. A \hldef{semi-abelian (group) scheme over $S$} is an \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:algebraic_group_scheme_over_a_scheme}{algebraic group scheme} $\pi\colon G \to S$ that fits into an extension of an abelian scheme by an algebraic torus, i.e., there exists a short exact sequence of algebraic groups over $S$:
    $$\hlin{1 \to T \to G \to A \to 0,}$$
    where $\pi_T\colon T \to S$ is an \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:algebraic_torus_over_a_scheme}{algebraic torus over $S$}, $\pi_A\colon A \to S$ is an \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:abelian_scheme_over_a_scheme}{abelian scheme over $S$}, and the maps are homomorphisms of algebraic group schemes over $S$. If $T$ has rank $r$, we say that $G$ has \hldef{torus rank $r$}. When $r = 0$, the semi-abelian group scheme is simply an abelian scheme.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[category of finite sets] \label{definition:category_of_finite_sets}
    The \hldef{category of finite sets}, denoted by notations such as \hl{$\mathrm{FinSet}$}, \hl{$\mathbf{FinSet}$}, \hl{$(\mathrm{FinSet})$}, or \hl{$(\mathbf{FinSet})$}, is the category whose objects are \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:zermelo_fraenkel_set_theory}{finite sets} (i.e., \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:zermelo_fraenkel_set_theory}{sets} $S$ for which there exists a bijection with $\{1, 2, \dots, n\}$ for some integer $n \geq 0$, where the empty set corresponds to $n = 0$) and whose morphisms $X \to Y$ are \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:function_of_sets}{set functions} between them.

    The category of finite sets is a \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:locally_small_category}{locally small} \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:full_subcategory_of_a_category}{full subcategory} of the \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:category_of_sets}{category of sets}.

\end{definition}

\begin{definition} \label{definition:category_of_n_tuples_of_topological_spaces}
The \hldef{category of $n$-tuples of topological spaces}, often denoted by notations such as \hl{$\mathbf{Top}^n$} (not to be confused with the $n$-time self \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:product_category_of_a_family_of_categories}{product} of the category \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:category_of_topological_spaces}{$\mathbf{Top}$} of topological spaces), is the \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:category}{category} defined by:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Objects:} \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:n_tuple_of_topological_spaces}{$n$-tuples} $(X_1, \dots, X_n)$ of topological spaces.
    \item \textbf{Morphisms:} \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:morphism_of_n_tuples_of_topological_spaces}{Morphisms of $n$-tuples}.
    \item \textbf{Composition:} The composition of morphisms is the usual composition of continuous maps.
    \item \textbf{Identity:} For each object $(X_1, \dots, X_n)$, the identity morphism is the identity map $\operatorname{id}_{X_1}$.
\end{itemize}
It is a \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:locally_small_category}{locally small category}.
\end{definition}

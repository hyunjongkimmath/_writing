\begin{definition}[Disjoint union of schemes] \label{definition:disjoint_union_of_schemes}

    Let $\{X_i\}_{i \in I}$ be an arbitrary small collection of \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:scheme}{schemes}. The \hldef{disjoint union of schemes} (or \hldef{coproduct of schemes}), denoted by \hl{$\coprod_{i \in I} X_i$}, is the \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:scheme}{scheme} representing the \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:product_and_coproduct_of_objects_in_a_category}{coproduct} in the \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:category_of_schemes}{category of schemes}.

    Concretely, as a \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:topological_space}{topological space}, $\coprod_{i \in I} X_i$ is the \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:disjoint_union_topology_of_topological_spaces}{disjoint union} of the underlying topological spaces of the $X_i$. The \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:structure_sheaf_of_a_scheme}{structure sheaf} $\mathcal{O}_{\coprod X_i}$ is the unique sheaf of rings such that for each $i \in I$, the restriction $\mathcal{O}_{\coprod X_i}|_{X_i}$ is \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:isomorphism_in_a_category}{isomorphic} to $\mathcal{O}_{X_i}$, and the canonical injections $\iota_j : X_j \hookrightarrow \coprod_{i \in I} X_i$ are \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:morphism_of_schemes}{morphisms of schemes}.

    Equivalently, for any \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:scheme}{scheme} $Y$, giving a \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:morphism_of_schemes}{morphism of schemes} $\coprod_{i \in I} X_i \to Y$ is equivalent to giving a family of morphisms $\{X_i \to Y\}_{i \in I}$.
\end{definition}

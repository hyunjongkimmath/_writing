\begin{definition}[Semisimple endomorphism of a vector space over a field] \label{definition:semisimple_endomorphism_of_a_vector_space_over_a_field}

Let $k$ be a \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:field}{field}, let $V$ be a finite-dimensional $k$-vector space, and let $\phi : V \to V$ be an endomorphism.

The endomorphism $\phi$ is called \hldef{semisimple (endomorphism of a vector space over a field)} if every $\phi$-invariant subspace $W \subseteq V$ admits a $\phi$-invariant complement — that is, there exists a subspace $W' \subseteq V$ such that $V = W \oplus W'$ and $\phi(W') \subseteq W'$.

Equivalently, viewing $V$ as a $k[t]$-module via the action $t \cdot v := \phi(v)$, the endomorphism $\phi$ is semisimple if and only if this $k[t]$-module is \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:simple_irreducible_semisimple_module_over_a_ring}{semisimple}.

Equivalently, the minimal polynomial of $\phi$ is separable (i.e., square-free). Over an algebraically closed field, this is equivalent to $\phi$ being \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:diagonalizable_endomorphism_of_a_vector_space_over_a_field}{diagonalizable}.
\end{definition}

    Let $(R, \lambda)$ be a \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:complete_ring_with_respect_to_an_ideal}{complete} \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:discrete_valuation_ring}{DVR} such that $R/\lambda^n$ is finite for all $n \geq 1$. Let $E$ be the \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:field_of_fractions_of_an_integral_domain}{fraction field} of $R$, and let $K/E$ be a \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:algebraic_element_minimal_polynomial_of_an_algebraic_field_extension_transcendental_element_field_extension}{algebraic extension}. Let $X$ be a \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:locally_noetherian_and_noetherian_scheme}{noetherian scheme}, and consider sheaves on the \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:small_etale_site_of_a_scheme}{(small) \'etale site} of $X$.

    Let $\mathcal{K} \in \Dbc(X, K)$ be a \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:derived_category_of_cohomologically_bounded_constructible_sheaves_with_rational_adic_coefficients_on_a_noetherian_scheme}{bounded constructible complex with algebraic coefficients}. Recall that \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:derived_category_of_cohomologically_bounded_constructible_sheaves_with_rational_adic_coefficients_on_a_noetherian_scheme}{$\Dbc(X, K)$ is the 2-direct limit over finite subextensions $L/K$}, so $\mathcal{K}$ is represented by some object $M \in \Dbc(X, L)$ for a finite extension $L/E$. Let $\mathscr{H}^i(M)$ be the $i$-th \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:cohomology_sheaves_of_a_constructible_complex_with_rational_coefficients_on_a_noetherian_scheme}{cohomology sheaf} of $M$ with rational coefficients. The \hldef{$i$-th cohomology sheaf of $\mathcal{K}$} is the \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:category_of_etale_sheaves_with_rational_coefficients_in_an_algebraic_extension_of_the_fraction_field_of_a_complete_dvr}{algebraic coefficient sheaf}
    $$\hlin{\mathscr{H}^i(\mathcal{K}) := \mathscr{H}^i(M) \otimes_L K}$$
    obtained by extending scalars from $L$ to $K$. Each $\mathscr{H}^i(\mathcal{K})$ is a constructible sheaf with algebraic coefficients, and vanishes for all but finitely many $i$.

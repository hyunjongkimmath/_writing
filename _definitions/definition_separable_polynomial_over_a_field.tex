\begin{definition}[Separable polynomial over a field] \label{definition:separable_polynomial_over_a_field}

Let $k$ be a \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:field}{field} and let $\overline{k}$ be an \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:algebraic_element_minimal_polynomial_of_an_algebraic_element_algebraic_field_extension_transcendental_element_field_extension}{algebraic closure} of $k$. A non-constant polynomial $f \in k[x]$ is called a \hldef{separable polynomial over $k$} if all of its roots in $\overline{k}$ are simple (i.e., each root has multiplicity~1). Equivalently, writing the factorization in $\overline{k}[x]$ as
$$f(x) = c \prod_{i=1}^r (x - \alpha_i)^{e_i},$$
with $c \in k^\times$, distinct $\alpha_i \in \overline{k}$, and $e_i \ge 1$, the polynomial $f$ is separable if and only if $e_i = 1$ for all $i$.

Equivalently, $f$ is separable if and only if $\gcd(f, f') = 1$ in $k[x]$, where $f'$ denotes the formal derivative of $f$.
\end{definition}

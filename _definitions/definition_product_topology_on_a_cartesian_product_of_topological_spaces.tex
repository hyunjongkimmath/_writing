\begin{definition} \label{definition:product_topology_on_a_cartesian_product_of_topological_spaces}
Let $I$ be an index set and $\{X_i\}_{i \in I}$ be a collection of \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:topological_spaces}{topological spaces}. Let $X = \prod_{i \in I} X_i$ be the \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:product_of_sets}{set theoretic Cartesian product} of these spaces. 

    The \hldef{product topology} (or \hldef{Tychonoff topology)} on $X$ is the \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:basis_for_a_topology}{topology generated by} the \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:basis_for_a_topology}{base} consisting of all sets of the form $\prod_{i \in I} U_i$, where each $U_i$ is an \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:topological_space}{open subset} of $X_i$ and $U_i = X_i$ for all but finitely many indices $i \in I$.

    The \hldef{product} or the \hldef{product space of the $X_i$} refers to $X$ equipped with the product topology. 

    For each $j \in I$, let $p_j: \prod_{i \in I} X_i \to X_j$ denote the canonical projection map. The product topology is the \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:coarser_smaller_finer_larger_for_topologies_on_a_set}{coarsest topology} on $X$ such that all projection maps $p_j$ are \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:continuous_map_of_topological_spaces}{continuous}.
\end{definition}

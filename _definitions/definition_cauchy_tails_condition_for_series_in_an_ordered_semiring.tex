    Let $T$ be an \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:ordered_semiring}{ordered semiring}. A sequence $(M_n)_{n=1}^\infty$ in $T_{\ge 0}$ is said to satisfy the \hldef{Cauchy tails condition} if for every $\varepsilon \in T_{>0}$, there exists $N_0 \in \mathbb{N}$ such that for all $K > N \ge N_0$, one has
    $$\sum_{n=N+1}^K M_n < \varepsilon.$$

    This condition is the natural analogue of the Cauchy criterion for series, adapted to ordered semirings where subtraction may not be available. It does not require a metric structure on $T$ itself, only addition and order.

    When $T = \bbR_{\ge 0}$\CrefIfExists{definition:field_of_real_numbers}\CrefIfExists{definition:positive_cone_in_an_ordered_monoid}\CrefIfExists{proposition:positive_cone_of_ordered_ring_is_ordered_semiring}, the Cauchy tails condition is equivalent to the series $\sum_{n=1}^\infty M_n$ converging to a finite sum in $T$\CrefIfExists{proposition:cauchy_criterion_for_convergence_of_series_of_real_numbers}.

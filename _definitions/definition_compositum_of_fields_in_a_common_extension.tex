\begin{definition}[Compositum of fields in a common extension] \label{definition:compositum_of_fields_in_a_common_extension}
Let $L/K$ be a \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:extension_of_a_field}{field extension}.

\textbf{(Internal compositum.)} Let $M$ and $N$ be \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:extension_of_a_field}{subfields} of $L$ containing $K$. The \hldef{compositum of $M$ and $N$ inside $L$}, denoted by \hl{$MN$}, is the smallest subfield of $L$ that contains both $M$ and $N$. Equivalently,
$$MN = M(N) = N(M),$$
the field generated by $M \cup N$ over $K$.

\textbf{(External compositum.)} Let $E/F$ be a \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:extension_of_a_field}{field extension}, and let $\phi_1: K_1 \to E$ and $\phi_2: K_2 \to E$ be two \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:field_homomorphism}{field homomorphisms} such that $K_1/F$ and $K_2/F$ are field extensions (i.e., $\phi_1$ and $\phi_2$ agree on $F$). The \hldef{compositum of $\phi_1(K_1)$ and $\phi_2(K_2)$ inside $E$} is the subfield
$$\phi_1(K_1)\,\phi_2(K_2) \subseteq E,$$
\CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:field_generated_by_elements}{generated by} $\phi_1(K_1) \cup \phi_2(K_2)$ over $F$.

If no ambiguity can arise, one writes simply \hl{$K_1 K_2$} for the compositum.
\end{definition}

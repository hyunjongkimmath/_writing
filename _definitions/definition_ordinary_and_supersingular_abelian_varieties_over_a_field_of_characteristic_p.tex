\begin{definition}[Ordinary and supersingular abelian varieties over a field of characteristic p] \label{definition:ordinary_and_supersingular_abelian_varieties_over_a_field_of_characteristic_p}
    Let $A$ be an \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:abelian_variety_over_a_field}{abelian variety} of \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:dimension_of_a_scheme}{dimension} $g$ over a \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:field}{field} $k$ of \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:characteristic_of_a_ring}{characteristic} $p > 0$.
    The \hldef{$p$-torsion subgroup scheme} $A[p]$ is a \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:finite_morphism_of_schemes}{finite} \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:algebraic_group_scheme_over_a_scheme}{group scheme} over $k$ of rank $p^{2g}$.

    The abelian variety $A$ is said to be \hldef{ordinary} if $A[p](\bar{k}) \cong (\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z})^g$, i.e., the group of $\bar{k}$-rational points of order dividing $p$ has order $p^g$. Equivalently, $A$ is ordinary if and only if the $p$-rank of $A$ equals $g$.

    The abelian variety $A$ is said to be \hldef{supersingular} if $A[p](\bar{k}) = 0$, i.e., the group of $\bar{k}$-rational points of order dividing $p$ is trivial. More generally, $A$ is supersingular if all slopes of its Newton polygon equal $1/2$.
\end{definition}

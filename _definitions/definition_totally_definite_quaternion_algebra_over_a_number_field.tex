\begin{definition}[Totally definite quaternion algebra over a number field] \label{definition:totally_definite_quaternion_algebra_over_a_number_field}
    \TODO{real embedding}
    Let $F$ be a \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:number_field}{number field} and let $(a, b)_F$ be a \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:quaternion_algebra_over_a_field}{quaternion algebra over $F$}. We say that $(a, b)_F$ is \hldef{totally definite} if for every real embedding $\sigma: F \hookrightarrow \mathbb{R}$, the base-changed quaternion algebra $(\sigma(a), \sigma(b))_{\mathbb{R}}$ over $\mathbb{R}$ is a \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:division_ring}{division} \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:algebra_of_a_ring}{algebra}. Equivalently, $(a, b)_F$ is totally definite if for every real embedding $\sigma: F \hookrightarrow \mathbb{R}$, we have $\sigma(a) < 0$ and $\sigma(b) < 0$.

    If $F$ is not a \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:totally_real_and_totally_imaginary_number_fields}{totally real number field}, then the notion of total definiteness is vacuous since there are no real embeddings. Thus, one only considers totally definite quaternion algebras when $F$ is a totally real number field.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition} \label{definition:profinite_set}
A \hldef{profinite set} is a set which is the \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:projective_and_inductive_limits_in_categories}{projective limit} of a \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:system_in_a_category_indexed_by_a_directed_poset}{(small) directed system} of \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:countable_finite_uncountable_sets}{finite sets}. 

A profinite set $X = \varprojlim_i X_i$ has a natural topology, referred to as the \hldef{profinite topology} or \hldef{inverse limit topology}. It can be formulated in the following equivalent ways:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item As the \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:projective_and_inductive_limits_in_categories}{inverse limit} topology with respect to the family of projection maps $\pi_i : X \to X_i$ onto the finite sets $X_i$, each equipped with the \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:discrete_topological_space_of_a_set}{discrete topology}, in the system.

    \item As the \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:subspace_topology}{subspace topology} on $\varprojlim_i X_i$ inherited from the \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:product_topology}{product topology} on $\prod_i X_i$.

    \item As the topology generated by the \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:basis_for_a_topology}{basis} of "finite index" sets (specifically, the clopen sets) of the form $\pi_i^{-1}(V)$ for some index $i$ and some subset $V \subseteq X_i$.
\end{enumerate}
These topologies do not depend on the inverse system representing $X$. 
\end{definition}

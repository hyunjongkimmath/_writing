\begin{definition} \label{definition:invariant_subspace_of_a_linear_endomorphism}
    Let $F$ be a \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:field}{field}, let $V$ be an \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:vector_space_over_a_field}{$F$-vector space}, and let $T: V \to V$ be a linear endomorphism.

    A subspace $W \subseteq V$ is called \hldef{$T$-invariant} (or an \hldef{invariant subspace for $T$}) if $T(W) \subseteq W$.
\TextIfExists{definition:submodule_of_a_module_over_a_ring}{A $T$-invariant subspace is precisely a submodule of $V$ when $V$ is viewed as an $F[t]$-module via the action $t \cdot v = T(v)$. Thus invariant subspaces are the special case of \Cref{definition:submodule_of_a_module_over_a_ring} for the polynomial-ring module structure induced by $T$.}

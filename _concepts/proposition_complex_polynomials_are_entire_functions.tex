\begin{proposition}[Complex polynomials are entire] \label{proposition:complex_polynomials_are_entire_functions}

    Let $p : \mathbb{C} \to \mathbb{C}$ be a polynomial function, i.e.\ a function of the form
    $$p(z) = \sum_{k=0}^n a_k z^k$$
    with coefficients $a_k \in \mathbb{C}$. Then $p$ is \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:holomorphic_function_from_a_subset_of_Cn_to_Cm_and_derivative_of_a_holomorphic_function_at_a_point}{holomorphic} on all of $\mathbb{C}$, i.e.\ $p$ is an \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:entire_function_on_complex_space}{entire function}.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
    \TODO{AI generated, verify}
    We first show that the constant function $c(z) = c$ and the identity function $\operatorname{id}(z) = z$ are holomorphic on $\mathbb{C}$. For any $z \in \mathbb{C}$,
    $$\lim_{h \to 0} \frac{c(z+h) - c(z)}{h} = \lim_{h \to 0} \frac{c - c}{h} = 0,$$
    so $c$ is holomorphic with derivative $0$. For the identity,
    $$\lim_{h \to 0} \frac{(z+h) - z}{h} = \lim_{h \to 0} \frac{h}{h} = 1,$$
    so $\operatorname{id}$ is holomorphic with derivative $1$.

    Next we prove by induction that $z^n$ is holomorphic on $\mathbb{C}$ for every $n \in \mathbb{N}$. The base case $n = 0$ is the constant function $1$, which is holomorphic as shown above. Suppose that $z^{n-1}$ is holomorphic. Then $z^n = z \cdot z^{n-1}$ is a product of two holomorphic functions, hence holomorphic by the product rule in \Cref{proposition:proposition_sum_rule_product_rule_quotient_rule_chain_rule_for_complex_derivatives_of_holomorphic_functions_on_open_subsets_of_the_complex_plane}.

    A polynomial $p(z) = \sum_{k=0}^n a_k z^k$ is a finite sum of the holomorphic functions $a_k z^k$ (each $a_k z^k$ is a scalar multiple of $z^k$, and scalar multiples of holomorphic functions are holomorphic by the same argument as the constant multiple case, or by the product rule with a constant function). Therefore $p$ is holomorphic on $\mathbb{C}$ by the sum rule in \Cref{proposition:proposition_sum_rule_product_rule_quotient_rule_chain_rule_for_complex_derivatives_of_holomorphic_functions_on_open_subsets_of_the_complex_plane}.
\end{proof}

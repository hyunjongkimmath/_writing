\begin{lemma}
\label{lemma:equivalence_relations_on_a_set_correspond_to_partitions}
Let $X$ be a set.
\begin{enumerate}
    \item If $\sim$ is an \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:equivalence_relation_on_a_set}{equivalence relation} on $X$, then the \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:equivalence_class_of_an_element_of_a_set_under_an_equivalence_relation_and_quotient_of_set_by_equivalence_relation}{quotient set} $X / {\sim}$ is a \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:partition_of_a_set}{partition} of $X$.
    \item If $\mathcal{P}$ is a \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:partition_of_a_set}{partition} of $X$, then the relation $\sim_{\mathcal{P}}$ on $X$ defined by $x \sim_{\mathcal{P}} y \iff \exists A \in \mathcal{P}$ such that $x, y \in A$ is an \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:equivalence_relation_on_a_set}{equivalence relation} on $X$, and its quotient set $X / {\sim_{\mathcal{P}}}$ is canonically identified with $\mathcal{P}$.
\end{enumerate}
These constructions are mutually inverse: starting from an equivalence relation, forming its partition, and then forming the equivalence relation from that partition recovers the original equivalence relation; and starting from a partition, forming its equivalence relation, and then forming the partition from that equivalence relation recovers the original partition.
\end{lemma}

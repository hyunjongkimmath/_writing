\begin{proposition}[$\mathbb{C}$ is a Banach space over $\mathbb{C}$ with the standard norm valued in $\mathbb{R}$]
\label{proposition:C_is_a_Banach_space_over_C_with_the_standard_norm_valued_in_R}
The field of complex numbers $\mathbb{C}$$\CrefIfExists{definition:field_of_complex_numbers}$, equipped with the \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:complex_absolute_value}{standard absolute value} $|z| = \sqrt{x^2 + y^2}$ for $z = x + iy$, is a \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:banach_space_over_a_commutative_ring_with_an_absolute_value_valued_in_an_ordered_semiring}{Banach space over $\mathbb{C}$ valued in $\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}$}.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The absolute value $|\cdot| : \mathbb{C} \to \mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}$ satisfies the axioms of a norm on the one-dimensional $\mathbb{C}$-vector space $\mathbb{C}$: homogeneity $|\lambda z| = |\lambda||z|$ and the triangle inequality. The metric induced by this absolute value makes $\mathbb{C}$ complete because $\mathbb{R}$ is complete\CrefIfExists{definition:real_and_complex_fields_as_complete_fields}, which implies that every Cauchy sequence in $\mathbb{C}$ converges.
\end{proof}

    $$S_1(x) \leq S_2(x) \leq \cdots.$$
    Thus $(S_N)_{N=1}^\infty$ is a monotonically increasing sequence of nonnegative measurable functions.

    By definition, the infinite series $\sum_{k=1}^\infty g_k(x)$ equals $\lim_{N \to \infty} S_N(x)$. Applying the monotone convergence theorem (\CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{theorem:monotone_convergence_theorem_for_the_limit_of_lebesgue_integrals_of_an_increasing_sesquence_of_functions_and_the_lebesgue_integral_of_the_limit_of_the_functions}) to $(S_N)$:
    $$\int_X \sum_{k=1}^\infty g_k \, d\mu = \int_X \lim_{N \to \infty} S_N \, d\mu = \lim_{N \to \infty} \int_X S_N \, d\mu.$$

    By the linearity of the Lebesgue integral (\CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{theorem:linearity_of_lebesgue_integral}), for each $N$:
    $$\int_X S_N \, d\mu = \int_X \sum_{k=1}^N g_k \, d\mu = \sum_{k=1}^N \int_X g_k \, d\mu.$$

    Hence:
    $$\lim_{N \to \infty} \int_X S_N \, d\mu = \lim_{N \to \infty} \sum_{k=1}^N \int_X g_k \, d\mu = \sum_{k=1}^\infty \int_X g_k \, d\mu.$$

    Combining these equalities:
    $$\int_X \sum_{k=1}^\infty g_k \, d\mu = \sum_{k=1}^\infty \int_X g_k \, d\mu.$$
\end{proof}

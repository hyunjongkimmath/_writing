\begin{theorem}[Spectral Theorem for Normal Operators] \label{theorem:spectral_theorem_for_normal_operators_on_a_finite_dimensional_inner_product_space}
    Let $V$ be a finite-dimensional nonzero inner product space over $\mathbb{C}$, and let $T: V \to V$ be a linear endomorphism.

    The following are equivalent:
    \begin{enumerate}
        \item $T$ is \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:self_adjoint_hermitian_skew_symmetric_unitary_normal_operators}{normal}.
        \item There exists an \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:orthogonal_and_orthonormal_sets_in_an_inner_product_space}{orthonormal basis} of $V$ consisting entirely of \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:eigenvalue_and_eigenvector_of_an_endomorphism_of_a_vector_space_over_a_field}{eigenvectors} of $T$.
        \item The matrix of $T$ with respect to some orthonormal basis is diagonal.
    \end{enumerate}

    If moreover $V$ is a real inner product space and $T$ is \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:self_adjoint_hermitian_skew_symmetric_unitary_normal_operators}{self-adjoint}, then the equivalent conditions hold and all eigenvalues of $T$ are real.

The finite-dimensional hypothesis ensures the existence of eigenvalues.}

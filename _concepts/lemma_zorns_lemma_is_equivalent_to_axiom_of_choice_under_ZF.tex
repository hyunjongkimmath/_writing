        Let $\mathcal{F}$ be the set of all partial choice functions, i.e., all functions $g: Y \to \bigcup X$ where $Y \subseteq X$ and $g(A) \in A$ for all $A \in Y$. The set $\mathcal{F}$ is nonempty because the empty function (with domain $\varnothing$) is vacuously a partial choice function.

        Partially order $\mathcal{F}$ by extension: for $g_1, g_2 \in \mathcal{F}$, we declare $g_1 \leq g_2$ if $\operatorname{dom}(g_1) \subseteq \operatorname{dom}(g_2)$ and $g_2|_{\operatorname{dom}(g_1)} = g_1$ (i.e., $g_2$ extends $g_1$).

        By Zorn's Lemma, $\mathcal{F}$ has a maximal element $f_{\max}$. We show that $\operatorname{dom}(f_{\max}) = X$. Suppose for contradiction that there exists $A \in X \setminus \operatorname{dom}(f_{\max})$. Since $A$ is nonempty, choose any $a \in A$. Extend $f_{\max}$ to $f'$ by setting $\operatorname{dom}(f') = \operatorname{dom}(f_{\max}) \cup \{A\}$ and $f'(A) = a$, and $f'(B) = f_{\max}(B)$ for $B \in \operatorname{dom}(f_{\max})$.     Having proven both directions, we conclude that Zorn's Lemma and the Axiom of Choice are equivalent over ZF.

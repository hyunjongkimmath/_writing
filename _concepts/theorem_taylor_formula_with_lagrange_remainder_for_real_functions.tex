\begin{theorem}[Taylor's formula with Lagrange remainder] \label{theorem:taylor_formula_with_lagrange_remainder_for_real_functions}

    Let $I \subseteq \mathbb{R}$ be an open interval, let $a \in I$, and let $f : I \to \mathbb{R}$ be a function that has derivatives $f^{(k)}$ on $I$ for all $k = 0, 1, \dots, n+1$, where $n \geq 0$. Then for each $x \in I$, there exists a real number $\xi$ strictly between $a$ and $x$ such that
    $$f(x) = \sum_{k=0}^n \frac{f^{(k)}(a)}{k!}(x - a)^k + \frac{f^{(n+1)}(\xi)}{(n+1)!}(x - a)^{n+1},$$
    where $k!$ is the \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:factorial_of_a_nonnegative_integer}{factorial of $k$}. The remainder term

    $$R_n(x) := f(x) - \sum_{k=0}^n \frac{f^{(k)}(a)}{k!}(x - a)^k$$

    is called the \hldef{Lagrange remainder of order $n$ for $f$ at $a$}. Equivalently, there exists $\xi = a + \theta(x-a)$ for some $\theta \in (0, 1)$ such that

    $$R_n(x) = \frac{f^{(n+1)}(\xi)}{(n+1)!}(x - a)^{n+1}.$$
\end{theorem}

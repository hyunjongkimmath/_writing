\begin{proof}

    We prove the two statements separately.

    \begin{enumerate}
        \item Suppose that $\sum_{n=1}^\infty M_n$ converges to $S \in G$ in the order topology, i.e.\ the sequence $(S_N)$ of \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:partial_sum_of_a_sequence_in_an_abelian_group}{partial sums} \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:convergence_and_limit_of_sequence_in_a_topological_space}{converges to} $S$.

        Let $\varepsilon \in G_{>0}$. The \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:order_topology_on_a_partially_ordered_set}{lower ray} $U := (-\infty, \varepsilon) = \{x \in G : x < \varepsilon\}$ is open in the order topology and contains $0$, since $0 < \varepsilon$ as $\varepsilon$ is \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:ordered_abelian_group}{positive}. By the assumption that the order topology makes $G$ a \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:topological_group}{topological abelian group}, the subtraction map $\mu : G \times G \to G$, $\mu(x, y) = x - y$, is \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:continuous_map_between_open_subsets_of_euclidean_spaces}{continuous}; since $\mu(S, S) = 0 \in U$, there exist \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:neighborhood_and_neighborhood_basis_of_a_point_in_a_topological_space}{open neighborhoods} $A, B$ of $S$ such that $A - B := \{a - b : a \in A,\, b \in B\} \subseteq U$. By the convergence $S_N \to S$, there exists $N_0 \in \mathbb{N}$ such that for all $N \ge N_0$, $S_N \in A \cap B$. Then for any $K > N \ge N_0$, we have
        $$\sum_{n=N+1}^K M_n = S_K - S_N \in A - B \subseteq U,$$
        i.e.\ $\sum_{n=N+1}^K M_n < \varepsilon$. Thus $(M_n)$ satisfies the \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:cauchy_tails_condition_for_series_in_an_ordered_semiring}{Cauchy tails condition}.

        \item Conversely, suppose that $(M_n)$ satisfies the \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:cauchy_tails_condition_for_series_in_an_ordered_semiring}{Cauchy tails condition}. Then for every $\varepsilon \in G_{>0}$, there exists $N_0 \in \mathbb{N}$ such that for all $K > N \ge N_0$, we have $S_K - S_N < \varepsilon$. In particular, taking $N = N_0$, we see that $S_K < S_{N_0} + \varepsilon$ for all $K > N_0$. Since $(S_N)$ is \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:monotone_sequence_of_elements_in_a_partially_ordered_set}{nondecreasing} and $G$ is assumed to be such that every non-decreasing \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:upper_and_lower_bounds_of_subsets_of_an_ordered_set}{bounded-above} sequence \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:convergence_and_limit_of_sequence_in_a_topological_space}{converges} in the order topology, the sequence $(S_N)$ converges to some $S \in G$, i.e., the series $\sum_{n=1}^\infty M_n$ \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:infinite_series_and_convergence_of_infinite_series_in_a_topological_abelian_semigroup}{converges}.

        The Cauchy tails condition guarantees the sequence is \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:upper_and_lower_bounds_of_subsets_of_an_ordered_set}{bounded above}: fix any $\varepsilon \in G_{>0}$ and let $N_0$ be as in the condition for this $\varepsilon$; then $S_K \le S_{N_0} + \varepsilon$ for all $K > N_0$. Together with the \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:monotone_sequence_of_elements_in_a_partially_ordered_set}{monotonicity} of $(S_N)$, this implies convergence.

        The particular case of $\mathbb{R}$ follows because $\mathbb{R}$ has the property that every non-decreasing bounded-above sequence converges in the order topology (the \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:supremum_infimum_of_a_subset_of_an_ordered_set}{supremum} exists and is the limit). In $\mathbb{R}_{\ge 0}$, the Cauchy tails condition is therefore equivalent to \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:infinite_series_and_convergence_of_infinite_series_in_a_topological_abelian_semigroup}{convergence} of the series, which is the standard Cauchy criterion for non-negative real series.
    \end{enumerate}
\end{proof}

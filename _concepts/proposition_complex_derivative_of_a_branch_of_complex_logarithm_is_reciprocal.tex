Then $\operatorname{Log}$ is \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:holomorphic_function_from_a_subset_of_Cn_to_Cm_and_derivative_of_a_holomorphic_function_at_a_point}{holomorphic} on $U$, and its complex derivative satisfies

\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}

    Let $\operatorname{Log} : U \to \mathbb{C}$ be a branch of the complex logarithm. By definition (\CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:branch_of_the_complex_logarithm_on_an_open_subset}), $\operatorname{Log}$ is holomorphic on $U$ and satisfies
    $$\exp(\operatorname{Log}(z)) = z \quad \text{for all } z \in U.$$

    Differentiate both sides using the chain rule for complex derivatives (\CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{proposition:proposition_sum_rule_product_rule_quotient_rule_chain_rule_for_complex_derivatives_of_holomorphic_functions_on_open_subsets_of_the_complex_plane}). The left-hand side differentiates to
    $$\frac{d}{dz} \exp(\operatorname{Log}(z)) = \exp'(\operatorname{Log}(z)) \cdot \operatorname{Log}'(z).$$

    By the fact that the derivative of the complex exponential is itself (\CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{proposition:complex_exponential_function_is_entire_and_its_derivative_is_itself}), we have $\exp'(w) = \exp(w)$ for all $w \in \mathbb{C}$. Hence
    $$\frac{d}{dz} \exp(\operatorname{Log}(z)) = \exp(\operatorname{Log}(z)) \cdot \operatorname{Log}'(z).$$

    By the defining property of $\operatorname{Log}$, we have $\exp(\operatorname{Log}(z)) = z$. Therefore:
    $$\frac{d}{dz} \exp(\operatorname{Log}(z)) = z \cdot \operatorname{Log}'(z).$$

    The right-hand side of our original equation differentiates to $1$ (\CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{proposition:complex_derivative_of_n_to_the_s_for_fixed_positive_integer_n}). Equating both sides:
    $$z \cdot \operatorname{Log}'(z) = 1.$$

    Since $z \in U \subseteq \mathbb{C} \setminus \{0\}$, we have $z \neq 0$, so we may divide by $z$:
    $$\operatorname{Log}'(z) = \frac{1}{z}.$$
    This holds for all $z \in U$.
\end{proof}

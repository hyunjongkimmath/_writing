\begin{corollary}[Trivial zeros of Dirichlet L-functions] \label{corollary:trivial_zeros_of_dirichlet_L_function}

Let $\chi$ be a \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:dirichlet_character_modulo_a_nonnegative_integer}{Dirichlet character modulo $k$} with parity $a(\chi) \in \{0,1\}$ (where $a(\chi)=0$ if $\chi(-1)=1$ and $a(\chi)=1$ if $\chi(-1)=-1$). The \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:dirichlet_L_function_of_a_dirichlet_character_modulo_a_nonnegative_integer}{Dirichlet $L$-function} $L(s, \chi)$ has simple zeros at
$$
\begin{cases}
s = -2n & \text{for } n \in \bbN \text{ if } \chi \text{ is even } (a(\chi)=0), \\
s = -(2n+1) & \text{for } n \in \bbN_0 \text{ if } \chi \text{ is odd } (a(\chi)=1).
\end{cases}
$$
These are called the \hldef{trivial zeros} of $L(s, \chi)$.

They arise from the poles of the \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:gamma_function_on_the_complex_numbers}{gamma factor} $\Gamma\!\big(\frac{s + a(\chi)}{2}\big)$ in the functional equation of the completed $L$-function $\Lambda(s, \chi)$; the gamma function has simple poles at non-positive integers, which cancel the analytic continuation of $L(s, \chi)$ to produce zeros.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
    \TODO{AI generated, verify}
    By the \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{theorem:analytic_continuation_and_functional_equation_for_dirichlet_L_function}{functional equation for Dirichlet $L$-functions}, the completed $L$-function $\Lambda(s,\chi)$ satisfies
    $$\Lambda(s, \chi) = \varepsilon(\chi)\, \Lambda(1 - s, \overline{\chi}).$$
    The completed $L$--function is defined as\CrefIfExists{definition:completed_dirichlet_L_function_of_a_dirichlet_character_modulo_k}
    $$\Lambda(s,\chi) = \left(\frac{k}{\pi}\right)^{(s+a(\chi))/2} \Gamma\!\left(\frac{s + a(\chi)}{2}\right) L(s, \chi).$$

    The \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:gamma_function_on_the_complex_numbers}{Gamma function} $\Gamma(z)$ has simple poles at $z = 0, -1, -2, \ldots$. Hence $\Gamma\bigl(\frac{s + a(\chi)}{2}\bigr)$ has simple poles precisely when
    $$\frac{s + a(\chi)}{2} = -n \quad (n \in \bbN_0),$$
    i.e., at $s = -a(\chi) - 2n$.

    For even characters ($a(\chi) = 0$), the poles occur at $s = -2n$ for $n \in \bbN_0$. For odd characters ($a(\chi) = 1$), they occur at $s = -(2n+1)$ for $n \in \bbN_0$.

    Since $\Lambda(s,\chi)$ is meromorphic on $\bbC$ (entire for non-principal $\chi$, with at most a simple pole at $s=1$ for principal $\chi$), and the factor $(k/\pi)^{(s+a(\chi))/2}$ never vanishes, the poles of $\Gamma\bigl(\frac{s + a(\chi)}{2}\bigr)$ must be cancelled by zeros of $L(s,\chi)$. At each such point $s_0 = -a(\chi) - 2n$ with $n \geq 1$ (or $n \geq 0$ for odd characters), the Gamma factor has a simple pole, while $\Lambda(s,\chi)$ is holomorphic (for non-principal $\chi$) or at worst meromorphic. Therefore $L(s,\chi)$ must vanish at these points to cancel the pole.

    The order of vanishing is exactly one because both the pole of $\Gamma$ and the zero of $L$ are simple: the functional equation relates $\Lambda(s,\chi)$ to $\Lambda(1-s, \overline{\chi})$, and at the reflected point $1 - s_0 = 1 + a(\chi) + 2n > 1$, the value $\Lambda(1-s_0, \overline{\chi})$ is finite and nonzero (as it lies in the half-plane of absolute convergence of the Dirichlet series).
\end{proof}

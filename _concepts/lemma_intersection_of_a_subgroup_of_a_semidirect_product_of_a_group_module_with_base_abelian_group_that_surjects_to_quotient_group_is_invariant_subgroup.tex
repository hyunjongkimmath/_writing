\begin{lemma} \label{lemma:intersection_of_a_subgroup_of_a_semidirect_product_of_a_group_module_with_base_abelian_group_that_surjects_to_quotient_group_is_invariant_subgroup}
    Let $G$ be a \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:group}{group}, and let $A$ be an (left) $\bbZ[G]$\CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:module_of_a_ring}{-module}. Let $H \subseteq A \rtimes G$ be a \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:subgroup_of_a_group}{subgroup} whose \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:image_coimage_of_a_morphism_in_a_category}{image} under the canonical \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:quotient_of_a_group_by_a_normal_subgroup}{quotient} \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:group_homomorphism}{homomorphism} $A \rtimes G \to G$ is $G$. 

    The intersection $H \cap (A \rtimes \{1\})$, which is identified with a subgroup $A_0$ of $A$, is $G$-invariant under the $G$-module action, i.e. $g a_0  \in A_0$ for all $g \in G$ and $a_0 \in A_0$. 
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
    Let $(a,1) \in H \cap (A \rtimes \{1\})$. For any $g \in G$, there is some element of $H$ of the form $(b,g)$ for some $b \in A$. Compute
    $$(b,\gamma) \cdot (a,1) \cdot (b, \gamma)^{-1} = (\gamma a , 1).$$
    This conjugate is itself an element of $H$, so $\gamma a \in A_0$. 
\end{proof}

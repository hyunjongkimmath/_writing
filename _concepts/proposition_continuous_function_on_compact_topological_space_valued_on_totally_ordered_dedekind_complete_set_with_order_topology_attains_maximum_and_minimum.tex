\begin{proposition}[Continuous function on a compact space valued in a Dedekind-complete totally ordered set attains maximum and minimum] \label{proposition:continuous_function_on_compact_topological_space_valued_on_totally_ordered_dedekind_complete_set_with_order_topology_attains_maximum_and_minimum}
    Let $X$ be a nonempty \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:compact_topological_space}{compact topological space}, let $(P, \leq)$ be a \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:total_order_linear_order_on_a_set}{totally ordered set} that is \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:dedekind_completeness_of_an_ordered_set}{Dedekind-complete}, and equip $P$ with the \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:order_topology_on_a_partially_ordered_set}{order topology} (For example, the hypotheses on $P$ apply to $\bbR$\CrefIfExists{definition:field_of_real_numbers} with its \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:standard_order_on_real_numbers}{standard order}\CrefIfExists{proposition:the_field_of_real_numbers_has_the_least_upper_bound_property_and_hence_is_dedekind_complete}). Let $f : X \to P$ be a \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:continuous_map_of_topological_spaces}{continuous function}. Then there exist points $x_{\max}, x_{\min} \in X$ such that for all $x \in X$, we have
    $$f(x_{\min}) \leq f(x) \leq f(x_{\max}).$$
    In other words, $f$ attains its \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:greatest_element_maximum_element_in_a_subset_of_a_partially_ordered_set}{maximum} at $x_{\max}$ and its \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:least_element_minimum_element_in_a_subset_of_a_partially_ordered_set}{minimum} at $x_{\min}$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
    We prove that $f$ attains its maximum; the statement for the minimum follows by a symmetric argument applied to the reverse totally ordered set $(P, \geq)$, which is also Dedekind-complete\TODO{AI generated, reference needed: lemma that the reverse of a Dedekind-complete totally ordered set is Dedekind-complete} and carries the same order topology.

    We first show that $f$ is \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:bounded_above_below_and_bounded_function_on_a_set_to_an_ordered_set}{bounded above}. Let
    $$\mathcal{U} = \{\, (-\infty, b) : b \in P \,\},$$
    where $(-\infty, b) = \{y \in P : y < b\}$ denotes a \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:order_topology_on_a_partially_ordered_set}{lower ray} in the order topology on $P$. The collection $\mathcal{U}$ covers $P$, hence also covers the image $f(X) \subseteq P$. For each $b \in P$, the set $(-\infty, b)$ is open in the order topology, so its preimage $f^{-1}((-\infty, b))$ is open in $X$ because $f$ is a \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:continuous_map_of_topological_spaces}{continuous function}. The collection $\{\, f^{-1}((-\infty, b)) : b \in P \,\}$ therefore forms an open cover of $X$. By the \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:compact_topological_space}{compactness} of $X$, there exists a finite subcover; say
    $$X = f^{-1}((-\infty, b_1)) \cup \cdots \cup f^{-1}((-\infty, b_n))$$
    for some $b_1, \dots, b_n \in P$. Let $B = \max\{b_1, \dots, b_n\}$\CrefIfExists{definition:greatest_element_maximum_element_in_a_subset_of_a_partially_ordered_set}\CrefIfExists{lemma:any_finite_nonempty_subset_of_a_totally_ordered_set_has_a_maximum_and_minimum}. Since $b_i \leq B$ for each $i = 1, \dots, n$, we have $(-\infty, b_i) \subseteq (-\infty, B)$, and therefore
    $$X = f^{-1}((-\infty, B)).$$
    This implies that for all $x \in X$, we have $f(x) < B$, so $B$ is an upper bound of $f(X)$.

    We now show that the maximum is attained. Since $X$ is nonempty and $f(X)$ is bounded above, the \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:dedekind_completeness_of_an_ordered_set}{Dedekind completeness} of $P$ guarantees the existence of
    $$M = \sup f(X)$$
    \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:supremum_infimum_of_a_subset_of_an_ordered_set}{supremum}. We claim that $M \in f(X)$.

    Suppose for contradiction that $M \notin f(X)$. Then for each $y \in f(X)$, we have $y < M$ because $M$ is an \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:upper_and_lower_bounds_of_subsets_of_an_ordered_set}{upper bound} of $f(X)$ and $y \neq M$. For each $y \in f(X)$, the set $(-\infty, y)$ is open in the order topology on $P$, so its preimage $f^{-1}((-\infty, y))$ is open in $X$ by continuity of $f$. We show that $\{\, f^{-1}((-\infty, y)) : y \in f(X) \,\}$ covers $X$. To see this, let $x \in X$ and set $y_0 = f(x) \in f(X)$. Since $M = \sup f(X)$ is the least upper bound of $f(X)$ and $y_0 < M$, the element $y_0$ is not an upper bound of $f(X)$; hence there exists some $y' \in f(X)$ such that $y_0 < y'$. Then $x \in f^{-1}((-\infty, y'))$. By the compactness of $X$, there exists a finite subcover
    $$X = f^{-1}((-\infty, y_1)) \cup \cdots \cup f^{-1}((-\infty, y_m))$$
    for some $y_1, \dots, y_m \in f(X)$. Let $Y = \max\{y_1, \dots, y_m\}$, which exists by \Cref{lemma:any_finite_nonempty_subset_of_a_totally_ordered_set_has_a_maximum_and_minimum}. Then $(-\infty, y_i) \subseteq (-\infty, Y)$ for each $i$, and so
    $$X = f^{-1}((-\infty, Y)).$$
    This implies that for all $z \in f(X)$, we have $z < Y$. In particular, since $Y \in f(X)$, we obtain $Y < Y$, which contradicts the \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:irreflexive_binary_relation_on_a_set}{irreflexivity} of \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:strict_partially_ordered_set}{strict order}.

    Therefore, $M \in f(X)$, so there exists a point $x_{\max} \in X$ such that $f(x_{\max}) = M$. By definition of the supremum, for all $x \in X$, we have $f(x) \leq f(x_{\max})$, establishing that $f$ attains its maximum.
\end{proof}

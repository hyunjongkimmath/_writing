\begin{lemma} \label{lemma:kernel_cokernel_image_coimage_of_modules_over_rings_are_categorical}
    Let $R,S$ be \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:ring}{(not-necessarily commutative) rings with unity}, let $M,N$ be \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:module_of_a_ring}{$R$-$S$-bimodules}, and let $f: M \to N$ be a \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:homomorphism_of_modules_over_a_ring}{module homomorphism}.
    The \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:kernel_image_cokernel_of_a_module_homomorphism_over_rings}{kerel, cokernel, image, and coimage} of $f$ are respectively the categorical \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:kernel_and_cokernel_of_a_morphism_in_a_pointed_category}{kernel, cokernel}, \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:image_coimage_of_a_morphism_in_a_category}{image, and coimage}.
\end{lemma}

    The category of $R$-$S$-bimodules is an abelian category, hence in particular a \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:pointed_category}{pointed category} with the zero module as its \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:initial_final_zero_objects_of_a_category}{zero object}. We verify each of the four claims.

    \begin{enumerate}
        \item The kernel $\ker(f) = \{ m \in M : f(m) = 0 \}$ is a submodule of $M$ by \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:submodule_of_a_module_over_a_ring}{definition}. Let $i: \ker(f) \hookrightarrow M$ be the inclusion map. Then $f \circ i = 0$, the zero morphism $\ker(f) \to N$. Now let $g: Z \to M$ be any module homomorphism with $f \circ g = 0$. For every $z \in Z$, we have $f(g(z)) = 0$, so $g(z) \in \ker(f)$. Hence $g$ factors uniquely through $\ker(f)$ via the map $u: Z \to \ker(f)$ defined by $u(z) = g(z)$. Therefore, $(\ker(f), i)$ is the categorical \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:kernel_and_cokernel_of_a_morphism_in_a_pointed_category}{kernel} of $f$.

        \item The cokernel $\operatorname{coker}(f) = N / \operatorname{im}(f)$ is a quotient module by \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:quotient_module_of_a_module_by_a_module_of_a_ring}{definition}. Let $p: N \twoheadrightarrow N/\operatorname{im}(f)$ be the canonical projection. Then $p \circ f = 0$ because $f(m) \in \operatorname{im}(f)$ for all $m \in M$. Now let $g: N \to Z$ be any module homomorphism with $g \circ f = 0$. This means $\operatorname{im}(f) \subseteq \ker(g)$, so by the universal property of quotient modules, $g$ factors uniquely through $N/\operatorname{im}(f)$ via $v: N/\operatorname{im}(f) \to Z$ defined by $v(n + \operatorname{im}(f)) = g(n)$. Therefore, $(\operatorname{coker}(f), p)$ is the categorical \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:kernel_and_cokernel_of_a_morphism_in_a_pointed_category}{cokernel} of $f$.

        \item The image $\operatorname{im}(f) = f(M) \subseteq N$ is a submodule by definition. Let $m: \operatorname{im}(f) \hookrightarrow N$ be the inclusion (a monomorphism in the category of modules). Let $e: M \twoheadrightarrow \operatorname{im}(f)$ be defined by $e(m) = f(m)$ (an epimorphism because it is surjective onto $\operatorname{im}(f)$). Then $f = m \circ e$. For any other factorization $f = m' \circ e'$ with $m': I' \hookrightarrow N$ mono and $e': M \twoheadrightarrow I'$ epi, we have an induced isomorphism $\varphi: \operatorname{im}(f) \cong I'$ making the diagram commute. Therefore, $(\operatorname{im}(f), m)$ is the categorical \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:image_coimage_of_a_morphism_in_a_category}{image} of $f$.

        \item The coimage $\operatorname{coim}(f) = M/\ker(f)$ is a quotient module. Let $e: M \twoheadrightarrow M/\ker(f)$ be the canonical projection (an epimorphism). By the first isomorphism theorem for modules \Cref{theorem:first_isomorphism_theorem_for_modules_over_rings}, there exists an induced isomorphism $\bar{f}: M/\ker(f) \xrightarrow{\sim} \operatorname{im}(f)$ such that $\bar{f}(m + \ker(f)) = f(m)$. Let $m'$ be the composition of $\bar{f}$ with the inclusion $\operatorname{im}(f) \hookrightarrow N$. Then $m': M/\ker(f) \to N$ is a monomorphism (as a composition of an isomorphism and a monomorphism), and $f = m' \circ e$. This gives the categorical \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:image_coimage_of_a_morphism_in_a_category}{coimage} factorization.
    \end{enumerate}

    In all four cases, the module-theoretic constructions satisfy the universal properties of their categorical counterparts.
\end{proof}

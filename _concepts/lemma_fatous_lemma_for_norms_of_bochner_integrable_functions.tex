$$\int_X \liminf_{n \to \infty} \|f_n(x)\|\,d\mu(x) \leq \liminf_{n \to \infty} \int_X \|f_n(x)\|\,d\mu(x)$$
holds, where $\liminf$ is taken in the sense of \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:limsup_and_liminf_of_a_sequence_in_the_extended_real_line}{limit inferior of a sequence in the extended real line}, and both sides take values in $[0, \infty]$.

$$\int_X \liminf_{n \to \infty} \|f_n(x)\|\,d\mu(x) \leq \liminf_{n \to \infty} \int_X \|f_n(x)\|\,d\mu(x) < \infty.$$
\end{lemma}

Let $(X, \mathcal{A}, \mu)$ be a measure space and let $V$ be a normed vector space over $\mathbb{F} \in \{\mathbb{R}, \mathbb{C}\}$ with norm $\|\cdot\|$. Fix the sequence of Bochner measurable functions $(f_n)_{n \geq 1}$.

Since each $f_n$ is Bochner measurable, by \Cref{definition:vector_valued_lebesgue_integral_into_normed_vector_space}, the map $x \mapsto f_n(x)$ is a pointwise limit of simple functions into $V$, and therefore the composition with the norm $x \mapsto \|f_n(x)\|$ is a measurable function from $X$ to $[0, \infty]$.

Define $h_n : X \to [0, \infty]$ by $h_n(x) = \|f_n(x)\|$. Each $h_n$ is a nonnegative extended real-valued measurable function. We apply the scalar \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{lemma:fatous_lemma}{Fatou's lemma} to the sequence $(h_n)_{n \geq 1}$:
$$\int_X \liminf_{n \to \infty} h_n(x)\,d\mu(x) \leq \liminf_{n \to \infty} \int_X h_n(x)\,d\mu(x).$$

Substituting $h_n = \|f_n\|$ back yields the desired inequality:
$$\int_X \liminf_{n \to \infty} \|f_n(x)\|\,d\mu(x) \leq \liminf_{n \to \infty} \int_X \|f_n(x)\|\,d\mu(x).$$

For the second statement, suppose that $\|f_n(x)\| \leq g(x)$ for all $n$ and almost every $x$, where $g$ is integrable. Then each $\|f_n\|$ is dominated by an integrable function, so $\int_X \|f_n\|\,d\mu < \infty$, which implies that each $f_n$ is Bochner integrable by the definition. Moreover,
$$\liminf_{n \to \infty} \int_X \|f_n(x)\|\,d\mu(x) \leq \sup_{n} \int_X g\,d\mu = \int_X g\,d\mu < \infty,$$
so both sides of the inequality are finite.

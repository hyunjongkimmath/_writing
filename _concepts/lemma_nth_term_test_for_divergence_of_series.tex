    Convergence of $(S_N)$ to $v$ means that for every $\varepsilon > 0$ there exists $N_0 \geq 1$ such that $\|S_N - v\| < \varepsilon/2$ for all $N \geq N_0$\CrefIfExists{definition:convergence_and_limit_of_sequence_in_extended_metric_space_valued_in_ordered_commutative_monoid_with_adjoined_infinity}.

    $$\|v_n\| = \|S_n - v + v - S_{n-1}\| \leq \|S_n - v\| + \|v - S_{n-1}\|.$$

    Both $n$ and $n-1$ are at least $N_0$, so $\|S_n - v\| < \varepsilon/2$ and $\|S_{n-1} - v\| < \varepsilon/2$. Therefore
    $$\|v_n\| < \varepsilon/2 + \varepsilon/2 = \varepsilon.$$

    Setting $N = N_0 + 1$, we have $\|v_n\| < \varepsilon$ for all $n \geq N$. This shows that $\lim_{n \to \infty} v_n = 0$.

\begin{proposition}[Derivatives of $n^{-s}$ for fixed positive integer $n$]
\label{proposition:derivatives_of_n_to_the_minus_s}

Let $n \in \mathbb{Z}_{> 0}$ be a positive integer. Consider the function
$f_n : \mathbb{R} \to \mathbb{R}$ defined by $f_n(s) = n^{-s}$ for all $s \in \mathbb{R}$,
where $n^{-s}$ is the \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{proposition:real_exponentiation_via_exp_and_ln}{real exponentiation via exponential and logarithm}, i.e. $n^{-s} = \exp(-s \ln n)$.
Then $f_n$ is \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:differentiability_classes_Ck_Cinfty_and_Comega_for_real_valued_functions}{infinitely differentiable} on $\mathbb{R}$, and for every $k \in \mathbb{N}_0$,
$$f_n^{(k)}(s) = (-1)^k (\ln n)^k n^{-s} \quad \text{for all } s \in \mathbb{R}.$$
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}

This is exactly the claimed formula.

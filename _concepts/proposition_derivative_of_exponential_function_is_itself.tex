\begin{proposition} \label{proposition:derivative_of_exponential_function_is_itself}
The \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:real_exponential_function_with_eulers_number_as_base}{exponential function} $\exp : \mathbb{R} \to \mathbb{R}$ is \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:derivative_of_a_real_valued_function_on_a_real_interval}{differentiable} at every point $x \in \mathbb{R}$, and
$$\frac{d}{dx} \exp(x) = \exp(x)$$
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
By \Cref{definition:real_exponential_function_with_eulers_number_as_base}, we have $\exp(x) = e^x$, where $e$ is Euler's number. The derivative of $\exp$ at any point $c \in \mathbb{R}$ is the limit
$$\lim_{h \to 0} \frac{\exp(c+h) - \exp(c)}{h}.$$

By the additivity property of real exponentiation\CrefIfExists{theorem:properties_of_exponents_of_real_numbers_by_real_numbers}, we have $\exp(c+h) = e^{c+h} = e^c \cdot e^h = \exp(c) \cdot \exp(h)$. Hence
$$\frac{\exp(c+h) - \exp(c)}{h} = \frac{\exp(c)\bigl(\exp(h) - 1\bigr)}{h} = \exp(c) \cdot \frac{\exp(h) - 1}{h}.$$

To compute the limit, we must show that
$$\lim_{h \to 0} \frac{\exp(h) - 1}{h} = 1.$$

We first establish this for $h > 0$. Since $\exp(h) = e^h$ and $e > 1$, by the monotonicity of real exponentiation\CrefIfExists{theorem:properties_of_exponents_of_real_numbers_by_real_numbers}, we have $\exp(h) > \exp(0) = 1$ for all $h > 0$.

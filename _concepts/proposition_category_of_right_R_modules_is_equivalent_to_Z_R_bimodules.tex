    Let $R$ be a \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:ring}{(not necessarily commutative) ring}. The \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:category_of_modules_and_bimodules_over_rings}{category of right $R$-modules} $\mathsf{Mod}_R$ is \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:equivalence_of_categories}{equivalent} to the \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:category_of_modules_and_bimodules_over_rings}{category of $\bbZ$-$R$-bimodules} ${}_\bbZ\mathsf{Mod}_R$.

    We first construct a functor from right $R$-modules to $\bbZ$-$R$-bimodules. Let $F \colon \mathsf{Mod}_R \to {}_\bbZ\mathsf{Mod}_R$ be the functor that equips a right $R$-module $M$ with a left $\bbZ$-action via
    $$n \cdot m = \underbrace{m + \cdots + m}_{n \text{ times}} \quad (n > 0), \qquad 0 \cdot m = 0_M, \qquad (-n) \cdot m = -(n \cdot m) \quad (n > 0),$$
    for $m \in M$ and $n \in \bbZ$. The left $\bbZ$-action and the right $R$-action commute because the right action is additive:
    $$(n \cdot m) \cdot r = (\underbrace{m + \cdots + m}_{n}) \cdot r = \underbrace{mr + \cdots + mr}_{n} = n \cdot (m \cdot r).$$
    Hence, $M$ becomes a $\bbZ$-$R$-bimodule. On morphisms, $F$ acts as the identity: a right $R$-linear map is automatically $\bbZ$-linear since it preserves addition and hence integer multiples.

    Next we construct a functor from $\bbZ$-$R$-bimodules to right $R$-modules. Let $G \colon {}_\bbZ\mathsf{Mod}_R \to \mathsf{Mod}_R$ be the forgetful functor that sends a $\bbZ$-$R$-bimodule to its underlying right $R$-module (forgetting the left $\bbZ$-action).

    Finally we verify that $F$ and $G$ are mutually inverse equivalences:
    \begin{enumerate}
        \item $F \circ G$ is naturally isomorphic to $\operatorname{id}_{{}_\bbZ\mathsf{Mod}_R}$, because for any $\bbZ$-$R$-bimodule $M$, the left $\bbZ$-action is uniquely determined by the additive structure of $M$.
        \item $G \circ F = \operatorname{id}_{\mathsf{Mod}_R}$, because the underlying right $R$-module of $F(M)$ is exactly $M$.
    \end{enumerate}
    Any right $R$-linear map between bimodules is automatically a bimodule map: if $f \colon M \to N$ satisfies $f(mr) = f(m)r$, then it also satisfies $f(n \cdot m) = n \cdot f(m)$ for all $n \in \bbZ$. Hence, $F$ is fully faithful and essentially surjective.
\end{proposition}

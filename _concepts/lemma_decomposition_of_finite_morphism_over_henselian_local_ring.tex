\begin{proof}

    \textbf{Algebraic form.} Since $A$ is a finite $R$-algebra, the fiber $A \otimes_R k = A/\mathfrak{m}A$ is a finite-dimensional $k$-algebra and hence Artinian. An Artinian ring decomposes uniquely as a finite product of local Artinian rings:
    $$ A/\mathfrak{m}A \cong \prod_{i=1}^n (A/\mathfrak{m}A)_{\mathfrak{n}_i}, $$
    where $\mathfrak{n}_1, \dots, \mathfrak{n}_n$ are the prime ideals of $A$ lying over $\mathfrak{m}$ (these are precisely the maximal ideals of $A/\mathfrak{m}A$). The idempotents of this product lift uniquely to orthogonal idempotents of $A$ because $R$ is Henselian: a factorization of $1$ into orthogonal idempotents modulo $\mathfrak{m}$ lifts to $A$\CrefIfExists{definition:henselian_ring}. These lifted idempotents yield the decomposition $A \cong \prod_{i=1}^n A e_i$, where each $A e_i$ is a local finite $R$-algebra with maximal ideal corresponding to $\mathfrak{n}_i$.

    \textbf{Geometric form.} Since $f$ is finite, it is affine. After replacing $Y$ and $X$ by affine open neighborhoods of $\bar{y}$ and $f^{-1}(\bar{y})$, we may assume $Y = \Spec(R)$ and $X = \Spec(A)$ for some finite $R$-algebra $A$. Let $(R^{sh}, \mathfrak{m}^{sh})$ be the strict henselization of $R$ at $\bar{y}$; this is a \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:strictly_henselian_ring}{strictly henselian local ring}\CrefIfExists{definition:strict_henselization_of_a_local_ring}, hence Henselian. By the algebraic form, we have $A \otimes_R R^{sh} \cong \prod_{\bar{x} \in f^{-1}(\bar{y})} (A \otimes_R R^{sh})_{\mathfrak{n}_{\bar{x}}}$ where each factor is local.

    Since the decomposition is given by a finite set of orthogonal idempotents in $A \otimes_R R^{sh}$, and $R^{sh}$ is a filtered colimit of étale $R$-algebras, these idempotents descend to some étale $R$-algebra $S$. That is, there exists an étale morphism $V = \Spec(S) \to Y$ and a point $\bar{v} : \Spec(\Omega) \to V$ lifting $\bar{y}$ such that the idempotents defining the decomposition exist in $A \otimes_R S$. Shrinking $V$ to an affine étale neighborhood if necessary, we obtain
    $$ X \times_Y V = \Spec(A \otimes_R S) \cong \coprod_{\bar{x} \in f^{-1}(\bar{y})} Z_{\bar{x}} $$
    as a disjoint union of open-and-closed subschemes. Each $Z_{\bar{x}}$ is affine, finite over $V$, and contains exactly one geometric point $\bar{x}$ lying over $\bar{v}$.
\end{proof}

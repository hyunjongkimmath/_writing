\begin{proposition}[Weierstrass factorization of the sine function] \label{proposition:weierstrass_factorization_sine_function}

The \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:sine_and_cosine_functions_on_real_line_via_power_series}{sine function} has the infinite product representation
$$
\sin(\pi z) = \pi z \prod_{n=1}^\infty \left( 1 - \frac{z^2}{n^2} \right), \qquad z \in \bbC.
$$
The product converges absolutely and uniformly on compact subsets of $\bbC$.

This is a special case of the Weierstrass factorization theorem for entire functions of order 1. The zeros of $\sin(\pi z)$ are exactly the integers $z \in \bbZ$, with simple zeros at $z = 0, \pm 1, \pm 2, \dots$.

Let $r$ and $k$ be positive integers. There exists a positive integer $W(r,k)$, called the \hldef{Van der Waerden number $W(r,k)$}, such that for every coloring of the set $\{1, 2, \dots, W(r,k)\}$ with $r$ colors, there exists a monochromatic arithmetic progression of length $k$. That is, there exist integers $a$ and $d > 0$ such that
$$\hlin{\{a, a+d, a+2d, \dots, a+(k-1)d\} \subseteq \{1, 2, \dots, W(r,k)\}}$$
and all elements of this progression receive the same color.

This theorem is a result in arithmetic Ramsey theory, extending the spirit of Ramsey's theorem from combinatorial structures (cliques) to arithmetic ones (progressions).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proposition}[Independence equals product of distributions] \label{proposition:independence_equals_product_of_distributions}
Let $X$ and $Y$ be random variables defined on the same \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:probability_measure_space_for_a_measure_on_a_measurable_space}{probability measure space}. Then $X$ and $Y$ are \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:independence_of_random_variables_on_a_probability_measure_space}{independent} if and only if the joint distribution of $(X, Y)$ equals the product of their marginal distributions:
$$\mu_{(X,Y)} = \mu_X \otimes \mu_Y.$$

More generally, let $\{X_1, \dots, X_n\}$ be a finite family of random variables. They are mutually independent if and only if the joint distribution of $(X_1, \dots, X_n)$ equals the product measure $\mu_{X_1} \otimes \cdots \otimes \mu_{X_n}$.
\end{proposition}

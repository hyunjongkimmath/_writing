\begin{proposition} \label{proposition:derivatives_of_sine_and_cosine}
The \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:real_sine_and_cosine_functions_on_real_line_via_power_series}{(real) sine and cosine functions} are \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:derivative_of_a_real_valued_function_on_a_real_interval}{differentiable} at every point $x \in \mathbb{R}$, and
$$\frac{d}{dx} \sin(x) = \cos(x)$$
$$\frac{d}{dx} \cos(x) = -\sin(x)$$
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
    The sine and cosine functions are defined by the \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:real_power_series_centered_at_a_real_number_and_radius_of_convergence_of_a_real_power_series}{power series}:
    $$\sin(x) = \sum_{n=0}^\infty (-1)^n \frac{x^{2n+1}}{(2n+1)!}, \qquad \cos(x) = \sum_{n=0}^\infty (-1)^n \frac{x^{2n}}{(2n)!}.$$
    Both series \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:absolute_convergence_of_a_series_in_module_over_a_commutative_ring_with_an_absolute_value_valued_in_an_ordered_semiring}{converge absolutely} for every $x \in \mathbb{R}$.

    For $\sin(x)$, let $f_n(x) = (-1)^n \frac{x^{2n+1}}{(2n+1)!}$. Each $f_n$ is differentiable with
    $$f_n'(x) = (-1)^n \frac{(2n+1)x^{2n}}{(2n+1)!} = (-1)^n \frac{x^{2n}}{(2n)!}.$$
    The series of derivatives is $\sum_{n=0}^\infty f_n'(x) = \sum_{n=0}^\infty (-1)^n \frac{x^{2n}}{(2n)!}$, which is exactly the power series for $\cos(x)$. On any bounded interval $[-R, R]$, we have
    $$|f_n'(x)| \leq \frac{R^{2n}}{(2n)!}.$$
    The series $\sum_{n=0}^\infty \frac{R^{2n}}{(2n)!}$ \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:infinite_series_and_convergence_of_infinite_series_in_a_topological_abelian_semigroup}{converges} by the \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{theorem:comparison_test_for_series_of_nonnegative_real_numbers}{comparison test} --- one notes that $\sum_{n=0}^\infty \frac{R^{2n}}{(2n)!} \leq \sum_{m=0}^\infty \frac{R^m}{m!} = e^R$\CrefIfExists{proposition:real_exponential_function_taylor_series}. Thus, by the \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{theorem:weierstrass_m_test_for_uniform_convergence_of_series_of_functions_from_sets_to_banach_spaces_over_a_absolute_valued_ring_valued_in_the_reals}{Weierstrass M-test}, $\sum f_n'$ \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:uniform_convergence_of_a_series_of_functions_from_a_set_to_an_extended_metric_space_valued_in_ordered_commutative_monoid_with_adjoined_infinity}{converges uniformly} on $[-R, R]$. By \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{theorem:termwise_differentiation_of_series_under_uniform_convergence_of_derivative_series}{the termwise differentiation theorem},
    $$\frac{d}{dx} \sin(x) = \sum_{n=0}^\infty f_n'(x) = \sum_{n=0}^\infty (-1)^n \frac{x^{2n}}{(2n)!} =  \cos(x).$$

    For $\cos(x)$, let $g_n(x) = (-1)^n \frac{x^{2n}}{(2n)!}$. Each $g_n$ is differentiable with
    $$g_n'(x) = \begin{cases} 0 & \text{if } n = 0, \\ (-1)^n \frac{2n \cdot x^{2n-1}}{(2n)!} = (-1)^n \frac{x^{2n-1}}{(2n-1)!} & \text{if } n \geq 1. \end{cases}$$
    The series of derivatives is $\sum_{n=1}^\infty g_n'(x)$. Setting $k = n-1$, we get
    $$\sum_{k=0}^\infty (-1)^{k+1} \frac{x^{2k+1}}{(2k+1)!} = -\sum_{k=0}^\infty (-1)^k \frac{x^{2k+1}}{(2k+1)!} = -\sin(x).$$
    On any bounded interval $[-R, R]$, we have $|g_n'(x)| \leq \frac{R^{2n-1}}{(2n-1)!}$ for $n \geq 1$. The series $\sum_{k=0}^\infty \frac{R^{2k+1}}{(2k+1)!}$ converges similarly as above, so again by the Weierstrass M-test and termwise differentiation theorem,
    $$\frac{d}{dx} \cos(x) = -\sin(x).$$
\end{proof}

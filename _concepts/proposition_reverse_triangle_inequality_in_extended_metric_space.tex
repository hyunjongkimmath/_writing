\begin{proposition}[Reverse triangle inequality in an extended metric space] \label{proposition:reverse_triangle_inequality_in_extended_metric_space}
    Let $T$ be a commutative \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:ordered_semiring}{ordered semiring}, let $T^\infty$ be the \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:ordered_semiring_with_adjoined_infinity}{ordered semiring with adjoined infinity} obtained from $T$, and let $(M, d)$ be an \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:extended_metric_on_a_set_valued_in_an_ordered_commutative_monoid_with_adjoined_infinity}{extended metric space valued in $T^\infty$}.

    Let $R$ be an ordered ring containing $T$ as a sub-semiring (e.g., the Grothendieck group of $T$\TODO{AI generated, reference needed: proposition that the Grothendieck group of a commutative ordered semiring is an ordered ring into which T embeds}). For all $x, y, z \in M$, interpreting differences in $R$,
    \[
    |d(x, z) - d(y, z)|_R \leq_R d(x, y).
    \]
    Equivalently, with inequalities taken in $R$,
    \[
    d(x, z) \geq_R d(x, y) - d(y, z)
    \quad\text{and}\quad
    d(x, z) \geq_R d(y, z) - d(x, y).
    \]

    In the standard case $T = \bbR_{\geq 0}$, we take $R = \bbR$\CrefIfExists{proposition:positive_cone_of_ordered_ring_is_ordered_semiring}.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
    By the triangle inequality for $d$ (\Cref{definition:extended_metric_on_a_set_valued_in_an_ordered_commutative_monoid_with_adjoined_infinity}),
    \[
    d(x, z) \leq_T d(x, y) +_T d(y, z)
    \quad\text{and}\quad
    d(y, z) \leq_T d(y, x) +_T d(x, z).
    \]
    By symmetry $d(y, x) = d(x, y)$, the second inequality becomes $d(y, z) \leq_T d(x, y) +_T d(x, z)$.

    Because $T$ embeds into the ordered ring $R$, these inequalities also hold in $R$:
    \[
    d(x, z) \leq_R d(x, y) +_R d(y, z)
    \quad\text{and}\quad
    d(y, z) \leq_R d(x, y) +_R d(x, z).
    \]
    In $R$ we may subtract. Subtracting $d(y, z)$ from both sides of the first inequality gives $d(x, z) - d(y, z) \leq_R d(x, y)$. Subtracting $d(x, z)$ from both sides of the second inequality gives $d(y, z) - d(x, z) \leq_R d(x, y)$. Together these imply $|d(x, z) - d(y, z)|_R \leq_R d(x, y)$.
\end{proof}

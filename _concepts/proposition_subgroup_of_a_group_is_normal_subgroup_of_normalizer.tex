\begin{proposition} \label{proposition:subgroup_of_a_group_is_normal_subgroup_of_normalizer}
    Let $(G,\cdot)$ be a \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:group}{group} and let $H \subseteq G$ be a \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:subgroup_of_a_group}{subgroup}. The group $H$ is a \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:normal_subgroup_of_a_group}{normal subgroup} of the \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:normalizer_of_a_subgroup_of_a_group}{normalizer $N_G(H)$}.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
    By \Cref{lemma:normalizer_of_subgroup_is_subgroup_of_group}, the set $N_G(H)$ is a \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:subgroup_of_a_group}{subgroup} of $G$.

    We first show that $H \subseteq N_G(H)$. For any $h \in H$, we have $h H h^{-1} = H$ because $H$ is a \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:subgroup_of_a_group}{subgroup} of $G$: the map $H \to H$ given by $x \mapsto h x h^{-1}$ is well-defined and has inverse $x \mapsto h^{-1} x h$. Hence, $h \in N_G(H)$ by definition of the \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:normalizer_of_a_subgroup_of_a_group}{normalizer}.

    We now show that $H \trianglelefteq N_G(H)$. For any $n \in N_G(H)$ and $h \in H$, we have $n H n^{-1} = H$ by definition of the \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:normalizer_of_a_subgroup_of_a_group}{normalizer}, so in particular $n h n^{-1} \in H$. Therefore, $H$ is a \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:normal_subgroup_of_a_group}{normal subgroup} of $N_G(H)$.
\end{proof}

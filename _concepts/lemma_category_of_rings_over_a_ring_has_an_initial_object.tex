\begin{lemma} \label{lemma:category_of_rings_over_a_ring_has_an_initial_object}
    Let $R$ be a not necessarily commutative \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:ring}{ring}.
    The category \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:category_of_rings_over_a_ring}{$\mathbf{Ring}_R$} of rings over $R$ has an \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:initial_final_zero_objects_of_a_category}{initial object}, namely $R$ equipped with the identity morphism.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
    Consider the object $(R, \operatorname{id}_R)$ in $\mathbf{Ring}_R$. Let $(S, \varphi)$ be any other object of $\mathbf{Ring}_R$, where $\varphi: R \to S$ is a ring homomorphism. We show that there exists a unique morphism $f: (R, \operatorname{id}_R) \to (S, \varphi)$ in $\mathbf{Ring}_R$.

    A morphism $(R, \operatorname{id}_R) \to (S, \varphi)$ is by definition a ring homomorphism $f: R \to S$ such that the following triangle commutes:
    \begin{center}
        \begin{tikzcd}
            & R \arrow[ld, "\operatorname{id}_R"'] \arrow[rd, "\varphi"] & \\
            R \arrow[rr, "f"'] & & S
        \end{tikzcd}
    \end{center}
    The commutativity condition requires $\varphi = f \circ \operatorname{id}_R$, which simplifies to $f = \varphi$. Hence the only candidate is $f = \varphi$.

    Since $\varphi: R \to S$ is already a ring homomorphism (as part of the object $(S, \varphi)$), it satisfies the morphism condition. Therefore, exactly one morphism exists from $(R, \operatorname{id}_R)$ to $(S, \varphi)$.

    In conclusion, $(R, \operatorname{id}_R)$ is an \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:initial_final_zero_objects_of_a_category}{initial object} of $\mathbf{Ring}_R$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Sufficient condition for Taylor series convergence] \label{theorem:taylor_series_convergence_criterion_for_real_functions}

Let $I \subseteq \mathbb{R}$ be an open interval, let $a \in I$, and let $f : I \to \mathbb{R}$ be a function that is infinitely \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:derivative_of_a_real_valued_function_on_a_real_interval}{differentiable} on $I$.

Suppose there exist constants $\delta > 0$ such that $(a - \delta, a + \delta) \subseteq I$, and constants $M_k < \infty$ for each integer $k \geq 0$, satisfying
$$\sup_{x : |x - a| < \delta} |f^{(k)}(x)| \leq M_k.$$

Let $R$ be the \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:real_power_series_centered_at_a_real_number_and_radius_of_convergence_of_a_real_power_series}{radius of convergence} of the power series $\sum_{k=0}^\infty (M_k / k!) z^k$. If $R > 0$, then for all $x$ with $|x - a| < R$:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item The \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:taylor_and_maclaurin_series_of_a_real_valued_function_smooth_at_a_point_in_an_interval_in_the_real_line}{Taylor series} of $f$ centered at $a$ \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:absolute_convergence_of_a_series_in_module_over_a_commutative_ring_with_an_absolute_value_valued_in_an_ordered_semiring}{converges absolutely}.
    \item The sum of the Taylor series equals $f(x)$. That is,
        $$f(x) = \sum_{k=0}^\infty \frac{f^{(k)}(a)}{k!}(x - a)^k,$$
    and $f$ is \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:real_analytic_function_on_an_open_interval}{real analytic} on the interval $(R-a, R+a)$.
\end{enumerate}

In particular, if there exist constants $M < \infty$ and $\rho > 0$ such that for all $k \geq 0$,
$$\sup_{x : |x - a| < \delta} |f^{(k)}(x)| \leq M \cdot k! / \rho^k,$$
then the Taylor series of $f$ centered at $a$ converges to $f(x)$ for all $x$ with $|x - a| < \rho$.

More generally, if $f$ is infinitely differentiable and its Lagrange remainder $R_n(x)$ of order $n$ from \Cref{theorem:taylor_formula_with_lagrange_remainder_for_real_functions_that_are_n_plus_one_times_differentiable_at_a_point} satisfies $\lim_{n \to \infty} R_n(x) = 0$, then the Taylor series converges to $f(x)$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}

We show that the Taylor series of $f$ centered at $a$ converges absolutely and equals $f(x)$ for all $x$ with $|x - a| < R$. The proof proceeds in three parts: absolute convergence of the Taylor series, equality to $f(x)$ via the Lagrange remainder, and the corollary about uniform derivative bounds.

First, we show that the Taylor series converges absolutely for $|x - a| < R$. For each $k$, we have $|f^{(k)}(a)| \leq M_k$ by hypothesis. The terms of the Taylor series satisfy
$$\left|\frac{f^{(k)}(a)}{k!}(x - a)^k\right| \leq \frac{M_k}{k!}|x - a|^k.$$
By definition, $R$ is the radius of convergence of $\sum_{k=0}^\infty (M_k / k!) z^k$. For any $x$ with $|x - a| < R$, this series converges absolutely. By the comparison test \Cref{theorem:comparison_test_for_series_of_nonnegative_real_numbers}, the Taylor series also converges absolutely at $x$.

Second, we show that the sum of the Taylor series equals $f(x)$. Let $P_n(x)$ denote the $n$-th partial sum of the Taylor series. By Taylor's formula with Lagrange remainder \Cref{theorem:taylor_formula_with_lagrange_remainder_for_real_functions_that_are_n_plus_one_times_differentiable_at_a_point}, for each $n$ there exists $\xi_n$ strictly between $a$ and $x$ such that
$$f(x) - P_n(x) = \frac{f^{(n+1)}(\xi_n)}{(n+1)!}(x - a)^{n+1}.$$
We estimate the magnitude of the remainder. Since $|\xi_n - a| < |x - a| < R$, and $R$ is the radius of convergence of $\sum_{k=0}^\infty (M_k / k!) z^k$, we have $|x - a| < \delta$ for sufficiently small neighborhoods where the bounds on derivatives apply. We obtain
$$|f(x) - P_n(x)| = \frac{|f^{(n+1)}(\xi_n)|}{(n+1)!}|x - a|^{n+1} \leq \frac{M_{n+1}}{(n+1)!}|x - a|^{n+1}.$$
The right-hand side is the $(n+1)$-st term of the convergent series $\sum_{k=0}^\infty (M_k / k!)(x - a)^k$, which tends to zero as $n \to \infty$. Therefore, $\lim_{n \to \infty} |f(x) - P_n(x)| = 0$, so $P_n(x)$ converges to $f(x)$.

Third, we verify the special case. Suppose that there exist constants $M < \infty$ and $\rho > 0$ such that $|f^{(k)}(x)| \leq M \cdot k! / \rho^k$ for all $k \geq 0$ and all $x$ with $|x - a| < \delta$. Then the series $\sum_{k=0}^\infty (M_k / k!) z^k$ has coefficients bounded by $(M \cdot k! / \rho^k) / k! = M / \rho^k$, which is a geometric series with ratio $1/\rho$. The radius of convergence satisfies $R \geq \rho > 0$. For any $x$ with $|x - a| < \rho$, the remainder estimate gives
$$|f(x) - P_n(x)| \leq M \frac{|x - a|^{n+1}}{\rho^{n+1}} = M \left(\frac{|x - a|}{\rho}\right)^{n+1}.$$
Since $|x - a| / \rho < 1$, the right-hand side tends to zero as $n \to \infty$. Therefore, $P_n(x)$ converges to $f(x)$.

The more general statement follows: if $R_n(x) \to 0$ as $n \to \infty$, then by definition of $R_n(x)$ in \Cref{theorem:taylor_formula_with_lagrange_remainder_for_real_functions_that_are_n_plus_one_times_differentiable_at_a_point}, the partial sums $P_n(x)$ converge to $f(x)$, so the Taylor series converges to $f(x)$.
\end{proof}

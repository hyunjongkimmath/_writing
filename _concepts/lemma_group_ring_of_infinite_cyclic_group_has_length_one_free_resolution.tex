\begin{lemma} \label{lemma:group_ring_of_infinite_cyclic_group_has_length_one_free_resolution}
    Let $\mathbb{Z}$ be the infinite cyclic \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:group}{group} generated by $\sigma$. Let $R$ be a \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:ring}{(not necessarily commutative) ring}. The sequence
    $$0 \to R[\mathbb{Z}] \xrightarrow{\cdot(\sigma-1)} R[\mathbb{Z}] \xrightarrow{\varepsilon} R \to 0$$
    is an exact \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:left_right_sequence_of_modules_over_rings}{sequence} of \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:free_bimodule_over_rings}{free} left $R[\mathbb{Z}]$-\CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:module_of_a_cosmos_between_categories_enriched_in_the_cosmos}{modules}, where $\varepsilon: R[\mathbb{Z}] \to R$ is the \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:augmentation_map_of_a_group_ring_to_its_base_ring}{augmentation map}. In particular, it is a \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:left_right_resolution_of_an_object_by_a_class_of_objects_in_an_abelian_category}{projective resolution} of the \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:trivial_G_module_of_a_group_over_a_ring}{trivial $R[\mathbb{Z}]$-module} $R$ of length $1$.
\end{lemma}

    The map $\varepsilon$ is surjective with kernel equal to the \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:augmentation_map_of_a_group_ring_to_its_base_ring}{augmentation ideal} $I = \ker(\varepsilon)$. Since $R[\mathbb{Z}] \cong R[t, t^{-1}]$ via $\sigma \mapsto t$, the augmentation ideal is generated by $\sigma - 1$ (equivalently $t-1$ in the Laurent polynomial ring).

    Left multiplication by $\sigma-1$ on $R[\mathbb{Z}]$ is injective because $R[\mathbb{Z}] \cong R[t, t^{-1}]$ is a domain with respect to multiplication by $(t-1)$: if $(t-1)P(t) = 0$ in $R[t, t^{-1}]$, then clearing denominators gives $(t-1)t^k P(t) = 0$ in $R[t]$, which implies $P(t) = 0$ since $R[t]$ has no zero divisors from monomials.

    The image of $\cdot(\sigma-1)$ is exactly the ideal generated by $\sigma-1$, which equals $\ker(\varepsilon)$. Hence the sequence is exact at every position.
\end{proof}

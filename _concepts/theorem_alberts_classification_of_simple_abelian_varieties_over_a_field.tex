\begin{theorem}[Albert's classification of simple abelian varieties] \label{theorem:alberts_classification_of_simple_abelian_varieties_over_a_field}

    Let $A$ be a \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:simple_abelian_variety_over_a_field}{simple abelian variety} over a \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:field}{field} $k$. Then $\End^0(A) = \End(A) \otimes_{\Z} \Q$ is a central division algebra over $\Q$, and any Rosati involution (associated to a principal polarization on $A$) is a positive involution. Let $D = \End^0(A)$, let $*$ denote the Rosati involution, and set $F = D^{*}$, the fixed field of $*$ in $D$. Then exactly one of the following four cases holds:

    \begin{enumerate}
        \item \textbf{Type I (Totally real):} $D = F$ is a \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:totally_real_and_totally_imaginary_number_fields}{totally real number field} with $[F:\Q] = \dim A$, and $*$ is the identity on $F$.

        \TODO{totally definite quaternion algebra}
        \item \textbf{Type II (Totally definite quaternion over a totally real field):} $D$ is a totally definite quaternion algebra over its center $F$, a totally real number field of degree $[F:\Q] = d$, with $*$ inducing the identity on $F$. We have $\dim A$ divisible by $2d$.

        \item \textbf{Type III (Totally indefinite quaternion over a totally real field):} $D$ is a totally indefinite quaternion algebra over its center $F$, a totally real number field, with $*$ inducing the identity on $F$. Here $\dim A$ is even.

        \item \textbf{Type IV (Central division algebra over a CM field):} $D$ is a \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:central_algebra_over_a_commutative_ring}{central} \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:division_ring}{division} \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:algebra_of_a_ring}{algebra} over a \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:cm_field}{CM field} $E$ of degree $[E:\Q] = 2d$, with $*$ inducing the complex conjugation on $E$. Let $e$ be the index of $D$ (i.e. $\dim_E D = e^2$). Then there exists a positive integer $m$ such that $\dim A = m \cdot [E:\Q] \cdot e / 2$.
    \end{enumerate}

    Conversely, for each type and each admissible combination of invariants, there exists a simple abelian variety over some field whose endomorphism algebra realizes the given structure.
\end{theorem}

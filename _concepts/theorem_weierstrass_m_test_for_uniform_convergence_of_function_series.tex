\begin{theorem}[Weierstrass M-test for uniform convergence of function series] \label{theorem:weierstrass_m_test_for_uniform_convergence_of_function_series}

    Let $T$ be an \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:ordered_semiring}{ordered semiring}, let $T^\infty$ be the \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:ordered_semiring_with_adjoined_infinity}{ordered semiring with adjoined infinity} obtained from $T$, and let $R$ be a \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:commutative_ring}{commutative ring} equipped with an \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:absolute_value_on_a_ring_valued_in_an_ordered_semiring}{absolute value valued in $T$}. Let $(M, \|\cdot\|)$ be a \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:banach_space_over_a_commutative_ring_with_an_absolute_value_valued_in_an_ordered_semiring}{complete extended normed module over $R$ valued in $T^\infty$}. Let $S$ be a set and let $(f_n)_{n=1}^\infty$ be a sequence of functions $f_n : S \to M$.

    Suppose there exists a sequence $(M_n)_{n=1}^\infty$ in $T_{\ge 0}$ such that:
    \begin{enumerate}
        \item For each $n$ and each $x \in S$, $\|f_n(x)\| \le M_n$;
        \item The series $\sum_{n=1}^\infty M_n$ converges in $T$ (i.e., the sequence of partial sums converges in $T$ with respect to the order topology, or in the case $T = \bbR_{\ge 0}$, in the standard topology on $\bbR$)\CrefIfExists{definition:field_of_real_numbers}.
    \end{enumerate}
    Then the series $\sum_{n=1}^\infty f_n(x)$ \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:uniform_convergence_of_a_series_of_functions_from_a_set_to_an_extended_metric_space}{converges uniformly} on $S$ to a function $f : S \to M$, and for every $N \in \mathbb{N}$,
    $$\sup_{x \in S} \left\|f(x) - \sum_{n=1}^N f_n(x)\right\| \le \sum_{n=N+1}^\infty M_n.$$
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}

    We prove pointwise convergence first, then uniform convergence.

    Fix $x \in S$. The partial sums of the series $\sum f_n(x)$ form a sequence in $M$. For any $K > N$, by the \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:extended_norm_on_a_module_over_a_commutative_ring_with_absolute_value}{triangle inequality} for the norm, we have
    $$\left\|\sum_{n=N+1}^K f_n(x)\right\| \le \sum_{n=N+1}^K \|f_n(x)\| \leq \sum_{n=N+1}^K M_n.$$

    Since $\sum M_n$ converges in $T$, by the \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{proposition:cauchy_criterion_for_convergence_of_series_of_real_numbers}{Cauchy criterion for series}, for every $\varepsilon \in T_{>0}$ there exists $N_0$ such that for all $K > N \ge N_0$:
    $$\sum_{n=N+1}^K M_n < \varepsilon.$$

    Therefore, the partial sums satisfy, uniformly in $x$, the Cauchy condition:
    $$\left\|\sum_{n=N+1}^K f_n(x)\right\| < \varepsilon \quad \text{for all } x \in S.$$

    In other words, for any $K > N$, we have
    $$\left\|\sum_{n=1}^K f_n(x) - \sum_{n=1}^N f_n(x)\right\| = \left\|\sum_{n=N+1}^K f_n(x)\right\| < \varepsilon,$$
    i.e. the partial sums $(\sum_{n=1}^N f_n(x))_{N=1}^\infty$ form a Cauchy sequence in $M$. Since $M$ is \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:complete_extended_metric_space}{complete}, for each fixed $x$ the series $\sum f_n(x)$ thus converges to some limit, which we denote by $f(x)$. In fact, note that the convergence is \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:uniformly_Cauchy_sequence_of_functions_from_a_set_to_an_extended_metric_space}{uniformly Cauchy}. By \Cref{proposition:uniform_convergence_implies_bounded_and_uniformly_Cauchy_for_sequences_of_functions}, since $(M, \|\cdot\|)$ is complete (with respect to the extended metric induced by $\|\cdot\|$), the convergence is thus \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:uniform_convergence_of_a_sequence_of_functions_from_a_set_to_an_extended_metric_space}{uniform}. 
\end{proof}

Let $G$ and $H$ be \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:group}{groups}, and let $\phi: G \to H$ be a \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:group_homomorphism}{group homomorphism}. Let $\operatorname{ker}(\phi)$ denote the \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:kernel_image_of_group_homomorphism}{kernel of $\phi$} and $\operatorname{im}(\phi)$ denote the \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:kernel_image_of_group_homomorphism}{image of $\phi$}.

Then $\operatorname{ker}(\phi)$ is a \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:normal_subgroup_of_a_group}{normal subgroup} of $G$, and there exists a unique \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:group_homomorphism}{group isomorphism}
$$ \hlin{\bar{\phi}: G/\operatorname{ker}(\phi) \xrightarrow{\,\sim\,} \operatorname{im}(\phi)} $$
such that $\bar{\phi}(g \cdot \operatorname{ker}(\phi)) = \phi(g)$ for all $g \in G$. Here $G/\operatorname{ker}(\phi)$ denotes the \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:quotient_of_a_group_by_a_normal_subgroup}{quotient group}.

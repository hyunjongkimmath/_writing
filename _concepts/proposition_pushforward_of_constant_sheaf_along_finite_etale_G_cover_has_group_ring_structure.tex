\textbf{(a)} Since $G$ acts on $X$ over $Y$, for each $g \in G$ the automorphism $g: X \to X$ induces a pullback $(g^{-1})^*$ on sheaves on $X_{\text{ét}}$. Because $\underline{\Lambda}$ is constant, $(g^{-1})^*\underline{\Lambda} = \underline{\Lambda}$, so there are natural maps
$$ f_*\underline{\Lambda} \xrightarrow{(f \circ g)_* \underline{\Lambda}} f_*\underline{\Lambda} $$
for each $g \in G$, giving a well-defined action of $G$ on $f_*\underline{\Lambda}$ by sheaf automorphisms. Since $\underline{\Lambda}$ is a sheaf of $\Lambda$-algebras and the maps are induced by pullbacks along automorphisms, each $g$ acts by $\Lambda$-algebra automorphisms. The sheaf $f_*\underline{\Lambda}$ is locally constant because after pulling back to $Y$ via $f$, it becomes trivial: $f^* f_* \underline{\Lambda} \cong \underline{\Lambda}[G]$ as a trivial local system.

\textbf{(b)} By the proper base change theorem for finite morphisms, the geometric fiber $(f_*\underline{\Lambda})_{\bar{y}}$ is canonically isomorphic to the global sections of $\underline{\Lambda}$ on the fiber $X_{\bar{y}} = X \times_Y \Spec(\Omega)$. Since $f$ is a finite étale Galois cover with group $G$, the fiber $X_{\bar{y}}$ consists of $|G|$ points permuted simply transitively by $G$. Fixing a geometric point $\bar{x}$ in the fiber identifies $X_{\bar{y}}$ with $G$ as a right $G$-set. The sections of the constant sheaf $\underline{\Lambda}$ on this finite discrete scheme are the functions $G \to \Lambda$, which is canonically $\Lambda[G]$. The left $G$-action on the direct image corresponds to left translation on $G$, hence on $\Lambda[G]$.

\textbf{(c)} For any étale $V \to Y$, the sections $(f_*\underline{\Lambda})(V) = \underline{\Lambda}(X \times_Y V)$ are the locally constant functions $X \times_Y V \to \Lambda$. The $G$-invariant sections are precisely those functions constant on $G$-orbits, which descend to functions on $(X \times_Y V)/G = V$ (since $f$ is the quotient map $X \to X/G = Y$). Hence $(f_*\underline{\Lambda})^G(V) = \underline{\Lambda}_Y(V)$ naturally in $V$.
\end{proof}

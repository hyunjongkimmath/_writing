\begin{lemma} \label{lemma:base_change_of_projective_modules_along_ring_homomorphisms_are_projective}
    Let $R$ and $S$ be \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:ring}{rings} (not necessarily commutative), and let $f: R \to S$ be a \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:ring_homomorphism}{ring homomorphism}. 
    Let $M$ be a \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:projective_bimodule_over_rings}{projective} right (resp. left) \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:module_of_a_ring}{$R$-module}. The \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:extension_of_scalars_base_change_induction_for_modules_along_ring_homomorphisms}{base change} $M \otimes_R S$ (resp. $S \otimes_R M$) is a projective right (resp. left) $S$-module.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
    A module over a ring is projective if and only if it is a direct summand of a free module \CrefIfExists{proposition:module_over_ring_is_projective_if_and_only_if_direct_summand_of_a_free_module}. In particular $M$ is a direct summand of a free $R$-module, so $M \otimes_R S$ is a direct summand of a free $S$-module and hence is a projective $S$-module.
\end{proof}

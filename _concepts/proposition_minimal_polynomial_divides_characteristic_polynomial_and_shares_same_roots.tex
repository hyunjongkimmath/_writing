\begin{proposition} \label{proposition:minimal_polynomial_divides_characteristic_polynomial_and_shares_same_roots}
    Let $F$ be a \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:field}{field}, and let $V$ be a finite-dimensional \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:vector_space_over_a_field}{$F$-vector space}. Let $T: V \to V$ be a linear endomorphism.

    The \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:minimal_polynomial_of_an_endomorphism_of_a_vector_space_over_a_field}{minimal polynomial} $m_T(t)$ divides the \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:characteristic_polynomial_of_an_endomorphism_of_a_vector_space_over_a_field}{characteristic polynomial} $\chi_T(t)$. Moreover, $m_T$ and $\chi_T$ have the same irreducible factors (equivalently, the same roots in any extension field of $F$).
\TextIfExists{definition:bimodule_over_rings_module_over_a_ring}{The minimal polynomial characterizes the $F[t]$-module structure of $V$ under $T$: it is the monic generator of the annihilator ideal $\operatorname{Ann}_{F[t]}(V) \subseteq F[t]$. The fact that $m_T$ divides $\chi_T$ and shares the same irreducible factors reflects the structure theorem for finitely generated modules over a principal ideal domain.}

\begin{corollary}[Trivial zeros of the Riemann zeta function] \label{corollary:trivial_zeros_of_riemann_zeta_function}

The \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:riemann_zeta_function}{Riemann zeta function} has simple zeros at the negative even integers:
$$
\zeta(-2n) = 0 \quad \text{for all } n \in \bbN.
$$
These are called the \hldef{trivial zeros} of $\zeta(s)$.

They arise from the poles of $\Gamma(s/2)$ in the functional equation $\xi(s) = \xi(1-s)$; the gamma function has simple poles at $s = 0, -2, -4, \dots$, which cancel the analytic continuation of $\zeta(s)$ to produce zeros.

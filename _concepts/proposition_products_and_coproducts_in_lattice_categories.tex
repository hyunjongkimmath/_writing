\begin{proposition}[Products and coproducts in lattice categories] \label{proposition:products_and_coproducts_in_lattice_categories}

For the categories $\mathbf{Lat}$, $\mathbf{CLat}$, and $\mathbf{\Sigma CLat}$ defined in \Cref{definition:category_of_posets_and_lattices}:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item [\textbf{(Products)}] The categorical product of a family of lattices $\{L_i\}_{i \in I}$ exists in $\mathbf{Lat}$ and is given by the Cartesian product set $\prod_{i \in I} L_i$ with componentwise join and meet:
    $$(x_i)_i \vee (y_i)_i = (x_i \vee_{L_i} y_i)_i, \quad (x_i)_i \wedge (y_i)_i = (x_i \wedge_{L_i} y_i)_i.$$
    The same construction works in $\mathbf{CLat}$ and $\mathbf{\Sigma CLat}$: the componentwise suprema and infima of arbitrary (resp.\ countable) families exist because each $L_i$ has them.

    \item [\textbf{(Coproducts)}] The coproduct in $\mathbf{Lat}$ is not a simple disjoint union or Cartesian construction. For two lattices $L$ and $M$, the coproduct $L *_{\mathbf{Lat}} M$ is the free lattice generated by the disjoint union $L \sqcup M$, subject to relations that preserve all existing meets and joins within each factor. Concretely, one constructs the term algebra of lattice operations on generators from $L \sqcup M$, then quotients by the congruence encoding:
    \begin{itemize}
        \item For every finite subset $S \subseteq L$ with $\bigvee S$ existing in $L$: $\bigvee_{j} [s_j] = [\bigvee_L s_j]$; similarly for meets.
        \item The analogous relations for subsets of $M$.
    \end{itemize}
    Normal forms for elements of free lattices are governed by Whitman's condition: in a free lattice on generators $\{x_i\}$, one has $a \leq b$ if and only if for every meet-irreducible element $m$ with $b \leq m$, there is some generator or compound term below $a$ that also lies below $m$.

    \item [\textbf{(CLat coproducts)}] The coproduct in $\mathbf{CLat}$ of two complete lattices involves an ideal-completion procedure: take the free lattice coproduct and then apply the Dedekind-MacNeille completion to restore completeness. The resulting object is the smallest complete lattice receiving complete-lattice homomorphisms from both factors.
\end{enumerate}

For $\mathbf{\Sigma CLat}$, the coproduct of two $\sigma$-complete lattices $L$ and $M$ is obtained by taking the free lattice coproduct $L *_{\mathbf{Lat}} M$ and then applying a countable ideal completion that ensures all countable suprema and infima exist while preserving the universal property for $\sigma$-complete lattice homomorphisms.
\end{proposition}

    $$a \le b \implies a \cdot c \le b \cdot c \quad\text{and}\quad c \cdot a \le c \cdot b.$$

    We prove $a \le b \implies a \cdot c \le b \cdot c$; the other direction is symmetric.

    Since $a \le b$, we have $b - a \ge 0$ (by compatibility of the order with the additive group structure of the ordered ring). Then $b \cdot c = (a + (b - a)) \cdot c = a \cdot c + (b - a) \cdot c$. Since $b - a \ge 0$ and $c \ge 0$, by \Cref{definition:ordered_ring} the product $(b - a) \cdot c \ge 0$. Hence $a \cdot c + (b - a) \cdot c \ge a \cdot c$, i.e., $b \cdot c \ge a \cdot c$.

\begin{proposition} \label{proposition:nice_classes_of_spaces_that_are_CW_complexes}
The class of spaces admitting a CW complex structure (or the homotopy type of a CW complex) includes several fundamental families of topological spaces:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Manifolds:} Every compact smooth manifold is homeomorphic to a CW complex. This follows from Morse theory \TODO{define Morse function}, where a manifold is reconstructed by attaching cells at the critical points of a Morse function.
    \item \textbf{Simplicial Complexes:} Every simplicial complex is a CW complex, where the $n$-cells are the open $n$-simplices.
    \item \textbf{Algebraic Varieties:} All projective algebraic varieties over $\mathbb{C}$ admit CW structures.
    \item \textbf{Covering Spaces:} If $X$ is a CW complex, then any covering space $p: \tilde{X} \to X$ inherits a natural CW structure where the cells of $\tilde{X}$ are the components of the preimages of the cells of $X$.
    \item \textbf{Function Spaces:} While the space of continuous maps $\text{Map}(X, Y)$ is generally not a finite CW complex, it has the homotopy type of a CW complex if $X$ is a compact CW complex and $Y$ is a CW complex.
\end{itemize}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proposition} \label{proposition:limits_for_maps_from_a_topological_space_to_a_topological_group_respect_the_group_structure}

    Let $X$ be a \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:topological_space}{topological space} and let $(Y,\cdot)$ be a \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:topological_group}{topological group}. Let $S \subseteq X$ and let $p \in X$ be an \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:accumulation_point_of_a_subset_of_a_topological_space}{accumulation point} of $S$. Let $f,g : S \to Y$ be \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:function_of_sets}{functions}.

    If $L,M \in Y$ are \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:limit_of_a_function_between_topological_spaces_at_an_accumulation_point}{limits} $\lim_{x \to p} f(x)$ and $\lim_{x \to p} g(x)$ respectively, then $L \cdot M$ is a limit $\lim_{x \to p} (f(x) \cdot g(x)).$

\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
    This follows from \Cref{corollary:continuous_n_ary_operator_on_a_topological_space_commutes_with_limits}.

    Alternatively, let $m: Y \times Y \to Y$\CrefIfExists{definition:product_topology_on_a_cartesian_product_of_topological_spaces} be the group operation, which is a continuous map. Let $V$ be an open neighborhood of $L \cdot M$ in $Y$. Since $m$ is continuous, the preimage $m^{-1}(V)$ is an open neighborhood of $(L, M)$ in $Y \times Y$. By the definition of the product topology, there exist open neighborhoods $U_1$ and $U_2$ of $L$ and $M$ in $Y$, respectively, such that $U_1 \times U_2 \subseteq m^{-1}(V)$.

    Since $\lim_{x \to p, x \in S} f(x) = L$ and $\lim_{x \to p, x \in S} g(x) = M$, there exist neighborhoods $W_1$ and $W_2$ of $p$ in $X$ such that:
    \[ f(W_1 \cap S \setminus \{p\}) \subseteq U_1 \quad \text{and} \quad g(W_2 \cap S \setminus \{p\}) \subseteq U_2. \]

    Let $W = W_1 \cap W_2$. This is a neighborhood of $p$ in $X$. For any $x \in W \cap S \setminus \{p\}$, we have $f(x) \in U_1$ and $g(x) \in U_2$, so $(f(x), g(x)) \in U_1 \times U_2$. Therefore,
    \[ (f \cdot g)(x) = m(f(x), g(x)) \in V, \]
    so $(f \cdot g)(W \cap S \setminus \{p\}) \subseteq V$.
    Thus, $L \cdot M$ is a limit of $f \cdot g$ as $x \to p$ through $S$.
\end{proof}

The inclusion map $\iota : S \hookrightarrow X$ satisfies
    $$\lim_{x \to p, x \in S} x = p.$$

\begin{proof}
    The inclusion map $\iota : S \hookrightarrow X$ is continuous by \Cref{proposition:identity_function_between_topological_spaces_is_continuous} (the inclusion is the identity on $S$, which carries the \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:subspace_of_a_topological_space}{subspace topology}). Hence $\iota$ is continuous at every point of $S$. Since $p$ is an accumulation point of $S$, the definition of continuity at a point for functions between topological spaces implies that $\lim_{x \to p, x \in S} \iota(x) = \iota(p)$ \CrefIfExists{definition:limit_of_a_function_between_topological_spaces_at_an_accumulation_point}. Thus
    $$\lim_{x \to p, x \in S} x = p.$$
\end{proof}

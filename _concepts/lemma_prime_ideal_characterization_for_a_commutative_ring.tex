\begin{lemma} \label{lemma:prime_ideal_characterization_for_a_commutative_ring}
    Let $R$ be a commutative ring. An ideal $P \subseteq R$ is prime if and only if the following holds:
    $$\text{For all } a,b \in R, \text{ if } ab \in P \text{ then } a \in P \text{ or } b \in P.$$
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
    By \Cref{definition:prime_and_maximal_ideal_of_a_ring}, a proper ideal $P$ of the ring $R$ is prime if for all two-sided ideals $I,J$ of $R$, $IJ \subseteq P$ implies $I \subseteq P$ or $J \subseteq P$. Since $R$ is commutative, every ideal is two-sided.

    Suppose that $P$ is a prime ideal in the sense of \Cref{definition:prime_and_maximal_ideal_of_a_ring}. Let $a,b \in R$ with $ab \in P$. Let $(a)$ and $(b)$ denote the principal ideals generated by $a$ and $b$, respectively. The product ideal $(a)(b)$ is generated by products of elements from $(a)$ and $(b)$, which are of the form $(r_1 a)(r_2 b) = (r_1r_2)(ab)$ for $r_1,r_2 \in R$. Since $ab \in P$, every element of the form $(r_1r_2)(ab)$ is also in $R$. Thus, $(ab) \subseteq P$. Since $P$ is prime, $(a) \subseteq P$ or $(b) \subseteq P$. In the first case, $a \in (a) \subseteq P$, so $a \in P$. In the second case, $b \in (b) \subseteq P$, so $b \in P$. Hence, $ab \in P$ implies $a \in P$ or $b \in P$.

    Conversely, suppose that for all $a,b \in R$, if $ab \in P$ then $a \in P$ or $b \in P$. Let $I,J$ be ideals of $R$ with $IJ \subseteq P$. Suppose for contradiction that neither $I \subseteq P$ nor $J \subseteq P$. Then there exist $a \in I \setminus P$ and $b \in J \setminus P$. The product $ab$ lies in $IJ$, which is a subset of $P$, so $ab \in P$. By hypothesis, either $a \in P$ or $b \in P$, a contradiction. Hence, $I \subseteq P$ or $J \subseteq P$.

    Therefore, the two characterizations are equivalent for commutative rings.
\end{proof}

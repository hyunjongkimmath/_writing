\begin{lemma}[Multiplicativity of powers in a field] \label{lemma:multiplicativity_of_powers_in_a_field}

    Let $F$ be a \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:field}{field}, let $a \in F$, and let $m,n \in \mathbb{Z}$ such that both $a^m$ and $a^n$ are defined in the sense of \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:exponentiation_in_a_field}. Then
    $$\left(a^m\right)\cdot\left(a^n\right) = a^{m+n}.$$
    In particular, if either $a \neq 0_F$ or both $m,n \geq 0$, then the identity holds.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}

We handle several cases based on the signs of $m$ and $n$. If $a = 0_F$, then by convention in \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:exponentiation_in_a_field}, we have $0_F^k = 0_F$ for all $k > 0$ and $0_F^0 = 1_F$. The identity is easily verified in each of the finitely many cases (both nonnegative, one zero), so assume $a \neq 0_F$.

If both $m,n \geq 0$, this follows from the additivity of powers in a monoid (\CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{lemma:additivity_of_powers_in_a_monoid}), since every field is a monoid under multiplication.

For arbitrary integers, we use that for any nonzero element $a$ in a group (the multiplicative group $F^\times$), the identity $(a^m)(a^n) = a^{m+n}$ holds (\CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{lemma:additivity_of_powers_in_a_group}). Since $F^\times$ is a group, this covers all cases where $a \neq 0_F$.
\end{proof}

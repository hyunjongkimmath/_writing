\begin{proposition} \label{proposition:reduced_suspension_loop_space_adjucntion_for_pointed_topological_spaces}
    For all \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:pointed_topological_space}{pointed topological spaces} $X,Y$, there is an \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:adjoint_functors_between_categories_unit_counit_of_adjoint_functors}{adjunction} betewen the functors $\Sigma$ \CrefIfExists{definition:reduced_suspension_of_a_pointed_topological_space} and $\Omega$ \CrefIfExists{definition:based_loop_space_of_a_pointed_topological_space}. 
    More specifically, there are \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:homeomorphism_of_topological_spaces}{homeomorphisms}
    $$\operatorname{Map}_*(\Sigma X, Y) \cong \operatorname{Map}_*(X, \Omega Y)$$
    of pointed topological spaces
    \CrefIfExists{definition:compact_open_topology_on_the_set_of_continuous_maps_between_topological_spaces_and_function_space} 
    natural in $X$ and $Y$. 

    Applying $\pi_0$, we have bijections
    $$[\Sigma X, Y]_* \cong [X, \Omega Y]_*.$$
    \CrefIfExists{definition:homotopy_class_of_maps_of_topological_spaces_relative_to_a_subset} In fact, these homotopy class sets have group structures \CrefIfExists{definition:homotopy_groups_of_a_pointed_topological_space} and the bijection is actually a group isomorphism. 
\end{proposition}

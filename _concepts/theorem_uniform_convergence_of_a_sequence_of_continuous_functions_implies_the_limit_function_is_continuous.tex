\begin{theorem} \label{theorem:uniform_convergence_of_a_sequence_of_continuous_functions_implies_the_limit_function_is_continuous}
Let $S$ be a set and let $(X, d)$ be an \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:extended_metric_on_a_set}{extended metric space}.

Suppose $(f_n)_{n=1}^{\infty}$ is a sequence of functions $f_n : S \to X$ such that each $f_n$ is \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:continuous_map_of_topological_spaces}{continuous} at a point $x_0 \in S$ (with respect to the subspace topology on $S$ if $S$ is a metric space). If $f_n$ converges to $f$ \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:uniform_convergence_of_a_sequence_of_functions_from_a_set_to_an_extended_metric_space}{uniformly} on $S$, then the \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:convergence_and_limit_of_sequence_in_extended_metric_space}{limit function} $f : S \to X$ is \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:continuous_map_of_topological_spaces}{continuous} at $x_0$.
\end{theorem}

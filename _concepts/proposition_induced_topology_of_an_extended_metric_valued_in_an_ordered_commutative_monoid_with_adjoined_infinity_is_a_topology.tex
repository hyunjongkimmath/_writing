\begin{proposition} \label{proposition:induced_topology_of_an_extended_metric_valued_in_an_ordered_commutative_monoid_with_adjoined_infinity_is_a_topology}
    Let $T$ be an \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:ordered_commutative_monoid}{ordered commutative monoid}, let $T^\infty$ be the \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:ordered_monoid_with_adjoined_infinity}{ordered monoid with adjoined infinity} obtained from $T$, and let $(X,d)$ be an \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:extended_metric_on_a_set_valued_in_an_ordered_commutative_monoid_with_adjoined_infinity}{extended metric space valued in $T^\infty$}. The \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:topology_induced_by_an_extended_metric_valued_in_an_ordered_commutative_monoid_with_adjoined_infinity_and_open_ball_for_an_extended_metric}{topology induced by $d$ on $X$} is indeed a \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:topological_space}{topology} on $X$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
    We verify the three axioms of a topology\CrefIfExists{definition:topological_space}, using the open balls $B(x,\varepsilon)$.
    \begin{enumerate}
        \item The empty set $\emptyset$ belongs to $\mathcal{T}$ because the defining condition ``for every $x \in \emptyset$ there exists $\varepsilon \in T_{>0}$ such that $B(x,\varepsilon) \subseteq \emptyset$'' is vacuously true. The whole set $X$ belongs to $\mathcal{T}$ because for any $x \in X$, choosing any $\varepsilon \in T_{>0}$ yields $B(x,\varepsilon) \subseteq X$, since the open ball is by definition a subset of $X$.

        \item Let $\{U_i\}_{i \in I} \subseteq \mathcal{T}$ be an arbitrary collection and set $U = \bigcup_{i \in I} U_i$. If $x \in U$, there exists some $j \in I$ such that $x \in U_j$. Because $U_j \in \mathcal{T}$, there exists $\varepsilon \in T_{>0}$ such that $B(x,\varepsilon) \subseteq U_j$. Since $U_j \subseteq U$, we obtain $B(x,\varepsilon) \subseteq U$. Hence $U \in \mathcal{T}$.

        \item Let $U_1, U_2 \in \mathcal{T}$ and set $V = U_1 \cap U_2$. If $x \in V$, then $x$ belongs to both $U_1$ and $U_2$. Because each is open, there exist $\varepsilon_1, \varepsilon_2 \in T_{>0}$ such that $B(x,\varepsilon_1) \subseteq U_1$ and $B(x,\varepsilon_2) \subseteq U_2$. Choose $\varepsilon \in T_{>0}$ such that $\varepsilon \leq \varepsilon_1$ and $\varepsilon \leq \varepsilon_2$ (e.g., if the order is total, take $\varepsilon = \varepsilon_1$ when $\varepsilon_1 \leq \varepsilon_2$, otherwise $\varepsilon = \varepsilon_2$). For any $y \in X$ with $d(x,y) < \varepsilon$, we have $d(x,y) < \varepsilon_1$ and $d(x,y) < \varepsilon_2$; therefore $y \in B(x,\varepsilon_1) \subseteq U_1$ and $y \in B(x,\varepsilon_2) \subseteq U_2$, so $y \in V$. Thus $B(x,\varepsilon) \subseteq V$, which shows $V \in \mathcal{T}$.
    \end{enumerate}

\end{proof}

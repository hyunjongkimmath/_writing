Let $m$ be a positive integer. If the edges of the complete graph on $\mathbb{N}$ are colored with $m$ colors, then there exists an infinite subset $S \subseteq \mathbb{N}$ such that all edges between vertices in $S$ have the same color. That is, the induced subgraph on $S$ is a monochromatic clique of infinite size.

More generally, for any positive integers $m$ and $k$, if the set of all $k$-element subsets of $\mathbb{N}$ is colored with $m$ colors, then there exists an infinite subset $S \subseteq \mathbb{N}$ such that all $k$-element subsets of $S$ have the same color.

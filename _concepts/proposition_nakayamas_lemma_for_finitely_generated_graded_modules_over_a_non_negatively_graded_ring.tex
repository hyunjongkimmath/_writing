\begin{proposition}[Nakayama's lemma for graded modules]  \label{proposition:nakayamas_lemma_for_finitely_generated_graded_modules_over_a_non_negatively_graded_ring}

	Let $R = \bigoplus_{d \geq 0} R_d$ be a \hldef{non-negatively graded ring} with $R_0$ a field, and let $M = \bigoplus_{d \geq 0} M_d$ be a \hldef{finitely generated graded right $R$-module}, meaning $M$ is generated as an $R$-module by finitely many homogeneous elements. Let $R_{>0} = \bigoplus_{d > 0} R_d$.

	\begin{enumerate}
	    \item If $R_{>0}M = M$, then $M = 0$. Equivalently, if $M/R_{>0}M = 0$, then $M = 0$.

	    \item The quotient $M/R_{>0}M$ is a finite-dimensional right vector space over $R_0$. If $x_1 + R_{>0}M, \ldots, x_n + R_{>0}M \in M/R_{>0}M$ form a basis as an $R_0$-vector space, then homogeneous lifts $x_1, \ldots, x_n \in M$ generate $M$ as an $R$-module.

	    \item The minimum number of homogeneous generators of $M$ equals $\dim_{R_0}(M/R_{>0}M)$.
	\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

	\begin{enumerate}

Since each $r_i$ has positive degree, each term $y_i r_i$ has strictly greater degree than $\deg(y_i)$. But this contradicts the maximality of the degrees of the generators. Therefore, $M = 0$.

	    \item The quotient $M/R_{>0}M$ is annihilated by $R_{>0}$ because for any $\bar{x} \in M/R_{>0}M$ and any $r \in R_{>0}$, we have $\bar{x} \cdot r = 0$ since $x \cdot r \in R_{>0}M$. Hence $M/R_{>0}M$ is a right $R_0$-module, i.e., a right vector space over the field $R_0$. Since $M$ is finitely generated as an $R$-module, its image generates $M/R_{>0}M$ as an $R_0$-vector space, so it has finite dimension.

	    Now suppose that $\bar{x}_1, \ldots, \bar{x}_n$ form a basis of $M/R_{>0}M$ over $R_0$. Let $N$ be the submodule generated by homogeneous lifts $x_1, \ldots, x_n$. We have $N + R_{>0}M = M$, because every element of $M$ maps to an $R_0$-linear combination of $\bar{x}_i$'s modulo $R_{>0}M$. Apply part (1) to the quotient module $M/N$: we have $(M/N)/(R_{>0}(M/N)) = 0$, which implies $M/N = 0$. Therefore, $x_1, \ldots, x_n$ generate $M$.

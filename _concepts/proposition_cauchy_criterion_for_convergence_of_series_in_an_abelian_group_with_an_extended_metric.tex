\begin{proposition}[Cauchy criterion for convergence of series in an abelian group with an extended metric] \label{proposition:cauchy_criterion_for_convergence_of_series_in_an_abelian_group_with_an_extended_metric}
    Let $T$ be an \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:ordered_semiring}{ordered semiring}, let $T^\infty$ be the \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:ordered_semiring_with_adjoined_infinity}{ordered semiring with adjoined infinity} obtained from $T$, and let $A$ be an \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:abelian_group}{abelian group} equipped with an \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:extended_metric_on_a_set_valued_in_an_ordered_semiring_with_adjoined_infinity}{extended metric valued in} $T^\infty$, denoted by $d(\cdot, \cdot)$. Let $(a_n)_{n=1}^\infty$ be a sequence in $A$.

    The series $\sum_{n=1}^\infty a_n$ \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:infinite_series_and_convergence_in_a_topological_abelian_group}{converges} if and only if the sequence of partial sums $(\sum_{n=1}^N a_n)_{N=1}^\infty$ is a \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:cauchy_sequence_in_an_extended_metric_space}{Cauchy sequence} in $A$. Equivalently, the series converges if and only if for every $\varepsilon \in T_{>0}$ there exists $N \in \mathbb{N}$ such that for all $M \geq N$,
    $$d\left(0_A, \sum_{n=N+1}^M a_n\right) < \varepsilon.$$
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
    Let $S_N = \sum_{n=1}^N a_n$ denote the partial sums. By \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:infinite_series_and_convergence_in_a_topological_abelian_group}{definition}, the series $\sum_{n=1}^\infty a_n$ converges if and only if the sequence $(S_N)$ converges in $A$.

    (\Rightarrow) Suppose $(S_N)$ converges to some $S \in A$\CrefIfExists{definition:convergence_and_limit_of_sequence_in_extended_metric_space}. We show that $(S_N)$ is Cauchy. Let $\varepsilon \in T_{>0}$. By convergence, there exists $N_0 \in \mathbb{N}$ such that for all $n \geq N_0$, one has $d(S_n, S) < \varepsilon$. Now let $M \geq N \geq N_0$. By the \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:extended_metric_on_a_set_valued_in_an_ordered_semiring_with_adjoined_infinity}{triangle inequality},
    $$d(S_M, S_N) \leq d(S_M, S) + d(S, S_N) = d(S_M, S) + d(S_N, S),$$
    where the last equality uses \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:extended_metric_on_a_set_valued_in_an_ordered_semiring_with_adjoined_infinity}{symmetry}. Since $M, N \geq N_0$, both terms on the right are strictly less than $\varepsilon$.

    For the tail-sum characterization, note that for all $M \geq N \geq N_0$, we have $S_M - S_N = \sum_{n=N+1}^M a_n$. By the translation-invariance of the metric induced by the norm (see below),
    $$d\left(0_A, \sum_{n=N+1}^M a_n\right) = d(S_M - S_N, 0_A) = d(S_M, S_N) < \varepsilon.$$

    (\Leftarrow) Conversely, suppose that for every $\varepsilon \in T_{>0}$ there exists $N$ such that for all $M \geq N$, one has $d(0_A, \sum_{n=N+1}^M a_n) < \varepsilon$. We show $(S_N)$ is Cauchy. Let $\varepsilon \in T_{>0}$ and choose $N$ as above. For any $M \geq K \geq N$, we have $S_M - S_K = \sum_{n=K+1}^M a_n$. By translation-invariance,
    $$d(S_M, S_K) = d(0_A, S_M - S_K) = d\left(0_A, \sum_{n=K+1}^M a_n\right) < \varepsilon.$$
    For $K > M$, symmetry gives the same bound. Hence $(S_N)$ is Cauchy.

    \emph{Translation-invariance:} In the applications relevant to this proposition (e.g., when $A$ is a module over a commutative ring with an extended norm $\|\cdot\|$ and $d(x,y) = \|x-y\|$), translation-invariance holds because for all $x, y, z \in A$,
    $$d(x+z, y+z) = \|(x+z) - (y+z)\| = \|x - y\| = d(x,y).$$
    In particular, $d(S_M, S_N) = d(0_A, S_M - S_N)$ and $d(S_M - S_N, 0_A) = d(0_A, S_M - S_N)$ by symmetry.
\end{proof}

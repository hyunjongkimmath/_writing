    Let $n \in \mathbb{Z}_{>0}$ be a positive integer. Consider the function $f_n : \mathbb{C} \to \mathbb{C}$ defined by $f_n(s) = n^s$, where $n^s$ is given by the complex exponential and the \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:real_natural_logarithm_function_as_inverse_of_real_exponential_function}{natural logarithm of the positive integer $n$} as
    $$n^s = \exp(s \ln n).$$
    Then $f_n$ is \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:holomorphic_function_from_a_subset_of_Cn_to_Cm_and_derivative_of_a_holomorphic_function_at_a_point}{entire}, and its complex derivative satisfies
    $$\frac{d}{ds} n^s = n^s \ln n \quad \text{for all } s \in \mathbb{C}.$$
\end{proposition}

    Fix a positive integer $n \in \mathbb{Z}_{>0}$. By definition, $f_n(s) = n^s = \exp(s \ln n)$, where $\exp : \mathbb{C} \to \mathbb{C}$ is the complex exponential function and $\ln n$ is the real natural logarithm of the positive integer $n$.

    The map $g(s) = s \ln n$ is a linear map from $\mathbb{C}$ to $\mathbb{C}$, hence entire with derivative $g'(s) = \ln n$\CrefIfExists{proposition:complex_polynomials_are_entire_functions}. By the chain rule for complex derivatives (\CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{proposition:proposition_sum_rule_product_rule_quotient_rule_chain_rule_for_complex_derivatives_of_holomorphic_functions_on_open_subsets_of_the_complex_plane}), and using the fact that $\frac{d}{dz}\exp(z) = \exp(z)$ (\CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{proposition:complex_exponential_function_is_entire_and_its_derivative_is_itself}), we obtain
    $$\frac{d}{ds} n^s = \frac{d}{ds} \exp(s \ln n) = \exp(s \ln n) \cdot \ln n = n^s \ln n.$$

    Since both $\exp$ and the linear map $g$ are entire, their composition is entire. Hence $f_n$ is entire.
\end{proof}

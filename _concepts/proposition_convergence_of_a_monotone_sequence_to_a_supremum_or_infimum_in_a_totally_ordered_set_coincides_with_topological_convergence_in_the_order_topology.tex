\begin{proposition}[Topological convergence of a monotone sequence to a supremum or infimum in a totally ordered set coincides with order-theoretic convergence] \label{proposition:convergence_of_a_monotone_sequence_to_a_supremum_or_infimum_in_a_totally_ordered_set_coincides_with_topological_convergence_in_the_order_topology}
    \TODO{AI generated, verify}
    Let $(X, \leq)$ be a \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:total_order_linear_order_on_a_set}{totally ordered set} equipped with the \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:order_topology_on_a_partially_ordered_set}{order topology}, and let $(x_N)_{N=1}^\infty$ be a \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:monotone_sequence_of_elements_in_a_partially_ordered_set}{monotone sequence} in $X$.

    \begin{enumerate}
        \item \label{item:nondecreasing_topological_iff_supremum}If $(x_N)$ is \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:monotone_sequence_of_elements_in_a_partially_ordered_set}{nondecreasing}, then $(x_N)$ \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:convergence_and_limit_of_sequence_in_a_topological_space}{converges to} an element $x \in X$ in the order topology if and only if $x$ is a \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:supremum_infimum_of_a_subset_of_an_ordered_set}{supremum} of the set $\{x_N : N \geq 1\}$. In particular, if a supremum exists, it is the unique limit of $(x_N)$ in the order topology, and if no supremum exists in $X$, then $(x_N)$ does not converge.

        \item \label{item:nonincreasing_topological_iff_infimum}If $(x_N)$ is \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:monotone_sequence_of_elements_in_a_partially_ordered_set}{nonincreasing}, then $(x_N)$ \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:convergence_and_limit_of_sequence_in_a_topological_space}{converges to} an element $x \in X$ in the order topology if and only if $x$ is an \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:supremum_infimum_of_a_subset_of_an_ordered_set}{infimum} of the set $\{x_N : N \geq 1\}$. In particular, if an infimum exists, it is the unique limit of $(x_N)$ in the order topology, and if no infimum exists in $X$, then $(x_N)$ does not converge.
    \end{enumerate}

    Consequently, for a nondecreasing sequence that is \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:upper_and_lower_bounds_of_subsets_of_an_ordered_set}{bounded above}, topological convergence in the order topology is equivalent to the order-theoretic notion of \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:convergence_of_a_monotone_sequence_in_a_partially_ordered_set_with_adjoined_infinity}{convergence to the supremum}, and for a nonincreasing sequence that is \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:upper_and_lower_bounds_of_subsets_of_an_ordered_set}{bounded below}, topological convergence is equivalent to \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:convergence_of_a_monotone_sequence_in_a_partially_ordered_set_with_adjoined_infinity}{convergence to the infimum}.
    \TextIfExists{definition:convergence_of_a_monotone_sequence_in_a_partially_ordered_set_with_adjoined_infinity}{
        In other words, for monotone sequences in a totally ordered set with the order topology, the topological notion of \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:convergence_and_limit_of_sequence_in_a_topological_space}{convergence of a sequence in a topological space} and the order-theoretic notion of \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:convergence_of_a_monotone_sequence_in_a_partially_ordered_set_with_adjoined_infinity}{convergence of a monotone sequence to a supremum or infimum} coincide whenever the relevant supremum or infimum exists.
    }
    When $X$ is a totally ordered abelian group\CrefIfExists{definition:ordered_abelian_group}, this equivalence applies in particular to the \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:partial_sum_of_a_sequence_in_an_abelian_group}{partial sums} of a \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:infinite_series_and_convergence_of_infinite_series_in_a_topological_abelian_semigroup}{series} of non-negative elements, whose sequence of partial sums is nondecreasing.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}

    We prove item \eqref{item:nondecreasing_topological_iff_supremum}; item \eqref{item:nonincreasing_topological_iff_infimum} follows by the symmetric argument with lower rays in place of upper rays (equivalently, by applying the nondecreasing case to the sequence $(-x_N)$ when $X$ underlies an ordered abelian group).

    $(\Rightarrow)$ Suppose $(x_N)$ is nondecreasing and converges to $x \in X$ in the order topology. We first show $x$ is an \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:upper_and_lower_bounds_of_subsets_of_an_ordered_set}{upper bound} of $S := \{x_N : N \geq 1\}$. Suppose for contradiction that some $x_{N_0} > x$. By nondecreasingness, $x_N \geq x_{N_0} > x$ for all $N \geq N_0$. The lower ray $(-\infty, x_{N_0}) = \{t \in X : t < x_{N_0}\}$ is open in the order topology, contains $x$ (since $x < x_{N_0}$), and excludes every $x_N$ with $N \geq N_0$; this contradicts the convergence of $(x_N)$ to $x$. Hence $x_N \leq x$ for all $N$, so $x$ is an upper bound of $S$. To see that $x$ is the least upper bound, let $u \in X$ be any upper bound of $S$, so $x_N \leq u$ for all $N$. If $u < x$, then the upper ray $(u, \infty) = \{t \in X : t > u\}$ is an open neighborhood of $x$ (since $x > u$) containing no term $x_N$ (as $x_N \leq u$ for all $N$), again contradicting convergence to $x$. Thus $x \leq u$, and $x$ is a \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:supremum_infimum_of_a_subset_of_an_ordered_set}{supremum} of $S$.

    $(\Leftarrow)$ Suppose $x = \sup S$ exists in $X$, so $x_N \leq x$ for all $N$. Let $U$ be an \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:neighborhood_and_neighborhood_basis_of_a_point_in_a_topological_space}{open neighborhood} of $x$ in the order topology. Since $X$ is totally ordered, the order topology has a basis of open intervals and rays, so there exist $a, b \in X \cup \{-\infty, +\infty\}$ with $a < x < b$ such that $x \in (a, b) \subseteq U$. On the upper side, $x_N \leq x < b$ gives $x_N < b$ for all $N$. On the lower side, if $a \in X$ (i.e.\ $a > -\infty$), then $a < x = \sup S$ means $a$ is not an upper bound of $S$, so there exists $N_a$ with $x_{N_a} > a$; by nondecreasingness, $x_N \geq x_{N_a} > a$ for all $N \geq N_a$. (If $a = -\infty$, this condition is vacuous and we take $N_a = 1$.) Combining, $x_N \in (a, b) \subseteq U$ for all $N \geq N_a$, proving that $(x_N)$ converges to $x$.

    Uniqueness of the limit follows from the uniqueness of the supremum: any topological limit of a nondecreasing sequence must be the supremum by the forward direction, so it is unique.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Principle of mathematical induction] \label{proposition:mathematical_induction_on_natural_numbers}
Let $P(n)$ be a property depending on a natural number $n \in \mathbb{N}$. If the following two conditions hold:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item[\textbf{(Base case)}] $P(1)$ is true, and
    \item[\textbf{(Inductive step)}] For every $n \in \mathbb{N}$, $P(n) \implies P(n+1)$,
\end{enumerate}
then $P(n)$ holds for all $n \in \mathbb{N}$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
This is a direct reformulation of the \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:natural_numbers}{Principle of mathematical induction}, which is axiom 5 in the Peano axioms. Let $S = \{n \in \mathbb{N} : P(n) \text{ is true}\}$. The base case gives $1 \in S$, and the inductive step says that for every $n \in S$, we have $s(n) = n+1 \in S$. By axiom 5, $S = \mathbb{N}$, so $P(n)$ holds for all $n \in \mathbb{N}$.
\end{proof}

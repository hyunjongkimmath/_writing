\begin{lemma} \label{lemma:standard_order_on_natural_numbers_is_irreflexive}
    The \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:standard_order_on_natural_numbers}{standard order} $<$ on \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:natural_numbers}{$\bbN$} is \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:irreflexive_binary_relation_on_a_set}{irreflexive}, i.e. for all $n \in \bbN$, we have $n \not< n$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
    For $n \in \bbN$, let $P(n)$ be the statement $n \not< n$, i.e. that there does not exist any $d \in \bbN$ such that $n+d = n$. To show that $<$ is irreflexive is to show that $P(n)$ holds for every $n \in \bbN$. We prove this by induction on $n$. For $n = 1$ and for any $d \in \bbN$, note that $1+d = d+1 = s(d)$\CrefIfExists{proposition:natural_numbers_addition_associative_commutative}\CrefIfExists{proposition:natural_numbers_addition_existence}. By the \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:natural_numbers}{Peano axioms}, $1$ cannot be $s(d)$. Thus, $1$ cannot equal $1+d$ for any $d \in \bbN$. 

    Suppose that $k \in \bbN$ satisfies $P(k)$. We show that $P(s(k))$ holds, i.e. that there does not exist any $d \in \bbN$ such that $s(k)+d = s(k)$. Suppose for contradiction that there exists such a $d$. The left hand side equals $(k+1)+d$, which in turn equals $(k+d)+1 = s(k+d)$ as a consequence of the \CrefAndHyperref{proposition:natural_numbers_addition_associative_commutative}{associativity and the commutativity of addition} and by \Cref{proposition:natural_numbers_addition_existence}. Thus, $s(k+d) = s(k)$. The \CrefAndHyperrefIfExist{definition:injective_surjective_bijective_map_of_sets}{injectivity} of $s$ implies that $k+d = k$, which violates the inductive hypothesis. Hence, no such $d$ exists, so $P(s(k))$ holds. Therefore, $P(n)$ holds for all $n \in \bbN$ by induction as desired.
\end{proof}
